# Confessional self-analysis

## Definition

**Confessional self-analysis** combines radical disclosure with sustained interpretation.

It is not merely recounting what happened.

It asks what the disclosed material implies about:

- motive;
- history;
- internal conflict;
- inherited rules;
- self-deception;
- identity;
- fear;
- status;
- desire;
- causal structure.

~~~text
disclosure
    ↓
description
    ↓
interpretation
    ↓
recursive scrutiny
    ↓
procedural review
    ↓
revised self-model
~~~

## Historical provenance

The method has an explicit genealogical relationship to practices of interior examination in which motive, sincerity, pride, doubt, corruption, and self-deception become objects of scrutiny.

That genealogy does not make the modern method religious.

It identifies historical machinery that can be translated into secular self-analysis.

## Difference from confession

**Confession** makes material available.

**Confessional self-analysis** does analytical work on the material.

~~~text
confession = exposure
self-analysis = investigation
~~~

## Radical scope

The method is strongest when it permits examination of socially or personally undesirable material without making examinability equivalent to guilt.

That requires simultaneous:

- anti-immunity;
- anti-cruelty;
- procedural review;
- retained interpretive sovereignty.

## Relation to recursive introspection

The analyst must remain inside the analysis.

Confessional self-analysis therefore uses [Recursive introspection](./recursive-introspection.md) and [Procedural self-analysis](./procedural-self-analysis.md).

## Failure modes

- confession as performance;
- self-analysis as prosecution;
- endless recursion;
- theory forcing;
- external sovereignty capture;
- self-exemption by the analytical voice.

## Relations

It is enabled by **confession**, uses **recursive introspection** and **procedural self-analysis**, and operationalizes the larger **confessional epistemology**.
