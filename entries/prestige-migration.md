# Prestige migration

## Definition

**Prestige migration** is the relocation of status value when an old status-bearing good becomes broadly accessible. The good may remain pleasurable or useful while losing much of its capacity to distinguish. Prestige then attaches to a different bottleneck: restraint, authenticity, attention, difficulty, knowledge, time, body maintenance, bespoke experience, or another scarce capacity.

The compact originating definition was:

> The movement of status value away from a good once access to that good becomes widespread, toward a newly scarce trait or behavior.

## Problem pressure

Post-scarcity thought often assumes universal access dissolves hierarchy. The puzzle here is that status competition can survive abundance by changing its object. If luxury food becomes universal, status need not disappear; it can move to the body, the diet, the provenance of food, or the ability to refuse it.

## Core model / mechanism

```text
status-bearing good
      ↓ access expands
signaling power falls
      ↓
search for new scarce discriminator
      ↓
prestige attaches elsewhere
```

## Claims and implications

Prestige is treated as relational and mobile rather than permanently attached to a category of goods. The mechanism predicts second-order scarcity: once material access equalizes, scarce capacities and experiences become more important.

## Examples

From abundant calories to thinness/fitness; from cheap mass goods to handmade provenance; from unlimited digital content to focused attention; from easy travel to remote difficult journeys.

## Non-examples

A good becoming cheaper while retaining exactly the same prestige; simple fashion change with no relation to accessibility or differential scarcity; prestige loss caused only by moral stigma.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Not all prestige changes are caused by abundance. Moral change, technology, politics, generational style, and imitation can move status independently. The mechanism requires evidence that broad access weakened the old signal and redirected competition.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track access rates and status associations over time. Look for an inverse relationship between diffusion of an old good and rising prestige of a harder-to-copy substitute. Historical cases of democratized luxury are especially useful.

## Relations

**Pleasure saturation** can weaken the prestige value of access; **prestige migration** then helps generate a **friction premium**, **conspicuous abstention**, and the larger **post-scarcity prestige ecology**.
