# Submission to correction

## Definition

Willingness to let reality wound or overthrow one’s theory without retaliating against reality for doing so.

Submission to correction is episodic and enacted; epistemic submission is the broader standing principle that gives evidence veto power.

## Problem pressure

The thread treated knowingly continuing in error as worse than the humiliation of being wrong. It needed a behavioral form of epistemic submission at the moment of defeat.

The broader thread was trying to preserve exceptional intellectual capacity without either converting it into jurisdiction over others or demanding that the person erase the ego that carries the capacity.

## Core model

~~~text
theory encounters defeating evidence
        ↓
error accepted
        ↓
no retaliation against evidence / critic / reality
        ↓
revision
~~~

## Claims and implications

A proud ego can be ethically and epistemically acceptable if correction is allowed to land cleanly.

This entry belongs to the architecture of **psychic sovereignty, epistemic submission, and social non-domination**.

## Examples

### Clean example

After discovering a central empirical claim is false, a thinker retracts it without inventing a replacement story whose main purpose is preserving status.

### Borderline example

A case in which similar outward behavior exists but the underlying motive, exit option, or constitutional status differs.

## Non-examples

Saying “I was wrong” while retaining the same conclusion through a replacement rationalization.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Correction can itself be mistaken or abusive. The principle is submission to warranted correction, not indiscriminate deference to whoever claims authority.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track the concrete features named by the concept rather than inferring equivalence from appearances alone. For dependency claims, inspect credible exit and fallback options. For epistemic claims, inspect actual response to correction. For psychic-governance claims, distinguish internal desire from external imposition.
