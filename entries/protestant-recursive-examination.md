# Protestant recursive examination

## Definition

**Protestant recursive examination** names a historically specific form of self-scrutiny in which even apparently good evidence about the self can become subject to renewed examination.

~~~text
"I am sincere."
      ↓
"How do I know?"
      ↓
"Could confidence in my sincerity itself be pride?"
      ↓
"Is the judge of sincerity trustworthy?"
~~~

The recursive step is what matters.

## Problem pressure

Ordinary moral examination asks whether conduct or motive is good.

Recursive examination asks whether the **procedure that certified goodness** is itself corrupted.

This produces a structure recognizable in later secular introspection:

~~~text
claim
→ interpretation
→ interpreter
→ reliability of interpreter
~~~

## Protestant specificity

The term should not flatten Protestant traditions into one psychology.

It identifies a recurring analytical possibility within some Protestant cultures, especially where:

- sincerity matters;
- inward faith matters;
- self-deception is feared;
- assurance is difficult;
- moral corruption is assumed to reach the evaluator itself.

The historical distribution remains an empirical question.

## Relation to recursive introspection

This is a historical instance of [Recursive introspection](./recursive-introspection.md).

The general mechanism is not inherently Protestant.

## Relation to the Fallen investigator problem

If the examiner is themselves fallen, biased, proud, fearful, or self-deceived, then self-examination cannot simply trust its own instrument.

That generates the [Fallen investigator problem](./fallen-investigator-problem.md).

## Benefits and hazards

Potential benefits:

- anti-complacency;
- motive sensitivity;
- procedural reflexivity;
- suspicion of self-serving narratives.

Potential hazards:

- scrupulosity;
- infinite regress;
- internal inquisition;
- inability to accept evidence of sincerity;
- punitive self-relation.
