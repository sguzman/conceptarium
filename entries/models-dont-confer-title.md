# Models don’t confer title

## Definition

Understanding, explaining, representing, simulating, or theoretically subsuming another person gives no corresponding right to govern, redesign, command, or override them.

The principle separates epistemic possession from normative authority.

## Problem pressure

The thread needed a direct answer to a recurrent system-builder temptation: explanatory mastery can feel like ownership or jurisdiction even when no such title has been granted.

The broader thread was trying to preserve exceptional intellectual capacity without either converting it into jurisdiction over others or demanding that the person erase the ego that carries the capacity.

## Core model

~~~text
modeling / understanding / prediction
        ↓ epistemic success
        ✕
no title deed to governance
~~~

## Claims and implications

A more accurate model can improve advice or prediction while leaving the modeled person’s sovereignty fully intact.

This entry belongs to the larger architecture of **psychic sovereignty, epistemic submission, and social non-domination**.

## Examples

### Clean example

An AI system predicts a user’s preferences extremely well but gains no moral right to make binding choices on the user’s behalf.

### Borderline example

A case in which the same outward condition exists but exit capacity, motive, actual power, or scope of authority differs.

## Non-examples

A person explicitly delegates a bounded decision to an expert or agent under revocable terms.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Expertise can create responsibilities and justified domain-specific roles. The principle denies automatic title, not all delegated authority.

Do not treat Fallen/Village vocabulary as a biological caste or proof of moral superiority.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track the concrete variables relevant to the concept: actual exit options, retained capacities, domain-specific authority, response to correction, consent, contestability, and whether a representational or status advantage is converted into claims over others.
