# Misandry proper

## Definition

**Misandry proper** names anti-male animus itself: contempt, hostility, disgust, ridicule, demeaning stereotypes, or moralized revulsion toward men, masculinity, or male sexuality as such. It is an attitude or affective orientation, not merely a distributional pattern.

## Problem pressure

The term was explicitly required to stop anti-male hostility from being minimized into neutral institutional oversight. Structural inequality and contempt are different phenomena and need different evidence.

The immediate discussion concerned how status, demographic classification, institutional norms, and possible animus can affect the moral weight assigned to audience preferences.

## Core model

```text
negative affect / stereotype directed at men as men
        ↓
contempt / hostility / disgust / ridicule / animus
        ↓
misandry proper

may or may not produce structural consequences
```

## Claims and implications

Misandry can intensify structural bias when hostile attitudes shape norms or decisions, but the two are not logically identical. Conversely, identifying structural bias does not establish misandry without attitude evidence.

This is a conceptual entry, not an empirical verdict about any specific industry. Applications should separate observed outcomes, inferred incentives, and directly evidenced attitudes.

## Examples

### Clean example

Public or internal rhetoric treats male sexuality as inherently disgusting, pathetic, threatening, or contemptible in ways not tied to a specific harmful act.

### Borderline example

A public argument uses both commercial segmentation and moral language, making it unclear how much of the decision is explained by market strategy, institutional prestige, structural bias, or hostility.

### Cross-domain example

Comparable dynamics can occur in politics, education, advertising, entertainment, philanthropy, and institutional policy whenever group classification changes how claims are weighted.

## Non-examples

A policy that disadvantages men for bureaucratic or redistributive reasons without evidence of hostility is not by itself misandry proper.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Criticism of a specific man's conduct, masculinity norm, or sexual behavior is not automatically misandry. The target must be men, masculinity, or male sexuality as a generalized category.

Do not collapse unequal outcomes into animus automatically, and do not erase documented contempt by redescribing it as neutral allocation. Structural bias and hostile affect are separate variables.

## Operationalization / evidence

Distinguish policy or allocation patterns from rhetoric and attitude evidence. Structural claims require repeated asymmetry or institutional rules; animus claims require evidence of contempt, hostility, disgust, ridicule, stereotyping, or similar negative affect directed at the group as such.

## Relations

Misandry proper **distinguishes from** structural anti-male bias but can **intensify** it and can **support** punitive anti-fanservice when frustrating male desire becomes morally satisfying in itself.
