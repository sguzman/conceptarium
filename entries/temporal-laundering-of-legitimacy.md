# Temporal laundering of legitimacy

## Definition

**Temporal laundering of legitimacy** is the mechanism by which a warrant that was valid **then** is used to legitimate authority **now** without re-establishing that the relevant conditions still hold.

The term “laundering” refers to transformation of temporal provenance:

~~~text
past legitimacy
      ↓
institutional memory / prestige
      ↓
origin conditions fade
      ↓
authority remains
      ↓
current power appears self-justifying
~~~

The original warrant may have been completely genuine.

## Problem pressure

Infinite license is not only about scope. A bounded authority can become effectively infinite simply by losing its expiration condition.

The discussion therefore needed a term for the move from:

> “This institution earned trust under circumstance X.”

to:

> “This institution is trustworthy as a permanent fact.”

## Core model

~~~text
time t0:
warrant W justifies authority A

time passes
conditions change
performance may change

time t1:
A persists because W once existed
      ↓
temporal laundering
~~~

## Claims and implications

Institutional continuity is not inherently illegitimate. Long-lived authority can be rational because reauthorization is costly and competence persists.

The mechanism becomes problematic when **historical legitimacy substitutes for current warrant** and meaningful review disappears.

## Examples

### Clean example

An agency receives extraordinary authority during a crisis, performs well, and decades later retains unrelated emergency powers because its crisis-era legitimacy has become institutional prestige.

### Status example

A political class repeatedly invokes a foundational victory or reform success as evidence that its descendants or institutional successors deserve indefinite governing deference.

### Borderline example

A judge serves a long term after appointment. Longevity is not temporal laundering if the constitutional design explicitly makes the original appointment sufficient for the full term.

## Non-examples

Authority lasting a long time under an explicit original mandate is not automatically laundered.

Nor is honoring past achievement or retaining institutional memory.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The concept should not assume legitimacy must be re-earned continuously from zero. Many institutions require stable time horizons.

The key question is whether the **current authority is still justified by an explicit rule** or merely protected by the glow of an old warrant.

## Operationalization / evidence

Record:

- original warrant;
- original duration;
- changed conditions;
- current performance;
- review schedule;
- renewal process;
- whether failure can reduce power;
- how often historical accomplishments are invoked in current legitimacy claims.

## Relations

Temporal laundering of legitimacy stabilizes **Infinite license** across time and enables **Revocation asymmetry** by making old merit easier to count than new failure. It threatens **Contestability** when current authority cannot be meaningfully re-opened.

- What mechanisms preserve institutional continuity while preventing old prestige from becoming permanent title?
