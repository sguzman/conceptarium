# institutional mediation

## Definition

**Institutional mediation** occurs when access to a need, right, opportunity, permission, or protection is routed through formal organizations and procedures rather than handled mainly through direct personal relationships.

Examples include:

- benefits offices;
- banks;
- schools;
- insurers;
- courts;
- employers;
- platforms;
- credential systems;
- housing authorities.

## Problem pressure

Modern freedom often depends on institutions.

A person can escape a patron, landlord, family elder, or local gatekeeper because rights become portable and formally mediated.

But mediation introduces new interfaces and dependencies. The institution must recognize the person, classify the claim, and apply a procedure.

This makes institutional mediation the hinge between **disembedding**, **legibility**, and **managed dependency**.

## Core model

~~~text
need / right / opportunity
        ↓
institutional classification
        ↓
procedure / record / eligibility rule
        ↓
institutional mediation
        ↓
access, denial, appeal, obligation
~~~

## Claims and implications

Institutional mediation can make treatment more impersonal, portable, and contestable.

It can also make life dependent on opaque systems whose categories do not fit lived reality.

The quality of mediation therefore depends on legibility in both directions: institutions must see people, and people must be able to understand and contest institutional decisions.

## Examples

### Benefits

A person receives support through standardized eligibility rather than personal patronage.

### Credential

A university degree mediates access to a profession.

### Court

A customary dispute becomes a formal claim processed through legal procedure.

## Non-examples

Any interaction with an organization is not necessarily institutional mediation.

The concept concerns the organization functioning as a necessary or consequential intermediary between a person and a social good or right.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Mediation can multiply administrative burden.

It can also hide power by distributing decisions across forms, databases, rules, and contractors.

A formally neutral procedure may still be dominating if exit and appeal are weak.

## Operationalization / evidence

Measure:

- proportion of key needs routed through formal organizations;
- number of institutional gatekeepers;
- standardization of eligibility;
- portability across places;
- appeals and error correction;
- administrative burden;
- dependence on identifiers and records.

Institutional mediation should be analyzed together with institutional legibility.

## Relations

Institutional mediation is enabled by **Institutional legibility**, produces forms of **managed dependency**, is associated with **Social disembedding**, and enables **political domestication** when governance operates through administrative channels rather than overt coercion.
