# Imperium without jurisdiction

## Definition

Unlimited empire over representation, imagination, and theory without automatic authority over persons. One may intellectually annex the world without acquiring title to rule it.

Imperium names scope of representation; jurisdiction names legitimate authority. The point is to separate them.

## Problem pressure

The thread needed to distinguish conceptual totality from political title: a model can subsume an object intellectually without creating rights over the object’s human participants.

The broader thread was trying to preserve exceptional intellectual capacity without either converting it into jurisdiction over others or demanding that the person erase the ego that carries the capacity.

## Core model

~~~text
theoretical / imaginative imperium
      ↓ may be unlimited as representation
      ✕
no automatic jurisdiction over represented persons
~~~

## Claims and implications

System-building can remain expansive while anti-domination governs the translation from explanation into command.

This entry belongs to the larger architecture of **psychic sovereignty, epistemic submission, and social non-domination**.

## Examples

### Clean example

A theorist constructs a model that explains a village’s institutions but recognizes that explanatory success gives no right to redesign village life unilaterally.

### Borderline example

A case in which the same outward condition exists but exit capacity, motive, actual power, or scope of authority differs.

## Non-examples

Treating accurate prediction of another person as permission to manipulate or override them.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Some models are themselves interventions when used for surveillance, automated decisions, or resource allocation. At that point the activity has crossed beyond private representation.

Do not treat Fallen/Village vocabulary as a biological caste or proof of moral superiority.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track the concrete variables relevant to the concept: actual exit options, retained capacities, domain-specific authority, response to correction, consent, contestability, and whether a representational or status advantage is converted into claims over others.
