# absorption remainder

## Definition

**Absorption remainder** is the part of a valued object that remains experientially **unabsorbed** from the person’s point of view.

It can consist of:

- unseen story;
- unexplored space;
- unlearned mechanics;
- unresolved interpretation;
- unfinished mastery;
- uncollected material;
- untested implications.

The concept is phenomenological. The remainder is what the person still feels there is **more of the object to take in**.

## Problem pressure

The defining phrase behind Closure pressure was not “I have not earned the completion badge.” It was:

> **There is still more of the object to absorb.**

That shifted the theory away from completionism and toward an experiential/epistemic remainder.

The canonical Closure pressure entry already contained a section titled “The remainder”; this term makes that component independently referable.

## Core model

~~~text
whole valued object
      ↓ partial engagement
absorbed portion
      +
ABSORPTION REMAINDER
      ↓
represented incomplete whole
      ↓
open object / closure pressure
~~~

## Claims and implications

The absorption remainder need not correspond to objective percentage completion.

A person can reach credits but still feel a large remainder because the world or mechanics remain unintegrated. Conversely, someone can stop halfway and feel no remainder because they have extracted what they wanted.

This distinguishes **technical remainder** from **psychological remainder**.

## Examples

### Clean example

A player reaches the last act of a game and feels the unseen ending, remaining areas, and unresolved mechanics as a compact experiential remainder.

### Intellectual example

A book has been read but not understood; the unresolved argument remains an absorption remainder even though page completion is 100%.

### Borderline example

Optional collectibles remain after the main story, but the player does not care about them. They are technical remainder without meaningful absorption remainder.

## Non-examples

Content merely existing is not sufficient. It must remain part of what the person still experiences as worth absorbing.

Unknown content that the person has no interest in is not psychologically load-bearing.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The concept can blur with curiosity or incompleteness. Its distinctive feature is relation to a **valued whole already partly engaged**.

It should also not imply that all available content deserves consumption.

## Operationalization / evidence

Ask the person what exactly remains unfinished *for them*. Compare formal completion metrics with subjective remaining value.

A large mismatch between technical completion and felt remaining content is evidence that absorption remainder is psychologically distinct.

## Relations

Absorption remainder is part of an **Open object**, can intensify **Closure pressure**, and can be reduced or rendered irrelevant through **Deliberate closure** even without consuming every remaining element.
