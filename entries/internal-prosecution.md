# Internal prosecution

## Definition

**Internal prosecution** occurs when introspection is organized to prove guilt rather than discover what is true.

~~~text
internal event
    ↓
treated as incriminating
    ↓
ambiguity resolved against the accused part
    ↓
explanation becomes prosecution
~~~

## Investigation versus prosecution

Investigation asks:

> What happened, why, and what follows from the evidence?

Prosecution asks:

> How does this prove the part is guilty, illegitimate, or dangerous?

The same evidence can therefore enter very different procedural regimes.

## Typical signs

- presumption of guilt;
- selective admission of evidence;
- punitive interpretations of ambiguity;
- motive scrutiny used only against disfavored parts;
- no clear standard for closing the case;
- suffering treated as proof of seriousness.

## Relation to internal inquisition

When prosecution becomes generalized and permanent, it can produce [Internal Inquisition](./internal-inquisition.md).

## Relation to evidence versus punishment

[Evidence versus punishment](./evidence-versus-punishment.md) prevents the slide from “this motive is informative” to “this motive deserves punishment.”

## Relation to no torture authority

[No torture authority](./no-torture-authority.md) constrains the prosecutor even when the underlying concern is real.

## Non-examples

Internal prosecution is not:

- ordinary guilt;
- accountability;
- recognizing harmful motives;
- behavioral containment;
- reaching an unfavorable conclusion after fair review.
