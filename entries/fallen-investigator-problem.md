# Fallen investigator problem

## Definition

The **Fallen investigator problem** is the problem of an unreliable introspective instrument.

If the person doing the examining is themselves shaped by:

- pride;
- fear;
- desire;
- self-deception;
- motivated reasoning;
- moral corruption;
- limited knowledge;

then introspection cannot simply assume that the investigator occupies a clean external vantage point.

~~~text
self examines self
      ↓
examiner is part of the same system
      ↓
examiner may be biased or corrupted
      ↓
examiner must also be examined
~~~

## Why "Fallen"?

The term preserves its theological origin pressure.

In Protestant settings, human fallenness can make the soul's own judgment suspect.

The concept is nevertheless analytically portable.

Secular versions replace "fallen" with:

- biased;
- motivated;
- cognitively limited;
- socially conditioned;
- state-dependent.

## Relation to investigating the investigator

[Investigating the investigator](./investigating-the-investigator.md) is the general procedural response.

The historical problem helps explain why recursive scrutiny becomes necessary.

## Relation to procedural self-analysis

Once the investigator is non-sovereign, introspection needs procedure:

- evidentiary standards;
- symmetry;
- revision;
- adversarial hypotheses;
- anti-cruelty constraints.

That links the historical genealogy directly to [Procedural self-analysis](./procedural-self-analysis.md).

## Infinite-regress risk

The problem does not require endless skepticism.

The goal is not a perfectly pure observer.

It is a **more corrigible procedure**.

~~~text
no infallible investigator
        ≠
no possible knowledge
~~~
