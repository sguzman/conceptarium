# Provenance of belief

## Definition

**Provenance of belief** is the historical chain of custody of a proposition.

It asks:

- When did this belief appear?
- Which version of me formed it?
- What evidence was available?
- What alternatives were imaginable?
- What function did the belief serve?
- What changed afterward?

## Provenance versus legitimacy

A belief's origin informs review but does not determine its present legitimacy.

~~~text
where a belief came from
        ≠
whether it is true now
~~~

A childhood belief can remain correct.

A sophisticated adult belief can be false.

## Why provenance matters

Without provenance, a present proposition can appear timeless.

Reconstructing its history restores:

- context;
- uncertainty;
- original constraints;
- authoring conditions;
- reasons for persistence.

## Relation to epistemic chain of custody

The general [Epistemic chain of custody](./epistemic-chain-of-custody.md) tracks how information reaches a present claimant.

Provenance of belief applies that discipline inward across versions of the self.

## Relation to epistemic review

[Epistemic review](./epistemic-review.md) uses provenance as one source of evidence when deciding whether an old proposition still deserves authority.
