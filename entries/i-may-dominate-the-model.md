# I may dominate the model; the model may not dominate reality; neither may I dominate other persons merely because I can model them

## Definition

A companion sentence to Fallen constitutionalism: the thinker may exercise authorship over representations, representations remain subordinate to reality, and representational mastery never grants jurisdiction over other persons.

The repeated word dominate changes referent across the sentence: authorship of a representation is legitimate; domination of reality or persons is not.

## Problem pressure

The thread needed a single sentence that places authority correctly across three layers: author over model, reality over model, and persons over their own lives.

The phrase preserves the architecture in compressed form so that later theory does not dissolve the original insight into generic language.

## Core model

~~~text
SELF → may dominate MODEL
REALITY → may veto MODEL
MODEL ✕ → may not confer title over PERSON
~~~

## Claims and implications

The phrase sharply distinguishes constructive intellectual imperium from epistemic dogmatism and social jurisdiction.

## Examples

### Clean example

A system designer freely rewrites their simulation, accepts empirical failure when the simulation mispredicts, and treats understanding of users as no warrant to override user consent.

## Non-examples

Treating predictive accuracy as justification for paternalistic control.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Authorship over models is itself constrained when models contain unlawfully acquired data or directly affect people through automated systems.

## Operationalization / evidence

Treat the phrase as compression of the linked framework rather than independent empirical evidence. Applications should be unpacked into the underlying claims about psychic sovereignty, epistemic correction, jurisdiction, and anti-domination.
