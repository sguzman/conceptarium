# Interior surveillance

## Definition

**Interior surveillance** is persistent observation of one's own inner states.

Its objects can include:

- motives;
- desires;
- temptations;
- emotional shifts;
- fantasies;
- intentions;
- doubts;
- sincerity;
- self-deception.

The mechanism is:

~~~text
inner event
    ↓
noticed
    ↓
named
    ↓
evaluated
    ↓
remembered / recorded
    ↓
future inner events watched more closely
~~~

## Why surveillance?

The term emphasizes recurrence and monitoring.

A single act of introspection is not yet surveillance.

Surveillance emerges when a person develops an ongoing expectation that inner events should be noticed, classified, and evaluated.

## Productive and punitive forms

Interior surveillance can increase self-legibility.

It can also become punitive.

~~~text
observation
    ≠
prosecution
~~~

The existence of an inward monitoring apparatus does not justify [Cruelty in the tribunal](./cruelty-in-the-tribunal.md).

## Historical role

Religious examination, diaries, confession, spiritual autobiography, moral inventories, and later psychological practices can all provide machinery for sustained inward observation.

The claim is about **techniques**, not exclusive cultural ownership of inwardness.

## Relation to reflective administration

Monitoring produces material that can later be categorized, compared, adjudicated, and integrated through [Reflective administration](./reflective-administration.md).

## Failure modes

- compulsive self-monitoring;
- moral scrupulosity;
- interpretive overreach;
- treating every thought as evidence of character;
- collapsing observation into condemnation.

## Relations

Interiorization enables interior surveillance.

It can intensify Protestant recursive examination and contributes to making the self available to itself.
