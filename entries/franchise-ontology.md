# Franchise ontology

## Definition

**Franchise ontology** identifies the kind of continuity that is supposed to unify installments. A franchise might fundamentally be a continuing place, a shared world, a protagonist lineage, a mythology, an institutional setting, an anthology procedure, or an aesthetic/narrative method. Different ontologies license different forms of variation.

## Problem pressure

Many franchise disputes are actually disagreements about ontology. If one person thinks *Silent Hill* is fundamentally a particular supernatural place while another thinks it is an anthology method for psychologically symbolic horror, they will disagree about acceptable settings even before judging any individual game.

The immediate research pressure came from studying *Silent Hill*: if a rights-holder can move setting, ontology, narrative grammar, and aesthetic assumptions while retaining the same franchise label, what—other than authorization—makes the new work an instance of the same thing?

## Core model

```text
What is the franchise fundamentally continuous in?
        ↓
place / world / lineage / mythology / method / anthology / genre
        ↓
licensed variation
        ↓
identity conditions
```

## Claims and implications

Ontology makes hidden premises explicit. A move from place-based continuity to method-based continuity can radically expand what counts without any literal retcon. This can be legitimate evolution, but it should be described as a change in the kind of thing the franchise is.

The concept is analytic rather than automatically evaluative. A change can be artistically excellent while still changing the franchise category.

## Examples

### Clean example

A series initially treated as stories about one supernatural location later becomes understood as an anthology of psychologically symbolic horror stories that may occur elsewhere.

### Borderline example

A sequel changes major surface features but preserves enough of the older franchise grammar that reasonable observers disagree about whether the category itself has changed.

### Cross-domain example

The same structure can appear in long-running film, comic, toy, software, restaurant, or institutional brands when a durable name outlives the narrower properties it once denoted.

## Non-examples

A change in graphics engine or release platform is not ordinarily an ontological change.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Franchises can be ontologically hybrid. A theory should not force one essence where several layers genuinely coexist. The useful question is which layer bears the burden of continuity when other layers change.

Do not infer incompatibility merely from novelty. A strong identity can be elastic, and a historically unusual installment can still reveal a latent possibility rather than violate the franchise.

## Operationalization / evidence

Useful evidence includes comparison across installments, creator statements, audience categorization, recurring settings and structures, marketing language, changes in what features are treated as optional, and counterfactual tests such as: **if the franchise name were removed, what properties would still identify this work as belonging to the lineage?**

## Relations

Franchise ontology **constrains** identity conditions and **shapes** franchise personality. Ontological migration names a change in this underlying answer.

## Provenance

### First known appearance

2026-09-05, in the franchise-identity vocabulary developed from discussion of *Silent Hill*, franchise boundaries, and branding strength.

### Immediate context

The discussion distinguished the external fact of corporate ownership from the internal properties that make a work feel continuous with a creative lineage.

### Problem being solved

A vocabulary was needed for separating legal canon, artistic continuity, brand recognition, category boundaries, and quality. Ordinary judgments such as “good sequel,” “different direction,” or “still canon” compressed these into one question.

## Open questions

- Can franchise ontology be plural without becoming vacuous?
- Which historical installment should receive the greatest weight when reconstructing ontology?
- Can rights-holders deliberately redesign ontology, or does it emerge only through accumulated works?
