# The Great Subjectification

## Definition

**The Great Subjectification** is the name for a proposed intellectual transformation in which things that had been imagined as singular, absolute, or simply *there* become something like position-bearing constructions: **a geometry**, **a logic**, **a narration**, **a model**, **a claim to truth**.

The shift is not adequately described as “everything became subjective.” In fact, the striking feature is almost the opposite. Once several models or subject positions are possible, the intellectual burden becomes *harder*: one must explain how a claim was produced, what assumptions it depends on, how it can be translated into rival systems, what remains invariant across perspectives, and what procedure could justify preferring one account over another.

The compressed thesis is:

> **Subjectification relocates objectivity from the thing to the procedure.**

The object no longer guarantees its own singular description. Objectivity has to be reconstructed in the relations among models, methods, evidence, transformations, and checks.

## Dictionary projection

A proposed shift from singular absolutes to plural, position-bearing models and claims, accompanied by a new demand for procedural justification, translation, and comparison.

## Problem pressure

The concept emerged from a recurring puzzle: **why did the fragmentation of singular intellectual authority not simply produce chaos?**

The triggering examples were strikingly parallel.

Geometry had once been easy to imagine as *Geometry*: the one geometry of space. Later there could be **a geometry** among geometries.

Logic could become **a logic** among formal systems.

Narration could become **a narration from a position**, carrying a bounded information set, rather than an unexplained voice hovering above the fictional world.

Truth claims increasingly demanded a source, method, chain of evidence, derivation, experiment, witness, or model.

This initially looked like atomization: proper nouns losing their capital letters and being distributed among subjects. The disturbing question was why this did not lead to madness—why Europe’s multiplication of models could instead produce extraordinarily precise mathematics, sciences, historiography, criticism, and narrative technique.

The phrase *Great Subjectification* preserves that problem.

It names not merely pluralism, but the historical/intellectual drama in which plurality itself seems to generate pressure for stronger procedures of compatibility and validation.

## Core model

The working structure is:

```text
singular absolute
      ↓
de-absolutization
      ↓
plural local models / claims / positions
      ↓
compatibility problem
      ↓
explicit provenance, translation, invariants, proof, procedure
      ↓
reconstructed objectivity
```

A short form is:

> **Fragmentation followed by abstraction.**

Once there are multiple geometries, one can ask what “geometry” is at a higher level.

Once there are multiple logics, logic itself becomes an object of comparison.

Once there are multiple narrators or information positions, narratology can formalize viewpoint, access, reliability, and framing.

Plurality therefore can produce a **metalevel** rather than merely disintegration.

## Bring your own model

A closely associated phrase is:

> **Bring your own model.**

The phrase captures the permissive half of the Great Subjectification. Different agents may arrive carrying different representational systems.

But the right to bring a model immediately produces obligations:

- What does the model assume?
- How does it map onto observations?
- Can it translate into another model?
- What does it preserve?
- Where does it fail?
- What would falsify it?
- How did the information entering it reach us?

Plural modeling therefore creates demand for **epistemic chain of custody**, **epistemic debt**, formalization, and invariants.

## Diegetization as a local instance

The theory was partly recognized through narration.

An unexplained omniscient narrator resembles a transcendent authority: information is simply available because the narration says so.

Modern framing devices often force that knowledge to acquire a location:

- a witness;
- a diary;
- a recovered manuscript;
- a letter;
- a recorder;
- a historian;
- a memory;
- an archive;
- a narrator with bounded access.

This is **diegetization**: forcing what floats above the represented world to acquire an address within or attached to it.

Narration therefore becomes a compact laboratory for the Great Subjectification.

## Claims and implications

The entry currently contains several claims of different strengths.

### Definitional claim

The Great Subjectification names the pattern described above.

This is a vocabulary claim.

### Interpretive historical claim

A significant strand of modern intellectual development can be interpreted as movement from self-authorizing singularity toward plural models plus explicit procedures of reconciliation.

This is a broad historical thesis and requires serious historical work.

### Generative claim

Pluralization can create higher-order abstraction because multiple instances make their shared structure visible.

This is a stronger theoretical claim but can be studied locally in mathematics, logic, narration, historiography, and science.

### Psychological/civilizational extension

The original conversation connected this process to the “Fallen” individual: the person whose drive toward mastery, model-building, and intellectual sovereignty had been constrained by inherited village structures and then found enormous institutional room to operate.

That extension is part of the genealogy of the idea but should not be smuggled into the definition. It belongs to a larger and more speculative theory.

## Examples

### Geometry

The move from Geometry as a singular description of necessary space toward multiple geometries permits “geometry” itself to become a generalized formal object.

### Logic

Multiple formal logics turn logic from an invisible background rule into something that can itself be parameterized, compared, and studied.

### Narration

A narrator becomes a position with specified access rather than an unexplained omniscient channel.

### Historical knowledge

A claim about the past is evaluated through documents, witnesses, provenance, source criticism, and chains of transmission rather than merely inherited authority.

## Non-examples

The Great Subjectification is **not** simply:

- individualism;
- relativism;
- “everyone has their own truth”;
- skepticism;
- secularization;
- postmodernism;
- the existence of disagreement.

Those may overlap historically or rhetorically. The distinctive mechanism here is **pluralization followed by the construction of explicit procedures that make plural claims comparable**.

## Boundaries and failure modes

### Grand-history overreach

The concept is dangerously easy to turn into one master explanation of modernity. It should instead be tested domain by domain.

### False chronology

Different fields pluralized at different times and for different reasons. The name must not imply one synchronized event.

### Eurocentric compression

The original formulation emerged while thinking about European modernity. Similar intellectual processes elsewhere must not be ignored or automatically forced into the same genealogy.

### Confusing subjectification with subjectivism

The theory specifically predicts that plural subject positions can produce *more* procedural rigor. Treating it as a synonym for relativism destroys the interesting part.

## Operationalization / evidence

A serious research program would look for recurring transitions in individual fields:

1. a previously singular or self-authorizing representational order;
2. proliferation of explicit alternatives;
3. a compatibility or legitimacy crisis;
4. new metatheory, source criticism, formalization, invariants, proof rules, or validation procedures;
5. stabilization of objectivity at the procedural/metalevel.

Counterexamples would be equally valuable: cases where pluralization simply destroyed common standards, or where strong procedural objectivity emerged without prior pluralization.

## Relations

### Epistemic chain of custody

Plural claims require traceable histories of how information entered the model.

### Epistemic debt

Once assertion is no longer self-authorizing, claims incur justificatory debt.

### Diegetization

A narratological instance of moving knowledge from a transcendent position into a located chain of access.

### Fallen / Village

The original intellectual-history speculation connected subjectification to the release of system-building individuals from local inherited constraints. This remains a separate and much more conjectural relation.

## Open questions

- Does “subjectification” name the phenomenon well enough, or does it overemphasize human subjects relative to models and procedures?
- Which historical domains best support the proposed sequence?
- Are there premodern analogues that weaken any specifically modern chronology?
- Does pluralization actually cause metalevel abstraction, or are both effects of some third institutional change?
- How does print culture alter the epistemic chain of custody?
- What role do institutions play in enforcing procedural compatibility?
- Can the framework explain failures—domains where “bring your own model” never reconverges on shared procedure?
