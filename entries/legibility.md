# Legibility

## Definition

**Legibility** is the condition in which something becomes sufficiently structured or represented to be described, categorized, compared, recorded, queried, or reasoned about.

The general transformation is:

~~~text
opaque / diffuse / implicit
        ↓
distinctions + categories + representation
        ↓
nameable
        ↓
recordable
        ↓
comparable
        ↓
available to reasoning or administration
~~~

Legibility is not the same as truth.

A legible model can be wrong.

The concept concerns **availability to structured cognition and action**.

## Problem pressure

Several Conceptarium domains use the same deep operation:

- institutions make populations legible;
- databases make records queryable;
- introspection makes motives describable;
- ontologies make concepts addressable;
- narratives make causal sequences interpretable.

Without a general concept, each domain appears unrelated.

**Legibility** provides the cross-cutting abstraction while allowing each specialized form to retain its own risks.

## Legibility is relational

Something is legible **to an observer or system**.

A phenomenon can be:

- highly legible to a person but illegible to an institution;
- legible statistically but opaque phenomenologically;
- legible linguistically but poorly measured;
- legible to experts but not ordinary participants.

The question must therefore include:

> Legible to whom, through what representation, for what purpose?

## Legibility versus truth

A representation can become more legible while becoming less accurate.

For example:

~~~text
messy reality
      ↓ compressed into
clean categories
      ↓
high administrative legibility
      +
possible category loss
~~~

Thus legibility creates both capacity and danger.

## Legibility versus visibility

Visibility means something can be noticed.

Legibility means it can be **structured enough to enter comparative reasoning**.

~~~text
visible
   ≠
legible

legible
   ≠
correctly interpreted
~~~

A feeling can be obvious yet difficult to categorize.

A statistical category can be legible while obscuring the lived phenomenon.

## Psychological legibility

In the psychological branch, legibility occurs when inner experience becomes:

- nameable;
- differentiable;
- comparable across time;
- recordable;
- linked to motives or contexts;
- available to self-interpretation.

The next queued concept, **Legibility of the self**, specializes this general operation inward.

## Institutional legibility

[State legibility](./state-legibility.md) is a specialized case in which institutions translate social reality into categories, records, and metrics they can perceive and act upon.

The general concept is broader than states and broader than institutions.

## Legibility and recursive expansion

[Recursive expansion](./recursive-expansion.md) produces new legibility by turning previously implicit analytical layers into explicit objects.

The investigator, tribunal, and procedure become nameable rather than transparent background.

## Benefits

Legibility can enable:

- comparison;
- debugging;
- accountability;
- memory;
- prediction;
- coordination;
- revision;
- contestability;
- cumulative research.

## Risks

Legibility can also enable:

- overclassification;
- surveillance;
- false precision;
- administrative domination;
- self-monitoring;
- treating models as reality;
- erasure of what does not fit categories.

The concept is therefore instrumentally ambivalent.

## Examples

A vague recurring mood becomes distinguishable into fear, shame, anger, and fatigue.

A government converts informal land use into cadastral records.

A knowledgebase assigns stable IDs and typed relations to concepts.

A person records repeated self-judgments and notices a previously invisible pattern in tribunal procedure.

## Non-examples

Legibility is not:

- truth;
- simplicity;
- visibility alone;
- surveillance by definition;
- perfect prediction;
- complete knowledge.

## Boundaries and failure modes

### Map-territory collapse

The representation can be mistaken for the thing represented.

### Legibility imperialism

Systems may force reality into the categories easiest for them to process.

### False precision

Structured data can imply more certainty than the source supports.

### Total-legibility fantasy

Some phenomena remain irreducibly ambiguous, changing, or observer-dependent.

## Operationalization

A phenomenon becomes more legible when it becomes easier to:

- name consistently;
- distinguish from neighbors;
- record over time;
- compare across cases;
- query systematically;
- identify relations;
- revise using explicit evidence.

## Relations

**Recursive expansion** can produce legibility.

**State legibility** is a specialized institutional form.

**Psychological territory** becomes more usable as inner experience becomes legible.

The queued concept **Reflective administration** describes what consciousness can do once legibility supports organization and adjudication.
