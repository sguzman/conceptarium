# Ideology-to-geometry pipeline

## Definition

The **ideology-to-geometry pipeline** traces how abstract values can become visible physical form. Ideology does not directly move vertices; it affects status judgments, review criteria, portfolio rules, art direction, and approval decisions that eventually shape face, jaw, body, clothing, makeup, posture, lighting, and camera.

## Problem pressure

The term was needed to explain how broad cultural theory might matter to literal character design without relying on magical or conspiratorial causation.

The immediate problem was how abstract ideology, prestige, corporate incentives, and audience judgments can become concrete design choices without requiring a single centralized command.

## Core model

```text
abstract doctrine / norm
      ↓
prestige and moral coefficients
      ↓
institutional review / portfolio allocation
      ↓
art direction and approval
      ↓
face / body / clothing / posture / lighting / camera
```

## Claims and implications

The framework makes causal claims decomposable. Analysts can test each intermediate stage rather than inferring ideology directly from a jawline.

The entry is a mechanism for inquiry, not a presumption of motive. A specific application should identify which stages of the mechanism are evidenced and which remain hypothetical.

## Examples

### Clean example

A norm favoring broader beauty representation affects portfolio criteria, which affects concept approval, which changes the distribution of faces and bodies in the final cast.

### Borderline example

A design decision fits the predicted mechanism but could also be explained by story needs, technology, individual artist taste, market research, or production constraints.

### Cross-domain example

Comparable governance and allocation mechanisms can operate in advertising, fashion, film, publishing, social platforms, and institutional communication.

## Non-examples

A character artist independently choosing a strong jaw because it fits one character is not an ideology-to-geometry pipeline.

## Boundaries and failure modes

This framework is especially vulnerable to overreading. Similar morphology can emerge from scanning, casting, technology, individual taste, realism goals, or production constraints.

Avoid monocausal explanations. Corporate strategy, sincere moral beliefs, professional prestige, audience research, and personal aesthetics can align without being identical causes.

## Operationalization / evidence

Map the proposed causal chain stage by stage. Seek evidence for doctrine, status incentives, decision rules, allocation changes, and concrete output changes separately rather than inferring the whole pipeline from the final artifact.

## Relations

The pipeline **depends on** aesthetic allocation and **uses** moral coefficients and aesthetic priors as possible intermediate mechanisms.
