# Semantic drift

## Definition

**Semantic drift** is the broader process by which the meaning or extension of a label changes over time. In the franchise cluster, the object of drift is the franchise name: later works alter what audiences, creators, and institutions understand the label to include.

## Problem pressure

A stable trademark can conceal a changing category. The same word appears on every box while the set of properties associated with that word gradually shifts.

The immediate case was franchise evolution around *Silent Hill*, especially the possibility that successful, acclaimed departures may change the category more effectively than obvious failures.

## Core model

```text
label X at t1 → category boundaries A
successive admitted cases
        ↓
label X at t2 → category boundaries B
```

## Claims and implications

Semantic drift is neutral with respect to quality and legitimacy. It is the parent concept under which more specific mechanisms—quality-legitimized drift and ontological migration—can be analyzed.

This family deliberately separates two judgments: **is the work good?** and **what does admitting this work do to the meaning of the franchise?**

## Examples

### Clean example

Across decades, a franchise name moves from denoting stories centered on one place to denoting a broader class of thematically related horror stories.

### Borderline example

A well-received installment introduces several new assumptions but also preserves much of the older grammar; the key dispute is whether the change is ordinary evolution or category drift.

### Cross-domain example

The same logic applies to institutions, political labels, genres, product categories, and intellectual traditions whose names persist while membership criteria change.

## Non-examples

A one-off experimental spin-off that does not alter later usage or expectations does not by itself establish drift.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The term is broad and pre-existing. Conceptarium should not pretend to have coined it. Its value here lies in the specific operationalization around franchise category boundaries and its relation to dilution, ontology, and precedent.

“Dilution” here is semantic, not automatically qualitative. A broader category can contain better works than the narrower one it replaced.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track changes in category extension across time: which works are admitted, which traits cease to be necessary, what audiences expect from the label, what creators imitate, and whether formerly exceptional features become normal. Separate quality measures from identity measures.

## Relations

Semantic drift **generalizes** quality-legitimized semantic drift and ontological migration, and can **cause** semantic dilution and accumulated-precedent redefinition.

## Provenance

### First known appearance

The term *semantic drift* is pre-existing. On 2026-09-05 it was explicitly adopted into the local franchise-identity framework and given typed relations to dilution, precedent, and ontology.

### Immediate context

The discussion distinguished ordinary franchise failure from the more subtle case where good work makes a broader or altered franchise definition harder to resist.

### Problem being solved

“Decline,” “bad sequel,” and “selling out” were inadequate because they assume aesthetic failure. The needed vocabulary had to describe identity change that can occur through success.

### Conceptual ancestors

Semantic change, category broadening, brand dilution, canon formation, path dependence, prototype effects, and franchise evolution.

### Later refinements

None yet.

### External antecedents

Semantic drift is established terminology in linguistics and adjacent fields. The franchise application is a local specialization and should eventually be compared with work on category extension and brand meaning.
