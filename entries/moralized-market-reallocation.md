# Moralized market reallocation

## Definition

**Moralized market reallocation** is audience reallocation whose public or internal justification is framed through universal moral goods—representation, inclusion, healthier standards, authenticity—rather than primarily as a choice among competing constituencies.

## Problem pressure

The term was needed because a company can be pursuing market expansion and sincere moral goals at the same time, while rhetoric presents the outcome as though no constituency tradeoff exists.

The immediate problem was how to describe finite character slots, marketing attention, art budgets, and audience targeting without pretending that every form of “inclusion” is purely additive.

## Core model

```text
commercial / strategic reallocation
        +
moral justification
        ↓
market tradeoff represented as universal improvement
```

## Claims and implications

Moral framing can make the losses borne by an incumbent constituency less legible, especially when those losses already receive a negative moral coefficient.

These concepts describe allocation under scarcity. They do not assume that reallocations are illegitimate; the purpose is to make tradeoffs and constituency effects explicit.

## Examples

### Clean example

A publisher shifts character design away from a legacy male audience toward broader demographics and describes the change solely as healthier representation rather than also as audience strategy.

### Borderline example

A sequel expands its cast and changes design priorities simultaneously, so some groups gain new representation while others lose relative share without losing absolute quantity.

### Cross-domain example

The same logic can apply to casting, publishing lists, advertising inventories, programming schedules, product portfolios, and institutional attention.

## Non-examples

A company openly says it is targeting a new market and acknowledges that some existing consumers may dislike the change.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Moral language can be sincere; the concept does not imply hypocrisy. The key feature is that allocation and moral framing are merged in a way that obscures competing interests.

Do not treat all cultural attention as strictly zero-sum. Budgets, roster sizes, and markets can expand. The relevant question is which resources are actually scarce in the decision being analyzed.

## Operationalization / evidence

Identify the scarce resource, the before/after allocation, the constituencies being targeted, and whether change is additive, substitutive, or mixed. Where possible, separate absolute quantity from portfolio share.
