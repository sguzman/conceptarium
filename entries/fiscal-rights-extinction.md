# Fiscal rights extinction

## Definition

**Fiscal rights extinction** is the broader institutional failure mode in which lawful collection is treated as a boundary after which contributor-specific residual rights cease to exist or cease to matter.

It is broader than **Claim extinction**:

- claim extinction names the mechanism/event of individualized claim disappearance;
- fiscal rights extinction names the legal-political architecture in which provenance, attribution, allocation, audit, refusal, or standing are treated as absent after collection.

## Problem pressure

When the August 22 discussion was recovered, the striking question was: **what happened at the arrow between private property and public revenue?**

The source already had “claim extinction,” but recovery revealed a larger object: not just disappearance of one accounting claim, but collapse of a bundle of rights at collection.

## Core model

~~~text
lawful compulsory collection
        ↓
claim extinction
        ↓
contributor removed from fiscal ontology
        ↓
no provenance / attribution / allocation / standing
        ↓
public revenue treated as morally unencumbered
~~~

## Claims and implications

The term does not assert that contemporary states literally recognize zero taxpayer rights. It is an analytical model for the degree to which rights vanish at the collection boundary.

A system can exhibit partial rights extinction: aggregate transparency may survive while individualized standing or allocation disappears.

## Examples

### Clean example

A legal order precisely individuates tax liabilities, then treats collection as ending every contributor-specific claim and recognizes only collective legislative control over resulting revenue.

### Borderline example

A government preserves provenance and individualized receipts but denies all allocation and refusal rights. Some rights survive; others are extinguished.

## Non-examples

Taxation itself is not fiscal rights extinction. A government can tax compulsorily while preserving extensive residual fiscal rights. Redistribution also does not require erasing provenance.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The concept should not smuggle in the conclusion that every proposed residual right is morally required. Each candidate right needs separate justification.

It should also not erase actual legal taxpayer protections merely because they are weaker than the proposed framework.

## Operationalization / evidence

Build a rights matrix for a fiscal system: provenance, attribution, allocation, refusal, audit, standing, correction, and appeal. Record which survive collection, at what strength, and with what remedy.
