# Adversarial preference governance

## Definition

**Adversarial preference governance** begins when an institution stops treating a preference as one legitimate input among others and instead treats the preference itself as a target of intervention.

## Problem pressure

The term was needed for the stronger case beyond ordinary audience balancing: the institution may want consumers not merely to receive less of what they want, but eventually to want differently.

The immediate problem was how abstract ideology, prestige, corporate incentives, and audience judgments can become concrete design choices without requiring a single centralized command.

## Core model

```text
constituency preference
      ↓ judged socially undesirable / immature / harmful
not merely balanced against competitors
      ↓ targeted for restraint / correction / transformation
```

## Claims and implications

This is a sharper claim than paternalism. Paternalism can protect consumers while still respecting their preferences; adversarial governance treats some preferences as objects to be changed.

The entry is a mechanism for inquiry, not a presumption of motive. A specific application should identify which stages of the mechanism are evidenced and which remain hypothetical.

## Examples

### Clean example

A studio frames reducing a type of sexualized character design partly as a way to move the audience away from what it regards as unhealthy expectations.

### Borderline example

A design decision fits the predicted mechanism but could also be explained by story needs, technology, individual artist taste, market research, or production constraints.

### Cross-domain example

Comparable governance and allocation mechanisms can operate in advertising, fashion, film, publishing, social platforms, and institutional communication.

## Non-examples

A company declining a preference because another audience is more profitable is ordinary allocation, not adversarial governance.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The concept requires evidence of a preference-transforming aim. Frustration of a constituency is not sufficient by itself.

Avoid monocausal explanations. Corporate strategy, sincere moral beliefs, professional prestige, audience research, and personal aesthetics can align without being identical causes.

## Operationalization / evidence

Map the proposed causal chain stage by stage. Seek evidence for doctrine, status incentives, decision rules, allocation changes, and concrete output changes separately rather than inferring the whole pipeline from the final artifact.
