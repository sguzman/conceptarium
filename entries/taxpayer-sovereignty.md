# Taxpayer sovereignty

## Definition

In Conceptarium, **Taxpayer sovereignty** means that a contributor remains a meaningful fiscal subject after compulsory collection.

Its strongest local formulation gives a contributor bounded authority over the disposition of the contributor’s own tax stream while refusing to infer general political superiority from wealth.

~~~text
greater contribution-linked fiscal claim
        ≠
greater human worth
        ≠
greater general political franchise
~~~

## Problem pressure

The source asked why governments can individuate liabilities precisely while treating downstream contributor individuation as unreasonable. Existing tax-choice proposals seemed weaker because they usually begin from government ownership of the allocation field and grant taxpayers a small optional choice.

The local theory reverses the presumption: contributor authority should need to be extinguished explicitly rather than assumed absent.

## Core model

~~~text
citizen contributes
      ↓
residual fiscal claim survives
      ↓
bounded contributor authority
      ↓
provenance / allocation / refusal / enforcement
~~~

General political authority remains governed by separate constitutional rules.

## Claims and implications

Taxpayer sovereignty is not automatically plutocracy. A billionaire having more allocation authority over the billionaire’s own contribution does not imply authority over another person’s contribution or vote.

The concept still creates distributive risks. Those must be treated as design problems rather than hidden by collapsing fiscal and general political sovereignty.

## Examples

### Clean example

Every taxpayer directs a constitutionally defined discretionary share of their own tax contribution among eligible public purposes, with no ability to purchase another citizen’s vote.

### Borderline example

A tax form allows 1% of liability to be assigned to one of several funds. This is tax choice but only a weak form of taxpayer sovereignty.

## Non-examples

A wealthy donor purchasing influence over general legislation is not taxpayer sovereignty. Neither is a legislature claiming to represent taxpayers while contributors retain no individualized fiscal rights.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Contribution-weighted allocation can create unequal influence over public budgets. Self-dealing, strategic underfunding of unpopular but necessary goods, free-riding, and geographic inequality are major hazards.

## Operationalization / evidence

Specify the contributor-directable fraction, eligible destinations, conflict-of-interest rules, universal funding floors, anti-self-dealing constraints, and how collective obligations override refusal where necessary.

## Relations

Taxpayer sovereignty is supported by **Residual fiscal rights** and **Treasury fungibility / taxpayer sovereignty**, implements an anti-domination intuition, and opposes **Fiscal rights extinction**.

### External antecedents
