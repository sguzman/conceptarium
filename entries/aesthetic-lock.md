# aesthetic lock

## Definition

**Aesthetic lock** is a temporary narrowing of motivational access around a specific **formal or sensory aesthetic**.

The lock may concern:

- color palette;
- lighting;
- visual density;
- animation style;
- camera distance;
- sound design;
- musical texture;
- interface style;
- architectural form;
- material texture.

It differs from Tone lock by emphasizing **form and sensory organization** rather than primarily affective atmosphere.

## Problem pressure

The attention-cluster archaeology recovered “aesthetic lock” alongside tone lock, media appetite, and latent feature bundle.

The distinction is useful because a person can crave a style even when the emotional tone changes. Conversely, two works can evoke similar moods while differing enough aesthetically that only one feels admissible.

## Core model

~~~text
current sensory / formal appetite
        ↓
specific aesthetic feature bundle privileged
        ↓
matching works become low-friction
        ↓
nonmatching forms lose motivational access
~~~

## Claims and implications

Aesthetic lock can overlap with genre and tone but need not.

For example, an anime-styled visual appetite can span science fiction, fantasy, comedy, and drama; a low-poly PS1 aesthetic can span horror and non-horror.

The concept is temporary and state-dependent, not a permanent taste identity.

## Examples

### Clean example

A player temporarily wants only visually dense neon urban environments and bounces off mechanically similar games with bright pastoral art direction.

### Cross-medium example

A viewer moves between music videos, animation, and games because all use grainy analog textures and heavy contrast.

### Borderline example

A long-term preference for hand-drawn animation is stable taste unless it temporarily acts as a gate excluding otherwise desired media.

## Non-examples

Aesthetic appreciation by itself is not aesthetic lock.

Neither is a technical accessibility constraint such as motion sickness, where rejection may be physiological rather than appetite-based.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Aesthetic and tone locks can be hard to separate because formal choices often create mood.

Use Aesthetic lock when the strongest explanatory variable is **how the work looks, sounds, or formally behaves**; use Tone lock when emotional atmosphere is primary.

## Operationalization / evidence

Compare selections across works with similar content but different style, and across different content with similar style. A lock is supported when style predicts motivational access better than genre or subject matter.
