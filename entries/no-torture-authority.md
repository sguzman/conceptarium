# No torture authority

## Definition

**No torture authority** states that introspective jurisdiction does not create punitive sovereignty.

~~~text
right to investigate
        ≠
right to humiliate

right to judge
        ≠
right to brutalize

need for containment
        ≠
license for erasure
~~~

This is the anti-cruelty half of the confessional constitution.

## Why it is necessary

A doctrine of universal self-investigation is dangerous if it only says:

> “Everything may be interrogated.”

Without a complementary limit, the system can become an [Internal Inquisition](./internal-inquisition.md).

No torture authority places a hard boundary on method.

## What the rule forbids

It forbids punitive surplus such as:

- humiliation for its own sake;
- contempt as evidence;
- psychological degradation;
- treating suffering as proof of seriousness;
- denying a part any path to revision or rehabilitation;
- trying to annihilate an internal phenomenon merely because it is unwanted.

## What the rule does not forbid

It does not prohibit:

- harsh truths;
- moral judgment;
- behavioral boundaries;
- containment;
- rejecting false interpretations;
- refusing harmful action;
- acknowledging disturbing motives.

The constitutional demand is **truth without gratuitous brutality**.

## Relation to cruelty in the tribunal

[Cruelty in the tribunal](./cruelty-in-the-tribunal.md) names the failure mode.

No torture authority supplies the direct rule against it.

## Pair with universal jurisdiction

The full constitution is:

~~~text
EVERYTHING MAY BE EXAMINED
        +
NOTHING MAY BE TORTURED
~~~

Neither rule is sufficient alone.
