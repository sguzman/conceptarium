# Containment without cruelty

## Definition

**Containment without cruelty** allows strict behavioral or executive limits without treating the constrained part as contemptible.

~~~text
"you may not do this"
        ≠
"you should not exist"
~~~

The principle is especially important when an impulse is genuinely dangerous, coercive, self-destructive, or incompatible with external obligations.

## Firmness and humanity

Containment can be firm.

It may require:

- refusal;
- delay;
- environmental controls;
- loss of executive authority;
- strong behavioral boundaries;
- repeated monitoring.

What it does not require is humiliation.

## Relation to containment

The existing registry concept **Containment** names firm restriction without denial or humiliation.

This entry provides the explicit constitutional principle linking that operation to the anti-cruelty architecture.

## Relation to no torture authority

[No torture authority](./no-torture-authority.md) prohibits punitive sovereignty.

Containment without cruelty implements that rule in cases where some real limitation is nevertheless required.

## Relation to internal inquisition

The Internal Inquisition assumes severity proves seriousness.

Containment without cruelty rejects that premise.

Effective governance can be strict without becoming inquisitorial.

## Anti-collapse

- humane ≠ permissive;
- standing ≠ executive authority;
- recognition ≠ endorsement;
- containment ≠ punishment;
- boundaries ≠ contempt.
