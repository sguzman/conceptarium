# blocked mobility

## Definition

**Blocked mobility** occurs when an actor has a credible or socially expected route upward and that route becomes inaccessible.

The important structure is not simply “low status.” It is:

~~~text
plausible upward trajectory
        ↓
closure / abolition / exclusion
        ↓
trajectory becomes unavailable
        ↓
status discrepancy
~~~

The loss can concern office, profession, prestige, credential value, political incorporation, geographic mobility, or another consequential ladder.

## Problem pressure

The Fallen framework needed to distinguish stable hierarchy from destabilizing interruption.

A person who never expected entry may adapt to a bounded position. A person educated, credentialed, socialized, or recruited toward advancement can react very differently when the path is closed after expectations have formed.

This mechanism also makes historical cases testable: the theory should predict stronger disruption where credible ladders are visibly removed or narrowed.

## Core model

~~~text
expectation of mobility
        +
credible route
        ↓
institutional closure
        ↓
blocked mobility
        ↓
status discrepancy + status threat
        ↓
alternative ladder / exit / counter-elite / revolt
~~~

## Claims and implications

Blocked mobility is predicted to matter more than absolute deprivation for some ascent-oriented actors.

It should be especially destabilizing when many similarly situated aspirants experience the same blockage and can coordinate.

The mechanism does not determine ideology. Actors may demand opening, restoration, rival institutions, emigration, or withdrawal.

## Examples

### Examination abolition

A long-standing route from education into office disappears, leaving trained aspirants without the institutional future their preparation implied.

### Credential crowding

A profession continues producing qualified candidates while consequential positions contract.

### Political exclusion

A faction that previously expected rotation into office is permanently excluded from meaningful participation.

## Non-examples

Low income, low rank, or lack of ambition are not blocked mobility by themselves.

A path that was purely imaginary or never socially credible is better treated as frustrated aspiration than institutional blockage.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The concept can become unfalsifiable if any failure is redescribed as a blocked ladder.

Analysts should establish that a route was genuinely available, promised, customary, or strongly expected before identifying blockage.

## Operationalization / evidence

Look for:

- abolished examinations or offices;
- narrowing recruitment;
- stalled promotion cohorts;
- changing eligibility rules;
- credential inflation with stagnant high-status roles;
- autobiographical evidence of expected trajectories;
- changes in political behavior following closure.

The best designs compare similarly situated actors exposed to different degrees of closure.

## Relations

Blocked mobility causes **Status discrepancy**, intensifies **status threat**, can produce a **Rising Fallen** position, and is itself caused by **ladder closure** when institutions remove or restrict credible ascent routes.
