# Constructive puppet

## Definition

A **constructive puppet** is a character whose body deliberately reveals its construction from geometric components.

The body can look assembled from:

- volumes;
- sweeps;
- plates;
- tapered limbs;
- joints;
- layered clothing shells;
- simple face primitives.

The construction is not a temporary technical scaffold that must disappear.

It is part of the style.

## Problem pressure

The concept emerged after attempts to proceduralize characters exposed a recurring tension.

A seamless sculpt demands enormous local detail and many special cases.

A visibly constructed body makes the generative rules legible.

Instead of fighting the procedural origin, the style embraces it.

The question became:

> What if the character looks *better* because you can see the system that built it?

## Aesthetic value

The constructive puppet can produce:

- clarity of silhouette;
- deliberate segmentation;
- playful artificiality;
- strong procedural consistency;
- easy exaggeration;
- readable deformation.

It also matches the broader preference for **primitive geometry as language**, not merely as a low-detail approximation.

## Puppet does not mean toy

The term does not require a literal wooden or doll-like character.

“Puppet” marks segmented embodiment.

A character can be serious, grotesque, elegant, old, human, or alien while retaining visible construction.

## Relation to geometric caricature

Constructive puppet describes **assembly**.

Geometric caricature describes **recognizable exaggeration through obvious geometry**.

They often overlap.

A puppet can be geometrically restrained.

A caricature can be geometrically seamless.

## Failure modes

### Accidental cheapness

Visible primitives can look unfinished rather than intentional.

### Joint incoherence

Segments fail to form a plausible body.

### Generic assembly

Every character becomes the same mannequin with different dimensions.

### Hidden sculpt creep

The workflow gradually adds bespoke sculpting until the constructive grammar no longer matters.

## Operationalization

Ask:

> If the construction lines remain visible, does the character look more intentional or merely unfinished?

A successful constructive puppet makes the construction itself expressive.

## Provenance

The recovered lexicon preserved:

> “Aesthetic of visibly segmented, procedurally assembled character bodies made from geometric parts rather than pretending to be a seamless sculpt.”

## Open questions

- Which joints should remain visibly segmented?
- How much anatomical continuity is necessary for emotional readability?
- Can clothing participate in the same constructive grammar?
