# Coalitional readability

## Definition

**Coalitional readability** is the degree to which an artifact, aesthetic, person, brand, or cue can be quickly sorted into a perceived social or ideological coalition.

It is a property of the **relationship between an object and an interpretive environment**, not merely a property of the object itself.

~~~text
same cue
+ different cultural code
= different coalitional readability
~~~

A cue may be highly legible to one subculture and opaque to another.

## Problem pressure

Consumer semiotics describes how audiences infer larger political meaning from small signs.

That immediately raises a scalar question:

> Why are some artifacts almost instantly sortable while others remain ambiguous even after extensive discussion?

The answer cannot be only “because they contain signs.”

Signs differ in legibility.

Some cues have become so culturally stabilized that observers require almost no context before assigning coalition meaning. Others remain noisy, mixed, contested, ironic, or obscure.

**Coalitional readability** names this variable.

## Core model

~~~text
cue visibility
      +
shared interpretive code
      +
stability of cue-coalition association
      +
low ambiguity / low contradiction
      ↓
high coalitional readability
      ↓
fast allegiance inference
~~~

Readability can rise through marketing, controversy, repetition, factional adoption, media framing, or deliberate signaling.

## High and low readability

### High coalitional readability

Observers converge quickly on “who this is for” or “which side this belongs to.”

### Low coalitional readability

The artifact contains mixed signs, unfamiliar codes, contradictory cues, or insufficient context.

### Asymmetric readability

One coalition recognizes a sign immediately while another does not.

### Adversarial readability

Opponents become better at detecting a coalition's signs than ordinary members of the coalition itself.

## Examples

A political slogan with a stable contemporary affiliation has high readability.

A character design that has become a recurring culture-war token may become highly readable even if the design itself is ambiguous outside that discourse.

A niche aesthetic may be highly readable inside its subculture and nearly invisible politically to outsiders.

A work mixing cues associated with several factions may have deliberately low readability.

## Non-examples

Coalitional readability is not:

- ideological extremity;
- quality;
- explicitness alone;
- popularity;
- authorial intent;
- actual coalition membership.

An artifact can be highly readable and misclassified.

## Boundaries and failure modes

### Readability-truth collapse

High social consensus about classification does not prove the classification is accurate.

### Observer dependence

Readability must be specified relative to an audience.

### Historical drift

A cue's readability can change as coalitional codes change.

### Binary simplification

Some cultural environments contain multiple coalitions, subcoalitions, or cross-cutting identities.

## Operationalization / evidence

Possible measures include:

- inter-rater agreement on coalition classification;
- response time before classification;
- confidence ratings;
- amount of artifact exposure required before classification;
- stability across audiences;
- error rate against independent evidence of creator or audience alignment.

## Relations

Coalitional readability **enables allegiance inference** by making the relevant coalition easy to infer.

It can be **intensified by semiotic capture**, where repeated public framing stabilizes the artifact's factional meaning.

It belongs to **Consumer semiotics** as a scalar variable describing how easily the mechanism can operate.
