# Stale proposition

## Definition

A **stale proposition** is a belief whose authority survived longer than its evidence.

~~~text
belief once made sense
        ↓
context changes
        ↓
belief is not reopened
        ↓
old proposition continues governing
~~~

## Staleness is temporal, not moral

A stale proposition need not have been irrational when formed.

Examples:

- a danger estimate formed under dependency;
- a rule formed during childhood;
- a social expectation learned in one environment;
- a self-conclusion formed during crisis.

The problem is that the proposition remains active without renewed comparison to current conditions.

## Signs of staleness

- its evidence is rarely articulated;
- the original context is forgotten;
- counterexamples are explained away automatically;
- current capability is ignored;
- the proposition functions as law rather than hypothesis.

## Relation to epistemic review

[Epistemic review](./epistemic-review.md) reopens the proposition.

Review can vindicate it.

~~~text
review
    ≠
automatic repeal
~~~

## Relation to refactoring

If a proposition contains valid information but is overbroad, [Psychological refactoring](./psychological-refactoring.md) can preserve the signal while replacing the obsolete rule.
