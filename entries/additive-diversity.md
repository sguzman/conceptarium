# Additive diversity

## Definition

**Additive diversity** expands the represented set while preserving incumbent categories. It seeks more variation through growth rather than displacement.

## Problem pressure

The term was needed because “diversity” can conceal two very different portfolio operations: expanding the menu or replacing part of the menu.

The immediate problem was how to describe finite character slots, marketing attention, art budgets, and audience targeting without pretending that every form of “inclusion” is purely additive.

## Core model

```text
existing portfolio A B C
      + add D E F
      ↓
more diversity
without intentional reduction of A B C
```

## Claims and implications

Additive diversity minimizes direct constituency conflict when expansion is feasible, though attention and prominence can still become relatively diluted.

These concepts describe allocation under scarcity. They do not assume that reallocations are illegitimate; the purpose is to make tradeoffs and constituency effects explicit.

## Examples

### Clean example

A game expands its roster to include older, heavier, less conventionally attractive, and differently styled women while retaining roughly the same number of conventionally glamorous women.

### Borderline example

A sequel expands its cast and changes design priorities simultaneously, so some groups gain new representation while others lose relative share without losing absolute quantity.

### Cross-domain example

The same logic can apply to casting, publishing lists, advertising inventories, programming schedules, product portfolios, and institutional attention.

## Non-examples

Keeping roster size fixed and replacing two glamorous characters with two alternative beauty types is substitutive diversity.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Even additive expansion consumes budget and attention, so it is not literally costless. The distinction concerns intentional suppression of incumbent categories, not absence of any resource cost.

Do not treat all cultural attention as strictly zero-sum. Budgets, roster sizes, and markets can expand. The relevant question is which resources are actually scarce in the decision being analyzed.

## Operationalization / evidence

Identify the scarce resource, the before/after allocation, the constituencies being targeted, and whether change is additive, substitutive, or mixed. Where possible, separate absolute quantity from portfolio share.

## Relations

Additive diversity **contrasts with** substitutive diversity and can **relieve** erotic opportunity cost relative to replacement-based strategies.
