# Reopening the case

## Definition

**Reopening the case** means treating an old psychological verdict as revisable.

~~~text
old verdict
    ↓
new evidence / changed circumstances / new capacity
    ↓
case reopened
    ↓
evidence heard again
    ↓
verdict retained, narrowed, revised, or overturned
~~~

## Why judicial language helps

Old conclusions can behave like precedent.

They do not need to be erased simply because they came from the past.

But neither should age confer permanent immunity.

## Reopening triggers

A case may deserve reopening when:

- circumstances changed;
- new evidence appeared;
- the original self lacked relevant information;
- the rule causes persistent present harm;
- the rule contradicts other reviewed beliefs;
- the original danger no longer exists.

## Relation to epistemic review

[Epistemic review](./epistemic-review.md) is the actual review procedure.

Reopening the case supplies the constitutional permission to perform it.

## Anti-collapse

Reopening does not mean acquittal.

The old verdict may survive intact.

The important point is **contestability across time**.
