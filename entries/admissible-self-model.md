# Admissible self-model

## Definition

The **admissible self-model** is the range of psychological states and descriptions a person allows to count as genuinely belonging to themselves.

The operative question is not merely:

> “Did I experience this?”

It is:

> “Am I willing to recognize this experience as evidence about what I can be?”

This creates a legitimacy boundary around self-description.

~~~text
inner event
   ↓
noticed
   ↓
interpreted
   ↓
admissibility test
   ├── admitted → can update self-model
   └── rejected → treated as alien / impossible / invalid / exceptional
~~~

## Problem pressure

A person can consciously notice material while refusing to incorporate it.

Examples of the refusal structure include:

- “I felt that, but that is not who I am.”
- “I wanted that, but the desire cannot count.”
- “I acted that way, but it was not really me.”
- “People like me do not have that impulse.”
- “That state was produced by circumstances, so it has no legitimacy.”

Some of these judgments may be correct.

The concept exists because the judgment itself is analytically important.

## Visibility versus admissibility

The most important distinction is:

~~~text
PSYCHOLOGICAL VISIBILITY
"I can see the state."

        ≠

SELF-MODEL ADMISSIBILITY
"I permit the state to count as evidence about me."
~~~

A person may have high access but narrow admissibility.

Another may admit broad possibilities while remaining poor at observing their actual inner state.

## What shapes admissibility

The boundary can be shaped by:

- morality;
- religion;
- gender roles;
- family expectations;
- class identity;
- political identity;
- professional role;
- shame;
- fear of social consequences;
- prior self-narrative;
- desire for coherence.

The concept does not assume these influences are illegitimate.

A self-model requires selection.

The issue is **what is excluded and on what grounds**.

## Admissibility is not approval

To admit a state into the self-model is not to endorse it.

A person can say:

> “This cruel impulse is mine.”

while also saying:

> “I reject acting on it.”

The concept therefore protects a three-way distinction:

~~~text
recognition
    ≠
legitimacy as evidence of self
    ≠
normative permission to act
~~~

In the local theory, honest self-knowledge often requires the middle step without automatically granting the third.

## Opening and admissibility

A psychological opening can enlarge the admissible self-model.

A previously impossible category becomes:

1. conceivable;
2. inspectable;
3. survivable as a self-description.

That does not force permanent identity commitment.

It expands the **set of live hypotheses about the self**.

## Durable expansion

The strongest evidence of a changed admissible self-model is persistence.

After the state or event that produced the opening ends, the person can still say:

> “That possibility belongs in the space of things I might genuinely be capable of.”

That persistent change is captured by **durable expansion of the self-model**.

## Examples

A person who once treated anger as incompatible with being good becomes able to recognize anger as part of their ordinary psychology without endorsing aggression.

A previously unthinkable identity category becomes a legitimate hypothesis rather than being dismissed before examination.

A person admits that both tenderness and competitiveness can belong to them rather than forcing one side to be fake.

## Non-examples

An admissible self-model is not:

- a personality inventory;
- objective truth about identity;
- moral approval;
- a list of behaviors one intends to perform;
- complete self-knowledge;
- mere awareness.

## Boundaries and failure modes

### Infinite admissibility

Not every passing thought deserves equal weight in the self-model.

### Authenticity absolutism

Admitting an impulse does not prove it is deep, innate, or enduring.

### Provenance erasure

Where a state came from can matter even if provenance alone does not settle legitimacy.

### Coherence tyranny

Demanding one perfectly coherent self can narrow admissibility artificially.

### Boundarylessness

A broad self-model can become meaningless if every imaginable state counts equally.

## Operationalization

An admissibility boundary can be studied through:

- categories a person refuses to apply to themselves despite relevant evidence;
- different standards applied to the same state in self versus others;
- changes in what self-descriptions feel possible after opening;
- persistence of newly admitted possibilities over time;
- explicit reasons given for excluding a state from the self-model.

## Relations

**Psychological closure** narrows the admissible self-model.

**Opening** can change the boundary.

**Durable expansion of the self-model** is the persistent result when newly admitted possibilities remain live after the initiating state ends.
