# Domain leakage

## Definition

**Domain leakage** occurs when authority or credibility earned in one domain spreads into another domain without an adequate bridging argument or new authorization.

It can involve:

- expertise;
- prestige;
- institutional trust;
- moral authority;
- emergency power;
- celebrity;
- political mandate.

The concept is broader than credentials. A person can have no formal credential and still enjoy leaked authority because success in one field creates generalized deference.

## Problem pressure

Infinite license has to become unbounded along some axis. **Domain leakage** names the scope axis.

The problem is common because competence is often correlated across domains enough to make the inference psychologically attractive. A brilliant engineer may indeed reason well about policy; a successful organizer may have useful management skills.

But correlation does not itself create jurisdiction.

## Core model

~~~text
success / authority in domain X
        ↓
prestige or trust
        ↓
assumed competence in Y
        ↓
assumed title in Y
        ↓
domain leakage
~~~

## Claims and implications

Domain transfer is not always illegitimate. Expertise can be genuinely interdisciplinary, and skills can generalize.

The concept applies when the new claim lacks either:

- relevant evidence of competence;
- legitimate delegation;
- contestable authorization;
- a clear explanation of why the original warrant transfers.

## Examples

### Clean example

A celebrity athlete’s excellence is treated as sufficient reason to give them authority over unrelated medical or fiscal policy.

### Institutional example

An emergency health agency gradually acquires permanent powers over unrelated areas because trust earned during a crisis is treated as generalized administrative legitimacy.

### Borderline example

An economist comments on labor law. The fields overlap; deciding whether this is leakage requires examining the specific claim, not job title alone.

## Non-examples

Interdisciplinary expertise is not domain leakage. Neither is an elected official exercising broad authority through a mandate that is independent of their prior profession.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Domains are fuzzy. Overly rigid use would create artificial silos and prevent legitimate transfer of knowledge.

The concept should therefore track **the warrant for transfer**, not demand categorical isolation.

## Operationalization / evidence

For each authority claim, record:

- source domain;
- target domain;
- evidence of transferability;
- authorization;
- alternatives and contestability;
- whether the source prestige substitutes for target-domain argument.

## Relations

Domain leakage enables **Infinite license** and can be enabled by **Credential-to-jurisdiction conversion**. **Models don’t confer title** acts as a conceptual counterconstraint by separating understanding from authority.
