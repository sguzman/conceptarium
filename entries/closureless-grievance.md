# Closureless grievance

## Definition

A **Closureless grievance** is a grievance that lacks a recognized condition under which the special normative consequences of the grievance end.

The remembered injury may persist forever. Closure does not require forgetting, forgiveness, reconciliation, or denying the historical fact. The narrow question is whether the injury continues to generate **exceptional claims** without a terminal condition.

## Problem pressure

The unbounded-claims discussion needed to distinguish emotional memory from political entitlement.

A community can remember an atrocity permanently while still defining exactly what reparative obligations exist. Conversely, a grievance can be closureless even when the original harm was comparatively small if every attempted remedy becomes merely the precondition for another claim.

## Core model

~~~text
injury
  ↓
grievance
  ↓
special claim
  ↓
remedy attempted
  ↓
is there a discharge condition?
       ├─ yes → bounded grievance
       └─ no  → closureless grievance
                    ↓
              renewable claim source
~~~

## Claims and implications

Closureless grievance is neither a diagnosis of insincerity nor proof of revanchism. It is one structural ingredient that can enable unbounded revanchism.

Some grievances may reasonably remain open while harm persists, evidence is incomplete, or responsible institutions refuse compliance. The concept applies when **the theory itself lacks closure**, not merely when closure has not yet been achieved.

## Examples

### Clean example

Every previously named remedy is completed, but the grievance doctrine contains no state in which the opposing category loses its inherited special liability.

### Social example

A person says a past offense can never be repaired and will permanently justify unilateral restrictions on the offender, regardless of later conduct.

### Borderline example

A community refuses closure because an agreed restitution process is incomplete. The grievance is not closureless if completion would discharge the special claim.

## Non-examples

Permanent historical remembrance is not closureless grievance.

Neither is refusing forgiveness. A person may never forgive while still making no continuing claim over the offender.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Closure is not morally mandatory in every relationship. Some harms permanently alter trust or justify permanent separation.

The concept should therefore distinguish **continuing personal boundary** from **continuing jurisdiction over another person**.

## Operationalization / evidence

Ask the claimant or doctrine to specify:

- the desired remedy;
- who must perform it;
- what counts as completion;
- whether the exceptional claim changes after completion;
- whether new harms are treated separately.

If every imaginable satisfied state is rejected in advance, closurelessness is strong.

## Relations

Closureless grievance enables **Unbounded revanchism**, can be intensified by **Settlement refusal**, and is associated with **Moralized grievance** without being identical to it.
