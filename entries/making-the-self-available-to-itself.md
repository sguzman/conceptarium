# Making the self available to itself

## Definition

**Making the self available to itself** is the broad process through which previously obscure inner experience becomes more accessible to deliberate reflection.

~~~text
experience occurs
    ↓
attention + language + record + comparison
    ↓
experience becomes describable
    ↓
patterns become inspectable
    ↓
self becomes more available to itself
~~~

This is the historical complement to [Legibility of the self](./legibility-of-the-self.md).

## Availability is graded

Something can become available in stages:

~~~text
felt
≠ noticed
≠ named
≠ remembered
≠ compared
≠ interpreted
≠ integrated
~~~

Historical techniques can alter any of these transitions.

## Instrument classes

Technologies of self-availability can include:

- confession;
- diaries;
- letters;
- autobiography;
- essays;
- novels;
- moral inventories;
- conceptual vocabularies;
- psychological interviews;
- psychoanalysis;
- questionnaires;
- digital self-tracking.

The concept is deliberately medium-neutral.

## Availability is not truth

Making experience available does not guarantee correct interpretation.

~~~text
available
    ≠
accurately explained
~~~

A richer archive of the self can support both insight and elaborate error.

## Availability is not sovereignty

This framework remains constrained by [Self-opacity](./self-opacity.md).

More of the self can become available without consciousness ever acquiring complete causal mastery.

## Historical importance

A culture can increase self-availability by inventing practices that improve:

- resolution;
- recursion;
- storage;
- comparison;
- vocabulary;
- procedural repeatability.

That is the central positive thesis of the historical branch.
