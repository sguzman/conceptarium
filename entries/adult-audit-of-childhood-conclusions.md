# Adult audit of childhood conclusions

## Definition

An **adult audit of childhood conclusions** revisits beliefs authored under childhood constraints.

The adult may now possess:

- more information;
- more autonomy;
- more physical power;
- more social options;
- better vocabulary;
- greater ability to leave;
- different dependencies.

That changes the evidentiary environment.

## Childhood rationality

A childhood conclusion can be rational relative to childhood conditions.

For example:

~~~text
"I cannot leave"
may have been literally true

        ↓ years later

the rule persists as
"I can never leave"
~~~

The audit distinguishes historical rationality from present applicability.

## Procedure

1. Identify the active rule.
2. Estimate when it formed.
3. Reconstruct the child's available evidence.
4. Identify constraints that were real then.
5. Compare present capability and environment.
6. Preserve valid danger information.
7. Revise obsolete scope.

## Relation to psychological refactoring

The desired outcome is often not deletion.

A childhood rule may contain good protective information in overgeneralized form.

[Psychological refactoring](./psychological-refactoring.md) preserves the valid function while changing implementation.

## Boundaries

The framework does not assume childhood is the source of every present problem.

Nor does adulthood guarantee superior judgment in every domain.

It is a targeted audit where developmental asymmetry matters.
