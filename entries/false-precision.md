# false precision

## Definition

**False precision** is the expression of more exactness than the evidence warrants.

It can appear as:

- unjustified decimal probabilities;
- exact causal percentages;
- specific timelines inferred from weak evidence;
- precise mechanism stories where only correlation is known;
- narrow confidence ranges built from vague premises;
- exact narrative claims unsupported by the narrator’s information set.

False precision is not merely factual error. It is a mismatch between **displayed resolution** and **epistemic resolution**.

## Problem pressure

The canonical Epistemic maturation entry already warns about **confidence theater**: numeric probabilities invented without calibration.

AI systems are particularly capable of producing exact-looking detail because fluent completion rewards specificity.

The queue term was needed because a response can be directionally reasonable yet epistemically misleading through unsupported precision.

## Core model

~~~text
weak / coarse evidence
       ↓
model produces high-resolution output
       ↓
surface specificity exceeds support
       ↓
FALSE PRECISION
       ↓
uncertainty becomes harder to see
~~~

## Claims and implications

Precision and accuracy are separate.

A claim such as “there is roughly a substantial risk” can be epistemically better than “63.7%” when no calibration process supports the number.

Likewise, a causal explanation can be highly detailed yet less trustworthy than a coarse statement of uncertainty.

The failure is especially dangerous because users often treat numerical or mechanistic specificity as evidence of stronger grounding.

## Examples

### Numeric example

A model reports “there is a 72% chance the bug is caused by the driver” based only on a few symptoms and no calibrated diagnostic model.

### Historical example

A source gives an exact casualty number where records only support a broad range.

### Narrative example

A narrator reports exact mental states of unseen characters despite no modeled access route.

## Non-examples

Real precision supported by measurement, formal proof, or calibrated statistics is not false precision.

Rounding or simplification can also reduce precision without increasing truth.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The analyst must know what resolution the evidence supports. Precision that looks suspicious can still be legitimate if generated by a real measurement procedure.

The concept should not become hostility to numbers. Numbers are often precisely what make uncertainty auditable.

## Operationalization / evidence

For any precise claim, ask:

1. what measurement or inference procedure produced this resolution?
2. how calibrated is it?
3. how sensitive is it to assumptions?
4. would a coarser statement be more faithful?

Compare reported resolution against source resolution.
