# Corporate moral fog

## Definition

**Corporate moral fog** is explanatory opacity created when several motives are narrated through one benevolent moral vocabulary. Commercial expansion, employee values, reputation management, prestige incentives, and sincere social commitments become difficult to disentangle.

## Problem pressure

The term was needed because corporate messaging can make a contested allocation look like a unitary moral necessity, preventing analysis of who gains, who loses, and what strategic incentives also operate.

The immediate problem was how abstract ideology, prestige, corporate incentives, and audience judgments can become concrete design choices without requiring a single centralized command.

## Core model

```text
market strategy + reputation + ideology + prestige
        ↓ compressed into
representation / inclusion / authenticity / healthier standards
        ↓
causal plurality becomes hard to see
```

## Claims and implications

Fog does not imply insincerity. A moral rationale can be genuine while still obscuring the multiplicity of causes and tradeoffs behind a decision.

The entry is a mechanism for inquiry, not a presumption of motive. A specific application should identify which stages of the mechanism are evidenced and which remain hypothetical.

## Examples

### Clean example

A publisher describes redesigns entirely as an inclusion project even though internal incentives also include market expansion, prestige, recruitment, and risk management.

### Borderline example

A design decision fits the predicted mechanism but could also be explained by story needs, technology, individual artist taste, market research, or production constraints.

### Cross-domain example

Comparable governance and allocation mechanisms can operate in advertising, fashion, film, publishing, social platforms, and institutional communication.

## Non-examples

A company clearly distinguishes its moral commitments, market goals, and expected constituency tradeoffs.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The concept should not become a way to dismiss all corporate moral language as fake. Its target is causal compression and opacity.

Avoid monocausal explanations. Corporate strategy, sincere moral beliefs, professional prestige, audience research, and personal aesthetics can align without being identical causes.

## Operationalization / evidence

Map the proposed causal chain stage by stage. Seek evidence for doctrine, status incentives, decision rules, allocation changes, and concrete output changes separately rather than inferring the whole pipeline from the final artifact.
