# Incumbent-audience extraction

## Definition

**Incumbent-audience extraction** occurs when a firm treats existing fans' accumulated attachment as a revenue base that can be harvested while design priorities move elsewhere.

## Problem pressure

The phrase was needed for the case where an audience is not simply abandoned. Its loyalty remains commercially useful even as the product becomes less oriented toward satisfying it.

The immediate problem was how to describe finite character slots, marketing attention, art budgets, and audience targeting without pretending that every form of “inclusion” is purely additive.

## Core model

```text
legacy audience attachment
        ↓ expected purchase / attention persists
brand continues extracting value
        +
design reallocates toward new constituencies
```

## Claims and implications

Extraction is especially plausible in strong franchises because brand attachment can delay consumer exit and obscure the cost of reallocation.

These concepts describe allocation under scarcity. They do not assume that reallocations are illegitimate; the purpose is to make tradeoffs and constituency effects explicit.

## Examples

### Clean example

A long-running game series relies on established fans for launch sales while redesigning characters, themes, or marketing primarily to broaden demographic reach.

### Borderline example

A sequel expands its cast and changes design priorities simultaneously, so some groups gain new representation while others lose relative share without losing absolute quantity.

### Cross-domain example

The same logic can apply to casting, publishing lists, advertising inventories, programming schedules, product portfolios, and institutional attention.

## Non-examples

A company openly pivots to a new market and accepts losing the old audience without relying on its continued spending.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Continued purchases alone do not prove extraction; the term requires evidence or a strong model that incumbent loyalty is being relied upon while satisfaction is deprioritized.

Do not treat all cultural attention as strictly zero-sum. Budgets, roster sizes, and markets can expand. The relevant question is which resources are actually scarce in the decision being analyzed.

## Operationalization / evidence

Identify the scarce resource, the before/after allocation, the constituencies being targeted, and whether change is additive, substitutive, or mixed. Where possible, separate absolute quantity from portfolio share.
