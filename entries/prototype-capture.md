# Prototype capture

## Definition

**Prototype capture** occurs when one member of a franchise becomes disproportionately representative in cultural memory. Its traits are no longer treated merely as the traits of that installment; they become the easiest available answer to “what is this franchise like?” Later creators then reproduce those traits, converting an initially descriptive prototype effect into a causal force on the corpus.

## Problem pressure

The problem appeared when considering how *Silent Hill 2* came to dominate public memory of the series. If later games imitate its psychological symbolism and personal-trauma structure, its original atypicality can disappear because the series is gradually rebuilt around it.

This vocabulary emerged from trying to explain how *Silent Hill 2* can be both a great installment and a dangerous template for understanding what the franchise as a whole “really is.”

## Core model

```text
exceptionally salient installment
        ↓
audience prototype
        ↓
creator imitation / market expectation
        ↓
trait frequency rises in later entries
        ↓
prototype becomes increasingly representative
```

## Claims and implications

Prototype capture creates a feedback loop between memory and production. Cultural representation influences what gets made next; what gets made next then validates the representation. This can generate path dependence without any centralized decision to redefine the franchise.

The framework distinguishes historical influence from normative legitimacy: becoming the dominant prototype does not prove that the prototype was originally representative.

## Examples

### Clean example

One acclaimed sequel becomes so dominant that later entries repeatedly imitate its monster symbolism and individualized psychological structure, making those features seem like timeless franchise essentials.

### Borderline example

A famous installment introduces a feature that later becomes common, but earlier entries also contain weaker versions of it; whether this is capture or genuine clarification remains disputed.

### Cross-domain example

The same mechanism can occur in superhero series, film franchises, bands, product families, institutions, or intellectual traditions when one celebrated member becomes the template through which the whole category is remembered.

## Non-examples

An installment being the most popular entry is not enough if later works do not reorganize around its traits.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Capture is strongest when the prototype was initially unrepresentative. If the famous installment merely perfected traits already central across the corpus, “prototype crystallization” may be a better description than capture.

Do not treat popularity itself as distortion. A prototype may be famous because it genuinely crystallized the franchise. The analytical burden is to compare the prototype with the preexisting distribution of traits.

## Operationalization / evidence

Compare the prototype with installments before and after it. Track which traits become more frequent, which are copied in marketing and design, which are cited by creators, and whether descriptions of older entries shift after the prototype's success.

## Relations

Prototype capture **produces** prototype cues, can **cause** prototype inversion, **enable** retroactive franchise rewriting, and **intensify** semantic drift through repeated imitation.

## Open questions

- Do remakes amplify capture by giving the prototype repeated generational salience?
- Can multiple prototypes compete within one franchise?
