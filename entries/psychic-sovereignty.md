# Psychic sovereignty

## Definition

The individual’s exclusive jurisdiction over their interior imaginative life, including grandiosity, self-mythology, fantasy, ambition, theoretical conquest, and private ego-expression, insofar as these do not impose obligations on others.

Psychic sovereignty protects interiority, not every action motivated by interiority.

## Problem pressure

The thread rejected moral systems that demand internal domestication as the price of ethical social behavior. It needed a strong protected zone for fantasy, ambition, and ego.

The broader thread was trying to preserve exceptional intellectual capacity without either converting it into jurisdiction over others or demanding that the person erase the ego that carries the capacity.

## Core model

~~~text
interior imaginative life
      ↓ exclusive personal jurisdiction
fantasy / grandiosity / ambition / theory
      ↓
protected until external claims are imposed
~~~

## Claims and implications

A person can maintain extravagant private self-conceptions while accepting stringent external anti-domination rules.

This entry belongs to the larger architecture of **psychic sovereignty, epistemic submission, and social non-domination**.

## Examples

### Clean example

Someone privately imagines themselves as a conquering intellectual monarch while treating other people’s actual choices and projects as sovereign.

### Borderline example

A case in which similar surface behavior exists but the underlying status, optionality, or jurisdictional structure differs.

## Non-examples

Claiming psychic sovereignty as a defense for harassment, coercion, or manipulation once private states are externalized.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Mental life is socially influenced and can affect conduct. The principle is jurisdictional, not a claim that the psyche is causally isolated from society.

Do not treat Fallen/Village vocabulary as a biological caste or proof of moral superiority.

## Operationalization / evidence

Where empirical, examine actual options, observable status dynamics, reflexive behavior, institutional authority, and whether prestige or representation is converted into external constraint. Where normative, ask whether the rule preserves equal standing without falsifying real asymmetries.
