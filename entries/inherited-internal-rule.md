# Inherited internal rule

## Definition

An **inherited internal rule** is a rule the present self continues to execute even though it was authored under earlier conditions.

~~~text
earlier self encounters problem
        ↓
rule is formed
        ↓
rule protects / organizes behavior
        ↓
time passes
        ↓
present self inherits the rule
~~~

The inheritance is intra-personal rather than intergenerational.

## Why inheritance matters

The present self may experience the rule as:

- obvious;
- moral;
- necessary;
- identity-defining;
- simply "how I am."

But the rule may have been written by a much younger or differently situated self.

## Rule versus proposition

An internal rule governs action or interpretation.

A [Stale proposition](./stale-proposition.md) is the underlying belief that may no longer fit current evidence.

The two often travel together.

## Review

[Epistemic review](./epistemic-review.md) asks whether the inherited rule still deserves present authority.

The answer can be:

- retain;
- narrow;
- update;
- contextualize;
- retire;
- refactor.

## Anti-collapse

Inherited does not mean false.

Earlier selves can discover durable truths.

The issue is **unreviewed authority across changed conditions**.
