# subsistence-security priority

## Definition

**Subsistence-security priority** is the principle that maintaining a viable household and community floor takes precedence over status ascent or high-risk optimization.

The logic is asymmetric:

~~~text
gain above sufficiency
        versus
catastrophic loss below subsistence
~~~

When failure threatens land, food, shelter, household continuity, or local survival, the downside can dominate decision-making even when a riskier strategy has higher expected return.

## Problem pressure

The Village concept needed an explanation for behavior that outside modernizers may call conservative, inefficient, or irrational.

Near subsistence, failure is not merely a lower score. It can destroy the household.

This makes known land arrangements, reciprocal ties, diversified risk, customary guarantees, and local security rational even when they limit maximum upward return.

## Core model

~~~text
catastrophic downside risk
        ↓
security-weighted decision rule
        ↓
preference for reliable subsistence /
reciprocity / known institutions
        ↓
continuity before prestige
~~~

## Claims and implications

The principle does not claim Village actors are risk-averse in all domains.

They can undertake dangerous collective action when customary security itself is threatened.

Indeed, the model predicts a threshold effect: apparent patience can turn into ferocious resistance when the subsistence floor is violated.

## Examples

### Agriculture

A household prefers a lower-yield but reliable crop mix because crop failure would be catastrophic.

### Land politics

A community resists commercialization that raises average returns but threatens secure access for vulnerable households.

### Rebellion

A population tolerates ordinary hierarchy until taxation or enclosure makes basic reproduction impossible.

## Non-examples

Poverty is not itself subsistence-security priority.

A wealthy actor can prioritize security, and a poor actor can pursue high-risk ascent.

The concept concerns decision logic and social institutions, not income category.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Security can become a justification for coercive conservatism or blocked mobility.

The framework therefore does not treat the priority as universally optimal.

At some point excessive protection can prevent adaptation and undermine long-term continuity.

## Operationalization / evidence

Indicators include:

- explicit fear of catastrophic shortage;
- preference for secure access over higher expected return;
- resistance triggered by threats to land, food, water, household, or commons;
- mutual-aid institutions that pool downside risk;
- political demands framed around minimum guarantees rather than prestige.

Tests should distinguish true subsistence risk from generalized loss aversion.
