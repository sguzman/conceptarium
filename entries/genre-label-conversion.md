# Genre-label conversion

## Definition

**Genre-label conversion** occurs when a franchise name ceases to denote a comparatively narrow creative lineage and begins operating like a subgenre or premium genre descriptor. Membership becomes increasingly justified by broad affective or thematic resemblance rather than by continuity of world, ontology, narrative grammar, or other historically stronger conditions.

## Problem pressure

The possibility that *Silent Hill* could come to mean “prestige psychological horror” exposed a category transition. That phrase may describe many excellent works, but if it becomes sufficient for franchise membership, the franchise has migrated toward the surrounding genre.

The concept arose while separating the artistic identity of *Silent Hill* from the commercial and cultural strength of the *Silent Hill* name.

## Core model

```text
specific franchise territory
        ↓
identity conditions loosen
        ↓
broad genre traits become sufficient
        ↓
franchise label ≈ subgenre label
```

## Claims and implications

Genre-label conversion expands creative freedom while reducing franchise specificity. It can be deliberate, gradual, or retrospective. It often accompanies ontological migration from a place/world-centered franchise to an anthology or method-centered one.

Quality, popularity, and franchise fit are separate variables. None should be silently substituted for another.

## Examples

### Clean example

Stories need no longer share a particular supernatural place; being psychologically symbolic horror with a recognizable prestige treatment becomes enough to justify the franchise label.

### Borderline example

A new installment preserves several familiar cues and is marketed as a major franchise entry, but whether its deeper organizing logic belongs to the older lineage remains disputed.

### Cross-domain example

Comparable dynamics can occur whenever a powerful label becomes portable across products whose internal similarity is weaker than their shared branding.

## Non-examples

A franchise that explores several genres while retaining a highly specific world or protagonist lineage has diversified genre without converting into a genre label.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Genre boundaries are themselves fuzzy. The claim should therefore be supported by evidence that previously central franchise-specific conditions have become optional, not merely that the series has always participated in a genre.

The concept should not assume that only the earliest formulation is authentic. Franchises can legitimately broaden; the analytical task is to name what changed.

## Operationalization / evidence

Compare what the franchise label predicts at different historical moments. Track marketing descriptors, repeated motifs, setting and ontology, audience expectations, creator rationales, and whether a work stripped of names and icons would still be classified as belonging to the same creative lineage.

## Relations

Genre-label conversion **causes** semantic dilution and **threatens** franchise specificity. It is often **associated with** ontological migration.

## Open questions
