# Internal despotism

## Definition

The attempt by one moral faction of the self—often the ascetic or anti-ego faction—to permanently suppress or abolish other legitimate drives such as ambition, authorship, prestige hunger, or creative expansion.

The concept does not require treating every impulse as equally legitimate. It targets permanent unilateral abolition of otherwise legitimate drives.

## Problem pressure

The anti-domination argument was turned inward: if domination is objectionable between persons, the thread asked whether one moralized subsystem of the self can also become tyrannical toward the rest of the personality.

The broader thread was trying to preserve exceptional intellectual capacity without either converting it into jurisdiction over others or demanding that the person erase the ego that carries the capacity.

## Core model

~~~text
moral faction claims exclusive legitimacy
        ↓
suppresses ambition / authorship / desire / prestige
        ↓
internal despotism
~~~

## Claims and implications

A stable ethical architecture may need internal pluralism or negotiated governance rather than purification by one victorious faction.

This entry belongs to the larger architecture of **psychic sovereignty, epistemic submission, and social non-domination**.

## Examples

### Clean example

An ascetic self-conception treats every resurgence of ambition or creative pride as contamination that must be eradicated.

### Borderline example

A case in which the same outward condition exists but exit capacity, motive, actual power, or scope of authority differs.

## Non-examples

Choosing not to act on a destructive impulse after reflective judgment.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Psychic metaphors of factions and government are heuristic and should not be mistaken for literal independent agents.

Do not treat Fallen/Village vocabulary as a biological caste or proof of moral superiority.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track the concrete variables relevant to the concept: actual exit options, retained capacities, domain-specific authority, response to correction, consent, contestability, and whether a representational or status advantage is converted into claims over others.
