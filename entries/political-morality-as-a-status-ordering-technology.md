# political morality as a status-ordering technology

## Definition

**Political morality as a status-ordering technology** is the claim that moral systems can perform institutional work on status orders.

Moral language can:

- delegitimize inherited closure;
- universalize access;
- justify qualifications;
- legitimate hierarchy;
- recruit constituencies;
- define worthy and unworthy status;
- sanctify incumbency;
- attack incumbent privilege.

This is a theory of **one political function of morality**, not a theory that morality is nothing but status interest.

## Problem pressure

The Fallen/Village framework observed that Rising and Established Fallen actors can emphasize different moral problems.

Excluded actors often see the injustice of closure.

Established actors often see the coordination problem of standards, competence, and institutional continuity.

A crude theory would call both sides hypocrites.

The stronger formulation allows sincere morality and positional incentives to coexist.

## Core model

~~~text
status position
      ↓
different institutional problems become salient
      ↓
moral language selects / generalizes them
      ↓
status order attacked, opened, justified, or reproduced
~~~

## Claims and implications

The framework predicts systematic relationships between status transition and political moral emphasis.

It does not predict complete ideological reversal, nor does it deny independently generated reciprocal morality, religious conviction, or principled universalism.

The strongest form—“all morality is status strategy”—is explicitly rejected.

## Examples

### Exclusion

An outsider converts a particular blocked path into a universal doctrine of equal access.

### Incorporation

A former insurgent later emphasizes competence and standards once responsible for maintaining an institution.

### Village morality

A local community defends reciprocal obligations that conflict with both rising and established elite projects, showing morality is not reducible to elite status ordering.

## Non-examples

A moral principle with no meaningful relation to status arrangements is not evidence for the framework.

Nor does finding a status benefit prove that the moral belief is insincere.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The main failure mode is total cynicism.

If every moral claim is redescribed as status strategy after the fact, the theory becomes unfalsifiable and destroys exactly the moral distinctions it was meant to analyze.

The framework must therefore preserve cases where actors maintain costly commitments against positional interest.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track:

- actors' status positions over time;
- rhetoric about openness, equality, qualification, order, merit, and legitimacy;
- policy preferences before and after incorporation;
- costly consistency against status interest;
- moral claims generated by embedded communities independently of elite brokerage.

Within-actor longitudinal evidence is particularly valuable.
