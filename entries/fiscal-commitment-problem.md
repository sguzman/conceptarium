# Fiscal commitment problem

## Definition

A **Fiscal commitment problem** arises when contributors cannot trust that the conditions under which they accept a levy will remain binding over time.

The source pattern was:

~~~text
narrow-purpose tax
      ↓
tax becomes permanent
      ↓
revenue becomes general
      ↓
provenance disappears
      ↓
spending discretion expands
~~~

If concessions are difficult to reverse, taxpayers may treat even desirable new levies as potential permanent expansions of discretion.

## Problem pressure

The source asked why citizens become hostile to taxation even when they support the immediate public purpose.

The answer was dynamic: if every fiscal concession can later lose its original restrictions, present consent is discounted by expectations of future institutional drift.

## Core model

~~~text
government asks for bounded fiscal authority
        ↓
taxpayer anticipates future unbounding
        ↓
trust falls
        ↓
resistance rises
        ↓
collective financing becomes harder
~~~

## Claims and implications

This produces an empirical hypothesis: stronger non-extinguishment and provenance may make taxation more politically sustainable by reducing fears of irreversible discretion.

That claim requires testing; some opposition to taxation is independent of downstream rights.

## Examples

### Clean example

A temporary hospital levy is repeatedly renewed, then merged into unrestricted general revenue. Voters learn that future temporary earmarks are not credible.

### Borderline example

A levy changes purpose through an explicit referendum. The commitment changed, but not necessarily through unaccountable drift.

## Non-examples

Ordinary policy revision is not automatically a commitment problem. The concept concerns inability to make credible bounds on future authority.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The concept becomes unfalsifiable if every policy change is interpreted as betrayal. Mature use should distinguish sunset clauses, reauthorization, administrative repurposing, and emergencies.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track original purpose, duration, earmarks, amendments, general-fund migration, contributor rights, and whether changes required renewed authorization. Test whether stronger commitment devices correlate with willingness to approve levies.

## Relations
