# Bring your own model

> **Bring your own model.**

## Definition

An amber phrase for the permissive side of the Great Subjectification.

The phrase says that no single representational system automatically owns reality.

Different people, theories, geometries, logics, narrators, schemas, or simulations may bring different models.

But this permission is not relativistic surrender.

Once multiple models exist, new questions become unavoidable:

- Can they translate into one another?
- Do they make compatible predictions?
- What remains invariant?
- Which assumptions differ?
- What evidence entered each model?
- Which model is complete nonsense?
- Under what procedure can one outperform another?

Plurality creates **metatheory**.

## Problem pressure

The phrase appeared while the user was connecting subjectification in mathematics and narration.

If geometry becomes “a geometry,” and narration becomes “a narration from a position,” then representation begins to look like a world where everyone arrives carrying their own formal machinery.

The immediate concern was chaos.

The answer was that models become comparable precisely because they are explicit enough to inspect.

## The anti-relativist core

“Bring your own model” does not mean:

> Every model is equally good.

It means:

> Models no longer receive authority merely by pretending not to be models.

A good pluralist regime therefore needs:

- formalization;
- provenance;
- translation;
- invariants;
- empirical constraint;
- adversarial comparison;
- explicit failure conditions.

## Relations

### The Great Subjectification

BYOM is one of its clearest compressed formulations.

### Epistemic chain of custody

Plural models require knowledge inputs whose provenance can be traced.

### Adversarial verification

Rival models become useful constraints on one another when their evidence and incentives are sufficiently independent.
