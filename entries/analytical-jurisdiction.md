# Analytical jurisdiction

## Definition

**Analytical jurisdiction** specifies what may legitimately be examined.

The governing rule for inward inquiry is:

> **The self remains inside the jurisdiction of its own analytical apparatus.**

That includes:

- motives;
- identities;
- memories;
- desires;
- fears;
- interpretations;
- evaluators;
- tribunal rules;
- the investigator;
- the procedure itself.

## Jurisdiction is not suspicion

To be inside analytical jurisdiction means **may be questioned**, not **is presumed guilty**.

~~~text
jurisdiction
    ≠
presumption of corruption
    ≠
permission to punish
~~~

This distinction is constitutionally paired with [No torture authority](./no-torture-authority.md).

## Relation to existing anti-immunity canon

The exact phrase [No part of me gets to say: do not investigate me](./no-part-of-me-gets-to-say-do-not-investigate-me.md) expresses the inward constitutional rule.

Analytical jurisdiction is the more general abstraction.

It gives us vocabulary for asking:

- What is inside the domain of inquiry?
- What has been granted exemption?
- Who claims power to define the boundary?

## Self-exemption

A person can analyze entire external systems while silently excluding their own motives from the same methods.

That is [Self-exemption](./self-exemption.md).

Analytical jurisdiction prevents this by refusing a privileged exterior standpoint for the self.

## Subpoena power

Once a thing lies within jurisdiction, it can in principle be called forward as evidence.

That operational consequence is [Subpoena power of introspection](./subpoena-power-of-introspection.md).

## Boundaries

Analytical jurisdiction does not require:

- constant active examination;
- equal suspicion toward every claim;
- disclosure to outsiders;
- immediate resolution;
- infinite recursion.

It establishes **contestability**, not compulsory prosecution.
