# mood-congruent selection

## Definition

**Mood-congruent selection** is the mechanism by which current affect makes some media options more likely to be selected because their emotional or representational properties fit the present state or the desired direction of regulation.

Congruence can mean either:

- **matching** the current mood; or
- fitting the mood-management strategy the person currently wants.

The local concept is therefore broader than simple “sad people choose sad media.”

## Problem pressure

Genre lock’s provenance listed mood-congruent media selection as an external neighbor because mood was one plausible driver of temporary admissibility narrowing.

The queue preserved the phrase because the broader attention model needs a mechanism between current affect and the selected object.

## Core model

~~~text
current mood / desired mood regulation
        ↓
candidate media evaluated for affective fit
        ↓
motivational admissibility shifts
        ↓
selection probability changes
~~~

## Claims and implications

Mood congruence is one influence on media appetite, not a complete explanation.

People sometimes deliberately choose contrastive media to change mood, and curiosity or social obligation can override affective fit.

The concept should therefore remain a mechanism rather than a universal law.

## Examples

### Clean example

During a melancholy period, a player repeatedly selects brooding atmospheric games and finds bright comedic games motivationally flat.

### Regulation example

A stressed person selects calm, low-intensity media because it fits the desired affective transition even though it does not match the current stress state.

### Borderline example

Someone picks a horror movie because friends are watching it. Social coordination may dominate mood congruence.

## Non-examples

A stable genre preference is not necessarily mood-congruent selection.

A random choice that happens to match mood is also not evidence of the mechanism.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The phrase overlaps existing psychological terminology, so Conceptarium should avoid claiming novelty.

The local value is relational: it helps explain how mood can shape **Media appetite**, **Tone lock**, and **Motivational admissibility**.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track mood before selection, desired mood change, candidate properties, and actual choice. Repeated within-person covariance between mood and chosen tone supports the mechanism more strongly than one-off anecdotes.
