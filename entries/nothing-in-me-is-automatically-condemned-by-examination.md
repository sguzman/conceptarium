# Nothing in me is automatically condemned by examination.

## Definition

**Nothing in me is automatically condemned by examination** is the complementary constitutional axiom to universal inward scrutiny.

~~~text
may be examined
        ≠
is guilty
        ≠
is illegitimate
        ≠
should be erased
~~~

The rule protects the distinction between **evidence production** and **verdict**.

## Why it matters

Without this rule, universal introspection can become terrifying.

A person may avoid examining a desire, identity, memory, or motive because merely exposing it seems equivalent to prosecuting it.

The axiom makes inquiry safer without making outcomes predetermined.

## Not automatic exoneration

The rule does not say that every internal phenomenon is good.

After examination, a claim may still be:

- false;
- harmful;
- distorted;
- badly generalized;
- in need of containment;
- morally objectionable.

The point is that those conclusions must follow from evidence and judgment rather than from the fact of examinability itself.

## Constitutional pair

Together with [No part of me gets to say: do not investigate me](./no-part-of-me-gets-to-say-do-not-investigate-me.md):

~~~text
NO IMMUNITY
nothing is exempt from examination

NO AUTOMATIC CONDEMNATION
examination itself creates no guilt
~~~

[No torture authority](./no-torture-authority.md) supplies the procedural enforcement mechanism.

## Relation to the internal tribunal

The internal tribunal retains power to adjudicate.

But admission into the tribunal is not itself a guilty verdict.

This preserves standing for disfavored internal material long enough for evidence to be heard.
