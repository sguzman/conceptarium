# Self-abnegation

## Definition

Deliberate reduction of ego, prestige, self-assertion, or self-centrality. In this thread, Weil represents its extreme form and the user rejects it as a general solution to Fallen danger.

The concept is descriptive. It does not by itself say whether self-abnegation is admirable, pathological, temporary, spiritual, strategic, or coercive.

## Problem pressure

The conversation needed a neutral name for the strategy admired in Weil but rejected as a general constitutional solution for a Fallen personality.

The broader thread was trying to preserve exceptional intellectual capacity without either converting it into jurisdiction over others or demanding that the person erase the ego that carries the capacity.

## Core model

~~~text
ego / prestige / self-assertion / centrality
        ↓ deliberately reduced
        ↓
self-abnegation
~~~

## Claims and implications

Once separated from corrigibility, self-abnegation can be evaluated as one possible moral technology rather than treated as the necessary price of being correctable.

This entry belongs to the architecture of **psychic sovereignty, epistemic submission, and social non-domination**.

## Examples

### Clean example

A person deliberately suppresses claims to recognition and tries to make their own ego less central to action.

### Borderline example

A case in which similar outward behavior exists but the underlying motive, exit option, or constitutional status differs.

## Non-examples

Admitting a specific mistake while retaining ambition, pride, and authorship.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Practices labeled self-abnegating can differ radically in motive and degree. The term should not collapse humility, self-hatred, discipline, religious asceticism, and coerced submission.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track the concrete features named by the concept rather than inferring equivalence from appearances alone. For dependency claims, inspect credible exit and fallback options. For epistemic claims, inspect actual response to correction. For psychic-governance claims, distinguish internal desire from external imposition.
