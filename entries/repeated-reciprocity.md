# repeated reciprocity

## Definition

**Repeated reciprocity** is social coordination built on the expectation that people will continue interacting.

Because relations persist, present action affects:

- future help;
- reputation;
- trust;
- obligation;
- sanctions;
- access;
- standing.

The key contrast is with anonymous one-shot exchange.

~~~text
today's interaction
      + remembered past
      + expected future
      ↓
reciprocal discipline
~~~

## Problem pressure

Village social order cannot be reduced to affection or shared identity.

Much of its durability comes from repeated relationships that make cooperation and obligation self-reinforcing.

The concept explains how local systems can coordinate without every obligation being formalized through contract, price, or centralized enforcement.

## Core model

~~~text
stable network
      ↓
repeated interaction
      ↓
memory + reputation
      ↓
reciprocal expectation
      ↓
cooperation / sanction / mutual aid
      ↓
network persists
~~~

## Claims and implications

Repeated reciprocity can support trust and mutual aid at low administrative cost.

It can also support exclusion, vendetta, conformity, or retaliatory cycles.

The mechanism is therefore morally neutral; the Village framework values its continuity function while anti-domination constrains its abuses.

## Examples

### Mutual aid

Households exchange labor across seasons because each expects future need and remembers prior contribution.

### Local credit

A shop extends informal credit based on durable reputation rather than formal scoring.

### Political organization

Communities mobilize quickly because prior reciprocal networks already exist.

## Non-examples

A one-time charitable donation is not repeated reciprocity.

A fully anonymous market transaction may be cooperative but need not rely on relational memory.

Nor is repeated interaction sufficient if no reciprocal expectations exist.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The mechanism can be romanticized.

Repeated relationships can enforce oppressive norms and make exit expensive.

Large systems can sometimes outperform local reciprocity in fairness, anonymity, or scale.

The useful claim is functional: repeated relations are one powerful substrate for local social order.

## Operationalization / evidence

Indicators include:

- repeated exchange among the same actors;
- informal debt and obligation;
- reputation-based access;
- reciprocal labor;
- mutual aid;
- sanctions based on remembered conduct;
- expectations of future interaction.

Network persistence and multiplexity are important evidence.

## Relations

Repeated reciprocity supports **Embedded community**, **Customary-rights orientation**, and **Adaptive continuity**, characterizes **Village**, and can be threatened by **Social disembedding** when relationships are replaced by anonymous or portable exchange.
