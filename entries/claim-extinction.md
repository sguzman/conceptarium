# Claim extinction

## Definition

**Claim extinction** is the transition by which an institution treats a contributor’s individualized fiscal claim as no longer meaningful after the contribution enters public revenue.

~~~text
individual contribution
      ↓ collection / pooling
undifferentiated public revenue
      ↓
individual claim treated as gone
~~~

The mechanism may occur legally, administratively, or simply in the ontology of the accounting system.

## Problem pressure

The source noticed that “general fund” and “claim extinction” are not logically identical. A treasury can pool funds for liquidity while preserving a shadow ledger underneath.

The term was needed to isolate the extra step by which contributor identity or claim is actually erased.

## Core model

Claim extinction can be decomposed into:

1. contribution is individuated at assessment and collection;
2. revenue is pooled;
3. contributor identity is removed from the downstream data model;
4. expenditures are represented only as state-to-program flows;
5. contributor-specific provenance or enforcement claims become difficult or impossible to express.

The crucial step is #3, not pooling itself.

## Claims and implications

The concept is descriptive and can be used without assuming claim extinction is always illegitimate.

A legal system may intentionally extinguish some claims for administrative simplicity, equal citizenship, legislative budgeting, or redistribution. The theoretical demand is that those reasons be stated explicitly rather than smuggled in through “money is fungible.”

## Examples

### Clean example

A tax system tracks each taxpayer exactly until payment settles, then permanently discards the contributor relation and recognizes no later attribution or standing.

### Borderline example

A system retains internal transaction provenance for audit but gives contributors no rights. Technical claim persistence exists; normative persistence does not.

### Cross-domain example

A platform pools customer balances and then treats users as having no individualized ledger claim.

## Non-examples

Pooling tax receipts is not itself claim extinction. Neither is redistribution. A contributor’s claim can remain traceable even when the final recipient is someone else.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The word “claim” must specify what survives. A taxpayer can have a provenance claim without retaining ownership, and an audit right without a spending veto.

Using claim extinction loosely for any disliked expenditure would destroy the mechanism’s precision.

## Operationalization / evidence

Inspect the fiscal data model and law at collection. Does a stable relation between contributor and contribution survive? Can it be propagated? Are contributor-specific causes of action recognized? What event terminates the relation?

## Relations

Claim extinction produces **Fiscal rights extinction**, threatens **Taxpayer sovereignty**, can cause a **Fiscal commitment problem**, and contrasts directly with **Physical pooling / legal individuation**.
