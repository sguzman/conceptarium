# Dominance discount

## Definition

A **dominance discount** is a weighting mechanism in which harms, losses, preferences, or claims associated with a supposedly dominant group are treated as cheaper because the group is assumed to possess compensating social power or privilege.

## Problem pressure

The term was needed to describe a specific inference: if “men” are classified as dominant in the abstract, then losses imposed on ordinary male consumers may be regarded as less morally costly even when those individuals possess little relevant institutional power.

The immediate discussion concerned how status, demographic classification, institutional norms, and possible animus can affect the moral weight assigned to audience preferences.

## Core model

```text
group classified as dominant
        ↓
assumed stock of compensating privilege
        ↓
individual loss receives lower marginal moral weight
        ↓
dominance discount
```

## Claims and implications

The mechanism can operate without explicit hatred. It can also coexist with animus and thereby magnify its institutional effects.

This is a conceptual entry, not an empirical verdict about any specific industry. Applications should separate observed outcomes, inferred incentives, and directly evidenced attitudes.

## Examples

### Clean example

A design tradeoff imposing dissatisfaction on an established male audience is defended as relatively unimportant because men are presumed already over-served or socially advantaged.

### Borderline example

A public argument uses both commercial segmentation and moral language, making it unclear how much of the decision is explained by market strategy, institutional prestige, structural bias, or hostility.

### Cross-domain example

Comparable dynamics can occur in politics, education, advertising, entertainment, philanthropy, and institutional policy whenever group classification changes how claims are weighted.

## Non-examples

Giving less weight to a constituency because it is numerically small or commercially unimportant is not a dominance discount.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Classification as dominant may sometimes track real differences in power, but the inference from aggregate group status to every individual interest requires separate justification.

Do not collapse unequal outcomes into animus automatically, and do not erase documented contempt by redescribing it as neutral allocation. Structural bias and hostile affect are separate variables.

## Operationalization / evidence

Distinguish policy or allocation patterns from rhetoric and attitude evidence. Structural claims require repeated asymmetry or institutional rules; animus claims require evidence of contempt, hostility, disgust, ridicule, stereotyping, or similar negative affect directed at the group as such.
