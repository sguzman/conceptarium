# Knowledge needs a return address

> **Knowledge needs a return address.**

## Definition

This is an **amber phrase**: wording preserved because the compression is itself useful.

The phrase means that a piece of information should not be treated as if it arrived from nowhere.

We should be able to ask:

- Who knew it?
- How could they know it?
- What recorded it?
- What transmitted it?
- What transformations occurred?
- Why is this claimant entitled to repeat it?

The “return address” is not necessarily one URL or citation.

It is the conceptual location from which the knowledge can be traced backward into an **epistemic chain of custody**.

## Problem pressure

The phrase emerged from the same frustration that produced diegetization.

Narration often hands the reader facts without giving those facts any location in the world.

Modern framing devices can feel satisfying because they give knowledge a place to live: a diary, witness, archive, recorder, memory, or narrator.

The phrase generalized that intuition beyond fiction.

## Why preserve the exact wording

“Provenance matters” is accurate but weak.

“Knowledge needs a return address” turns an abstract epistemological requirement into a spatial intuition.

A claim arrives like a letter.

The first question is not only “is the letter persuasive?” but:

> **Where could I send it back?**

That image makes provenance cognitively sticky.

## Relations

### Epistemic chain of custody

The chain is the full formalization of the return address.

### Epistemic debt

A return address does not automatically pay epistemic debt, but without one the debt is often difficult even to inspect.

### Diegetization

Diegetization gives fictional knowledge a return address inside the world.
