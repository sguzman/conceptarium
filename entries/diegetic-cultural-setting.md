# Diegetic cultural setting

## Definition

**Diegetic cultural setting** is the explicit cultural location inside the represented world: its language, geography, architecture, institutions, customs, history, and social environment.

## Problem pressure

The term was necessary to keep two levels separate. “This game is set in Japan” describes the diegesis. It does not answer “through what cultural-artistic sensibility is this world authored?”

The immediate motivating case was early *Silent Hill*: Japanese creators depicting an American-coded world that does not thereby become culturally “American-authored” in its artistic perception.

## Core model

```text
fictional world
   └── explicit cultural location
        = diegetic cultural setting

creator / tradition
   └── artistic perception and transformation
        = cultural authorship
```

## Claims and implications

Once separated, setting changes can be analyzed without assuming authorship changes. This is especially important in transnational genres, where one culture repeatedly imagines another.

The concept is intended to separate **represented culture** from **the cultural perspective doing the representing**. Those can coincide, but they need not.

## Examples

### Clean example

A Japanese-developed horror game is diegetically set in rural America. America is the diegetic cultural setting; Japanese influence may remain active at the authorship level.

### Borderline example

A work uses extensive foreign reference material and strives for documentary accuracy, yet selection, composition, pacing, atmosphere, and dramatic emphasis still reflect the makers' own artistic formation.

### Cross-domain example

The same distinction can apply to literature, animation, architecture, music, advertising, games, film, and historical reconstruction whenever one cultural tradition represents another.

## Non-examples

The cultural background of the development team is not itself part of the diegetic setting unless represented in the fictional world.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Some works use deliberately indeterminate or composite settings. In those cases diegetic cultural setting may be plural, fictionalized, or weakly specified.

Do not essentialize cultures into fixed psychologies. “Japanese,” “American,” or any other cultural authorship can contain competing traditions, generations, subcultures, and individual departures. The concept names patterned artistic mediation, not ethnic destiny.

## Operationalization / evidence

Useful evidence includes creator interviews, reference sources, production history, comparative formal analysis, recurring compositional choices, genre conventions, visual and sonic grammar, and contrasts with works made from within the represented culture. Claims should distinguish direct evidence of influence from interpretive inference.

## Relations

Diegetic cultural setting **distinguishes from** cultural authorship and tends to increase representational explicitness because cultural location is communicated through visible world elements.

## Provenance

### First known appearance

2026-09-05, in the *Silent Hill* cultural-authorship / franchise-analysis discussion.

### Immediate context

The discussion rejected the idea that a Japanese-created franchise needs an overtly Japanese diegetic setting in order to be culturally Japanese in an artistically meaningful sense.

### Problem being solved

Ordinary nationality labels were too coarse. “Japanese game set in America” did not explain how cultural perspective can remain active at the level of perception, selection, composition, atmosphere, and transformation.

### Conceptual ancestors

Cross-cultural representation, adaptation, cultural translation, genre formation, aesthetic influence, estrangement, and authorship.

### Later refinements

None yet.

### External antecedents

There are obvious overlaps with cultural studies, adaptation studies, transnational cinema, Orientalism/Occidentalism, reception studies, and theories of cultural translation. Future literature comparison should map overlap without erasing the local conceptual distinctions.
