# Erotic opportunity cost

## Definition

**Erotic opportunity cost** is the foregone erotic or aesthetic satisfaction associated with allocating scarce design resources elsewhere. It treats audience pleasure as one possible use of a limited cultural portfolio.

## Problem pressure

The term was needed because discussions of representation often count the benefit to newly served audiences while treating the satisfaction lost by incumbent audiences as if it were nonexistent rather than an allocation cost.

The immediate problem was how to describe finite character slots, marketing attention, art budgets, and audience targeting without pretending that every form of “inclusion” is purely additive.

## Core model

```text
scarce design resource
      ↓ choose constituency / objective B
cannot simultaneously use same resource for erotic preference A
      ↓
foregone satisfaction = erotic opportunity cost
```

## Claims and implications

Opportunity cost does not imply that the foregone preference should win. It simply makes the tradeoff visible rather than morally disappearing it.

These concepts describe allocation under scarcity. They do not assume that reallocations are illegitimate; the purpose is to make tradeoffs and constituency effects explicit.

## Examples

### Clean example

A fixed protagonist slot is assigned to a design intentionally avoiding conventional male-oriented erotic appeal, so the opportunity to use that slot for such appeal is foregone.

### Borderline example

A sequel expands its cast and changes design priorities simultaneously, so some groups gain new representation while others lose relative share without losing absolute quantity.

### Cross-domain example

The same logic can apply to casting, publishing lists, advertising inventories, programming schedules, product portfolios, and institutional attention.

## Non-examples

An unlimited mod ecosystem adding many alternative designs may have little direct erotic opportunity cost between constituencies.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The cost depends on actual scarcity and substitution. It should not be invoked when audiences can be satisfied independently at low marginal cost.

Do not treat all cultural attention as strictly zero-sum. Budgets, roster sizes, and markets can expand. The relevant question is which resources are actually scarce in the decision being analyzed.

## Operationalization / evidence

Identify the scarce resource, the before/after allocation, the constituencies being targeted, and whether change is additive, substitutive, or mixed. Where possible, separate absolute quantity from portfolio share.
