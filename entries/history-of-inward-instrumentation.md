# History of inward instrumentation

## Definition

The **history of inward instrumentation** studies intellectual history through the tools people build for investigating themselves.

Its object is not "the discovery of the self."

Its object is the changing **instrumentarium of self-observation**.

~~~text
confession
diary
autobiography
essay
novel
moral inventory
psychological vocabulary
clinical interview
psychoanalysis
self-tracking
        ↓
different instruments for
making subjective experience observable,
storable, comparable, and revisable
~~~

## Governing thesis

The strongest defensible formulation is:

> Cultures can increase the resolution, recursion, storage, and procedural elaboration of inward examination by developing new concepts, genres, institutions, and recording practices.

This does not imply that earlier cultures lacked subjectivity.

## Master genealogy

~~~text
EARLY-MODERN INWARD TURN
        ↓

        ↓

~~~

This chain is a research program, not a settled universal chronology.

## Instrument dimensions

An inward instrument can alter:

### Resolution
How finely inner states can be distinguished.

### Recursion
Whether the observer and procedure can themselves become objects.

### Storage
Whether subjective observations persist across time.

### Comparison
Whether present experience can be compared with earlier records or other persons.

### Vocabulary
Whether previously diffuse experiences acquire stable names.

### Procedure
Whether examination can be repeated according to explicit rules.

## Connection to self-legibility

The historical program explains one route by which [Legibility of the self](./legibility-of-the-self.md) can become culturally scaffolded.

It complements rather than replaces the phenomenological architecture.

## Anti-collapse rules

- instrumentation ≠ invention of inwardness;
- more records ≠ more truth;
- genealogy ≠ inevitability;
- resemblance ≠ descent;
- Europe ≠ universal history;
- recursive examination ≠ healthy examination;
- self-availability ≠ conscious sovereignty.

## Relation to being a problem to oneself

As inward tools become more elaborate, the observer can increasingly become an explanatory object.

That supports the condition named by [Being a problem to oneself](./being-a-problem-to-oneself.md).
