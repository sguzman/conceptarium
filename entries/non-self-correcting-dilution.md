# Non-self-correcting dilution

## Definition

**Non-self-correcting dilution** is semantic dilution that lacks effective negative feedback. The broadening work succeeds, gains defenders, becomes canonical, and supplies a template for later installments. Because the deviation is rewarded rather than punished, the franchise boundary does not rebound.

## Problem pressure

The user identified this as the more consequential scenario: a weak departure often dies; a strong departure reproduces. The very mechanisms that make a work worth keeping can prevent the franchise from restoring its older specificity.

The immediate case was franchise evolution around *Silent Hill*, especially the possibility that successful, acclaimed departures may change the category more effectively than obvious failures.

## Core model

```text
diluting departure
      + success
        ↓
positive audience / market feedback
        ↓
canonical adhesion + imitation
        ↓
no corrective marginalization
        ↓
dilution persists and compounds
```

## Claims and implications

Non-self-correction helps explain why franchise identity can change through a sequence of successes rather than a sequence of failures. It is a mechanism of stable semantic broadening.

This family deliberately separates two judgments: **is the work good?** and **what does admitting this work do to the meaning of the franchise?**

## Examples

### Clean example

A highly acclaimed departure becomes one of the most beloved entries, and its novel assumptions are repeated until they become part of the normal franchise definition.

### Borderline example

A well-received installment introduces several new assumptions but also preserves much of the older grammar; the key dispute is whether the change is ordinary evolution or category drift.

### Cross-domain example

The same logic applies to institutions, political labels, genres, product categories, and intellectual traditions whose names persist while membership criteria change.

## Non-examples

A popular installment that introduces novelty without weakening older identity conditions is successful evolution, not non-self-correcting dilution.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The term presupposes an argument that dilution occurred. Success alone does not establish it. Analysts must avoid treating any beloved change as corrupting simply because it altered precedent.

“Dilution” here is semantic, not automatically qualitative. A broader category can contain better works than the narrower one it replaced.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track changes in category extension across time: which works are admitted, which traits cease to be necessary, what audiences expect from the label, what creators imitate, and whether formerly exceptional features become normal. Separate quality measures from identity measures.

## Relations

The concept **specializes** semantic dilution, **contrasts with** self-correcting dilution, **overlaps with** good dilution, and is **intensified by** canonical adhesion.
