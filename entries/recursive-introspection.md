# Recursive introspection

## Definition

**Recursive introspection** is self-examination that includes the machinery of self-examination itself.

Ordinary introspection asks:

> What am I feeling, wanting, fearing, remembering, or avoiding?

Recursive introspection adds:

> Why am I interpreting it this way?

> What does the part of me asking the question want?

> What standards is the evaluator using?

> Why should those standards have authority?

The investigator stops occupying an invisible neutral position.

~~~text
impulse / memory / desire
        ↓ examined by
interpretation
        ↓ examined by
interpreter
        ↓ examined by
tribunal
        ↓ examined by
procedure
~~~

## Problem pressure

A self-analytical person can become extremely rigorous toward selected parts of the psyche while granting the analytical voice itself immunity.

That produces a hidden asymmetry:

~~~text
everything else
    → evidence object

the investigator
    → presumed neutral authority
~~~

The recursion exists to break that exemption.

## Recursion is not infinite skepticism

The concept does not require paralyzing every conclusion with endless doubt.

The point is that **no level acquires permanent immunity merely because it performs analysis**.

Recursion may stop provisionally when:

- evidence is adequate;
- the stakes are low;
- further inquiry produces little value;
- a conclusion remains revisable;
- no authority is being treated as metaphysically exempt.

## What becomes inspectable

Recursive introspection can turn scrutiny toward:

- motives behind interpretation;
- pride in being analytical;
- fear of a particular conclusion;
- desire for moral innocence;
- hidden punishment motives;
- assumptions about authenticity;
- standards of evidence;
- the emotional rewards of judging oneself;
- the procedure of the internal tribunal.

## Relation to self-interrogation

[Self-interrogation](./self-interrogation.md) questions claims, motives, contradictions, and defenses.

Recursive introspection **refines** it by adding the questioning process to the field of inquiry.

~~~text
self-interrogation:
"What is going on in me?"

recursive introspection:
"What is going on in the part deciding what is going on in me?"
~~~

## Relation to investigating the investigator

Recursive introspection is the general mechanism.

[Investigating the investigator](./investigating-the-investigator.md) is the focused operation of analyzing the reflective authority itself.

## Relation to recursive expansion

Every successful recursive step can expose another level that had previously been background.

That produces [Recursive expansion](./recursive-expansion.md):

~~~text
object
→ interpretation
→ interpreter
→ tribunal
→ procedure
→ standards behind procedure
~~~

The existence of another possible level does not mean every level always needs analysis.

## Examples

A person notices jealousy, then asks whether their harsh interpretation of jealousy is partly driven by pride in being morally disciplined.

Someone reaches a self-critical conclusion, then asks whether the evidence standard used against themselves would be accepted if applied to another person.

A person notices that “rational analysis” reliably ends in humiliation and investigates whether punishment has become part of the analytic method.

## Non-examples

Recursive introspection is not:

- endless rumination;
- distrusting every thought;
- refusing to make decisions;
- treating all internal voices as equally reliable;
- ordinary self-awareness;
- automatic self-condemnation.

## Boundaries and failure modes

### Infinite regress

Recursion can become sterile if every conclusion merely creates another compulsory level of review.

### Suspicion addiction

The fact that the investigator can be biased does not mean it is always deceptive.

### Analysis as avoidance

Recursive thought can displace action or direct emotional contact.

### Procedural theater

One can perform meta-analysis rhetorically while preserving the same favored conclusion.

## Operationalization

Evidence of recursive introspection includes:

- explicit analysis of the evaluator's motives;
- comparison of standards applied by the internal tribunal;
- willingness to revise the method of self-analysis;
- recognition that analytical authority itself needs provenance;
- ability to distinguish evidence from the evaluator's emotional investment.

## Relations

**Self-interrogation** is the broader practice refined by recursion.

**Investigating the investigator** is enabled by recursive introspection.

**Recursive expansion** is the structural consequence when each analytical level exposes another.

**Legibility** is associated because recursion converts formerly background operations into explicit objects of reasoning.
