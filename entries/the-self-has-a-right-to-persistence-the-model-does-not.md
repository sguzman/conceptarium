# The self has a right to persistence; the model does not

## Definition

The principle that correction should destroy error, not the person. Intellectual failure requires revision of the theory, not self-erasure.

The phrase assigns disposability to representations rather than to the person who authored them.

## Problem pressure

The thread needed a compact way to uncouple epistemic defeat from existential defeat.

The broader thread was trying to preserve exceptional intellectual capacity without either converting it into jurisdiction over others or demanding that the person erase the ego that carries the capacity.

## Core model

~~~text
self ≠ model
model falsified → revise / destroy model
self persists → continues building
~~~

## Claims and implications

This can make aggressive correction psychologically survivable by protecting continuity of authorship and selfhood.

This entry belongs to the architecture of **psychic sovereignty, epistemic submission, and social non-domination**.

## Examples

### Clean example

A theorist treats a failed framework as something to discard while continuing to value their own capacity to build the next one.

### Borderline example

A case in which similar outward behavior exists but the underlying motive, exit option, or constitutional status differs.

## Non-examples

Insisting that a cherished model must persist because abandoning it would threaten identity.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Persistence of self does not mean immunity from moral accountability for harms caused by a model.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track the concrete features named by the concept rather than inferring equivalence from appearances alone. For dependency claims, inspect credible exit and fallback options. For epistemic claims, inspect actual response to correction. For psychic-governance claims, distinguish internal desire from external imposition.
