# Mental note

## Definition

A **mental note** is a compressed conclusion stored by an earlier self.

Examples:

> “Dependence is dangerous.”

> “Never reveal this.”

> “If I fail here, I will be humiliated.”

> “People like this cannot be trusted.”

The note may preserve useful information while losing the circumstances that originally justified it.

## Compression

Mental notes trade detail for speed.

~~~text
complex experience
        ↓
compressed conclusion
        ↓
fast future guidance
~~~

That can be adaptive when a person lacks time, power, vocabulary, or safety.

## Temporal problem

The note can outlive:

- the original environment;
- the original dependency;
- the original threat;
- the evidence available at formation;
- the developmental capacity of the authoring self.

This makes the note a candidate for later review.

## Relation to inherited internal rule

Repeated or high-stakes mental notes can become [Inherited internal rules](./inherited-internal-rule.md).

A compact conclusion stops being merely remembered and starts governing behavior.

## Relation to provenance

A note becomes more intelligible when its [Provenance of belief](./provenance-of-belief.md) is reconstructed.

## Non-examples

A mental note is not every explicit memory or conscious plan.

The concept emphasizes a **compressed conclusion with durable governing potential**.
