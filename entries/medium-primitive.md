# Medium primitive

## Definition

**Medium primitive** is a basic building block or capability specific to a medium from which more complex expressive structures can be composed.

The term is being preserved as **provisional** because the distinction is already useful and load-bearing, while its exact boundary against neighboring animation vocabulary may sharpen through later comparative analysis.

## Problem pressure

The final part of the glossary moved from individual tricks to systems. Recurrent operators, medium primitives, invariants, and animator-specific conventions can form a grammar; if stable enough, that grammar begins to imply how change itself works in the represented world.

The concept exists to prevent that pressure from collapsing back into a generic claim that animation is merely "expressive." It names a more specific formal role.

## Core model

```text
medium primitives
      +
formal operators
      +
recurring constraints / invariants
      ↓
animation grammar
      ↓
possible local metaphysics of change
```

The important analytical move is to treat the named structure as something that can be inspected independently rather than inferred from overall quality or stylistic impression.

## Claims and implications

- Animation can carry information in **operations and transformations**, not only in static poses or depicted objects.
- A formal device becomes theoretically interesting when it recurs, constrains later choices, or changes what an observer can infer.
- Surface realism is therefore neither necessary nor sufficient for the kind of legibility this concept describes.
- The stronger claim—that the device forms part of a stable grammar or world-law—requires recurrence and should not be inferred from a single striking example.

## Example

A work repeatedly uses the same impossible snap, morph, reconstruction, or timing rule until viewers can anticipate what transformations are permitted even before a particular instance occurs.

For **Medium primitive**, the relevant question is specifically whether the phenomenon described in the gloss is doing interpretive work in that sequence.

## Non-example

A one-off stylistic flourish with no recurrence, no constraint, and no predictive value for later transformations.

A merely unusual image or transformation should not count just because it is visually dramatic.

## Boundaries and failure modes

### Inflation

Do not label every stylized movement as **Medium primitive**. The term should identify a repeatable formal distinction, not function as praise for animation one likes.

### Retrospective over-reading

A viewer may discover meaning after the fact that was not structurally supported by the sequence. Prefer interpretations that can be tied to observable timing, transformation, recurrence, or preserved invariants.

### Grammar overclaim

A single instance can establish an example of the phenomenon without establishing a full animation grammar, local physics, or metaphysics.

## Operationalization / evidence

Look for recurrence, constraint, compositionality, and predictive value. A grammar claim is stronger when knowing prior operations helps predict later allowable transformations.

Useful evidence includes repeated instances, contrasting realizations of similar state changes, and cases where removing or replacing the formal operation predictably changes interpretation.
