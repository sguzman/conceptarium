# Anti-domination

## Definition

**Anti-domination** is the central normative commitment that power should not become arbitrary, unilateral, and practically unanswerable.

The target is not all hierarchy, dependence, authority, expertise, coordination, or influence.

The target is a condition in which one actor or system can substantially determine another's options while the affected person lacks meaningful means to:

- resist;
- appeal;
- exit;
- negotiate;
- organize;
- inspect the rules;
- invoke counter-authority;
- alter the dependency.

The concept therefore differs from a simplistic ideal of total independence.

Humans are interdependent.

The question is whether dependence is **dominating**.

## Problem pressure

Anti-domination became the common normative thread connecting several otherwise different research programs.

### System-building

If system-building is valuable, what prevents the builder from turning people into components?

Answer: **Sovereign system builders**.

### Liberalism and dependency

If freedom from family merely shifts dependence to markets or institutions, how should the new system be judged?

Answer: by whether the new dependency is more contestable and less arbitrary.

### Village continuity

If communities deserve continuity, what prevents “continuity” from becoming a justification for coercing their own members?

Answer: community preservation is constrained by anti-domination.

### Utopian politics

The desire for a better world cannot license unlimited power over people in the name of the future.

The same constraint applies.

## Core model

```text
dependency or authority
      ↓
can the powerful party act arbitrarily?
      ↓
are rules visible and challengeable?
      ↓
are exit / appeal / counter-power real?
      ↓
degree of domination
```

## Domination versus interference

A system can interfere heavily without being maximally dominating if interference is:

- rule-bound;
- transparent;
- appealable;
- democratically revisable;
- constrained by rights;
- subject to counter-power.

Conversely, a relationship can involve little day-to-day interference but still be dominating if one party possesses unchecked discretionary power.

The threat can matter even when unused.

## Dependency is not automatically domination

A child depends on caregivers.

A patient depends on clinicians.

A citizen depends on infrastructure.

A worker depends on income.

The normative project is not to abolish these dependencies but to design them so that no single gatekeeper can easily convert necessity into arbitrary control.

## Contestability

**Contestability** is the primary institutional implementation of anti-domination.

A power becomes less dominating when the affected person can meaningfully challenge:

- a decision;
- a category;
- a rule;
- a factual claim;
- an allocation;
- an authority.

This is why legibility must be bidirectional: institutions should not only see people; people should be able to see enough of institutions to contest them.

## Village tension

Anti-domination produces a deliberate two-sided constraint.

Large systems should not casually destroy local communities.

Local communities should not casually dominate members in the name of continuity.

The desired architecture is therefore neither pure centralization nor romantic localism.

It is **nested sovereignty with contestable power**.

## Non-examples

Anti-domination is not:

- opposition to all authority;
- radical self-sufficiency;
- never being constrained;
- never depending on anyone;
- automatic preference for markets or states;
- automatic preference for local communities.

Each arrangement must be evaluated by its actual power structure.

## Boundaries and failure modes

### Formal contestability

An appeal right that is prohibitively expensive or inaccessible is not meaningful contestability.

### Exit fetishism

“Just leave” is inadequate when exit destroys one's livelihood, family, citizenship, or social world.

### Diffuse domination

Power can be spread across systems such that no single actor appears responsible while the person remains trapped.

### Counter-power capture

Institutions of appeal can themselves become closed.

## Operationalization / evidence

Ask:

- Who can make consequential decisions?
- How discretionary are they?
- Are reasons required?
- Are rules knowable?
- Can errors be corrected?
- Is there independent review?
- Can affected people organize?
- Are alternatives available?
- What is the cost of exit?
- Can the institution retaliate against challenge?

## Provenance

Anti-domination existed before Conceptarium, but became a central local organizing principle across the user's political, ethical, and system-building thought.

It is especially important because it constrains both the Fallen urge to totalize and the Village urge to preserve continuity.
