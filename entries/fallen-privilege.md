# Fallen privilege

## Definition

The claimed right of the developed, system-building intellect to enjoy its own expansiveness, ambition, distinction, and private grandeur without apologizing for those capacities, so long as they are not converted into coercive claims over others.

The word privilege is used normatively here: a permission to retain certain internal capacities, not a sociological claim that all Fallen people possess social privilege.

## Problem pressure

Once the thread rejected self-erasure as the solution to Fallen danger, it needed a positive account of what the ego is allowed to keep.

The broader thread was trying to preserve exceptional intellectual capacity without either converting it into jurisdiction over others or demanding that the person erase the ego that carries the capacity. The concept therefore belongs to a constitutional rather than purity-based solution to Fallen danger.

## Core model

~~~text
expansiveness / ambition / distinction / private grandeur
        ↓ legitimate enjoyment
external coercive claim?
        ├─ no → permitted
        └─ yes → constitutional restraint
~~~

## Claims and implications

Anti-domination need not require aesthetic, intellectual, or psychological self-diminishment.

This entry should be read as part of a larger architecture separating **psychic sovereignty**, **epistemic submission**, and **social non-domination**. It does not by itself establish an empirical generalization about all intellectually developed or status-sensitive people.

## Examples

### Clean example

A system-builder takes intense pride in constructing an ambitious theory while refusing to treat that achievement as authority over other people.

### Borderline example

A case in which outward behavior resembles the concept but motive, exit capacity, institutional power, or evidentiary status remains ambiguous.

## Non-examples

Using exceptional ability as justification for unilateral control of others.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The principle does not immunize vanity, cruelty, or exploitation from criticism merely because they are framed as private grandeur.

Do not treat Fallen/Village vocabulary as a biological caste or proof of moral superiority. It models formation, orientation, capacity, dependency, status pressure, and institutional hazard.

## Operationalization / evidence

Where the concept makes an empirical claim, look for observable differences in **exit options, response to contradiction, conversion of prestige into authority, willingness to accept correction, externalization of blame, consent procedures, and actual constraints on power**. Where it is normative, test whether the proposed rule protects other persons without requiring fictitious innocence or total self-erasure.

For autobiographical or historical applications, distinguish direct evidence of motive from inference.

## Provenance

### First known appearance

### Immediate context

The discussion admired Weil's capacity for self-abnegation while questioning whether that strategy is psychologically safe or normatively necessary for a strongly Fallen personality. The alternative that emerged was constitutional: preserve internal force, submit models to reality, and place strong borders around any movement from private theory into claims over other persons.

### Problem being solved

The existing Fallen/Village framework could describe differences in ascent, status, abstraction, and embeddedness, but it lacked a sufficiently precise account of how exceptional capacity can remain ethically legitimate without either self-purification or domination.
