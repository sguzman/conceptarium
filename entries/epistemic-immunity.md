# Epistemic immunity

## Definition

**Epistemic immunity** is exemption from challenge.

An internal claim has immunity when its authority is protected by status rather than continually justified by evidence, procedure, or revisability.

Examples include:

- “This identity cannot be questioned.”
- “The investigator is neutral because it is the investigator.”
- “This memory must be interpreted one way.”
- “This moral intuition cannot have self-interested causes.”

## Immunity versus stability

A stable belief is not necessarily immune.

~~~text
stable conviction
    +
in-principle revisability
        ≠
epistemic immunity
~~~

Immunity appears when no admissible evidence or procedure is allowed to place the claim at risk.

## Why immunity is dangerous

The self can become internally constitutional while still containing a sovereign exception.

One exempt authority can quietly determine:

- what counts as evidence;
- which identities are legitimate;
- what may be investigated;
- which verdicts are thinkable.

## Anti-immunity architecture

Epistemic immunity is constrained by:

- analytical jurisdiction;
- recursive introspection;
- investigating the investigator;
- the exact principle **No part of me gets to say: do not investigate me**.

## Anti-collapse

Denying immunity does not imply:

- universal distrust;
- constant investigation;
- equal evidentiary weight for all claims;
- inability to commit;
- automatic condemnation.

Contestability is enough.
