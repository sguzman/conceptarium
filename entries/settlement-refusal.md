# Settlement refusal

## Definition

**Settlement refusal** is the mechanism by which a grievance remains normatively open because actual or hypothetical settlements are denied the capacity to discharge it.

The strongest form is not merely rejecting a bad offer. It is the prior rule:

> **No possible settlement can terminate the special claim.**

A weaker form occurs when each previously accepted satisfaction condition is abandoned after performance.

## Problem pressure

The cluster needed to distinguish two reasons a conflict remains unresolved:

1. the parties have **not yet reached an adequate settlement**;
2. one side’s grievance architecture gives settlement **no terminating force at all**.

Only the second is settlement refusal in the present sense.

## Core model

~~~text
grievance
  ↓
stated remedy / settlement
  ↓
settlement performed
  ↓
is discharge recognized?
   ├─ yes → closure possible
   └─ no  → settlement refusal
              ↓
       closureless grievance
              ↓
       revanchist ratchet
~~~

## Claims and implications

Rejecting an offer is not evidence of settlement refusal. Many offers are inadequate, coercive, incomplete, or impossible to trust.

The concept is strongest when either:

- the doctrine explicitly denies that any remedy can discharge the claim; or
- previously stated closure conditions are repeatedly displaced after fulfillment without a new independent justification.

## Examples

### Clean example

A movement states that no amount of restitution, institutional reform, or transfer can ever remove the opposing group’s permanent special liability.

### Social example

After each agreed repair condition in a relationship is fulfilled, the injured party declares that fulfillment itself proves the other party remains permanently indebted and subject to unilateral control.

### Borderline example

A settlement is rejected because enforcement is weak and prior agreements were violated. Continued bargaining is not settlement refusal if a credible compliant state would close the claim.

## Non-examples

Refusing an unjust settlement, refusing reconciliation with an unsafe party, or retaining emotional anger after legal settlement are not settlement refusal.

One can also accept legal closure while refusing personal forgiveness.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The concept must not become a demand that victims accept whatever closure powerful actors offer.

Settlement has to be **substantively capable of satisfying the bounded claim** and credible in enforcement before refusal becomes diagnostic.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track:

- stated pre-settlement conditions;
- actual performance;
- later descriptions of what remains owed;
- whether conditions changed before or after compliance;
- whether the claimant recognizes any imaginable discharge state.

Distinguish renegotiation caused by new facts from systematic denial of closure.

## Relations

Settlement refusal stabilizes **Closureless grievance**, enables a **Revanchist ratchet**, and can support **Unbounded revanchism** by preserving the grievance as a renewable source of special claims.
