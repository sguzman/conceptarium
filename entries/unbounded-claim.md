# Unbounded claim

## Definition

An **Unbounded claim** is a normative claim for which the theory does not supply a usable answer to one or more of these questions:

- **How much is owed?**
- **Who owes it?**
- **What would satisfy it?**
- **When does the special claim terminate?**
- **What can falsify or reduce it?**
- **Can concessions discharge it, or do they merely create the basis for further claims?**

The defining property is not severity. It is the absence of a principled **closure condition**.

## Problem pressure

The user asked for a concept capturing political or social claims that seem capable of extending indefinitely.

The conversation distinguished this sharply from simply having a very large demand. A claim for an enormous, explicitly quantified remedy can still be bounded. A much smaller claim can be unbounded if no possible fulfillment ends the claimant’s special entitlement.

The diagnostic question that emerged was:

> **What event would cause the claimant to acknowledge that the special claim has ended?**

## Core model

~~~text
warrant / grievance / status
        ↓
special claim
        ↓
remedy or concession
        ↓
termination rule?
   ┌────┴────┐
  yes        no
bounded    claim remains open
claim         ↓
          expansion / renewal
~~~

Unboundedness can occur in amount, time, target population, domain, remedy type, or inheritance.

## Claims and implications

An unbounded claim can be sincerely held and morally motivated. The concept does not infer cynicism.

It also does not assume the underlying grievance or warrant is false. A perfectly real injury can generate a badly bounded remedy theory.

Conversely, a claim can be unjust and still bounded. **Justice and boundedness are separate variables.**

## Examples

### Clean example

A political movement claims a permanent asymmetrical entitlement based on a historical injury but supplies no condition under which the entitlement expires, even after all stated remedies are granted.

### Social example

A person invokes a past betrayal to justify indefinite monitoring and veto power over a partner, while every act of reassurance creates only new tests rather than closure.

### Borderline example

A reparations program lasts for generations but has explicit eligible classes, quantified remedies, legal review, and a termination rule. It may be large and long-lived without being unbounded.

## Non-examples

A claim is not unbounded merely because the opposing party dislikes its magnitude.

Ongoing harms can justify ongoing claims. If the injurious condition genuinely persists, failure to “move on” is not itself evidence of unboundedness.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The concept can be weaponized to delegitimize claims by demanding premature closure before the underlying harm stops.

Analysts must therefore distinguish:

~~~text
claim persists because harm persists
        ≠
claim persists because no closure condition exists
~~~

A legitimate closure condition also need not be simple or immediate.

## Operationalization / evidence

Write the claim as a rule and attempt to specify:

- liable parties;
- eligible claimants;
- remedy;
- maximum scope;
- renewal conditions;
- expiration conditions;
- treatment of new harms;
- appeal and reassessment.

If no imaginable compliant state would discharge the claim, it is structurally unbounded.

## Relations

**Warrant unbounding** produces unbounded claims. **Unbounded revanchism** is a grievance-driven specialization. **Closureless grievance** is one mechanism that can keep the claim perpetually open. The anti-domination question is whether the claim becomes a standing source of unilateral power.
