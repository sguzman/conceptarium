# Incumbent-audience discount

## Definition

An **incumbent-audience discount** is a commercial weighting rule: loyalty is treated as partially secure, so additional satisfaction delivered to the existing audience is valued less than design changes expected to attract new consumers.

## Problem pressure

The term distinguishes moral or ideological devaluation from a straightforward growth strategy. A company may underserve loyal fans simply because it thinks they are sticky.

The immediate problem was how to describe finite character slots, marketing attention, art budgets, and audience targeting without pretending that every form of “inclusion” is purely additive.

## Core model

```text
incumbent audience assumed retained
        ↓
marginal benefit of pleasing them discounted
        ↓
resources shifted toward acquisition targets
```

## Claims and implications

The mechanism can mimic ideological hostility from the consumer perspective while arising from ordinary market strategy. It can also combine with ideological or prestige incentives.

These concepts describe allocation under scarcity. They do not assume that reallocations are illegitimate; the purpose is to make tradeoffs and constituency effects explicit.

## Examples

### Clean example

A publisher assumes long-term male fans will buy the sequel anyway and redirects design effort toward making the property more appealing to consumers it does not already have.

### Borderline example

A sequel expands its cast and changes design priorities simultaneously, so some groups gain new representation while others lose relative share without losing absolute quantity.

### Cross-domain example

The same logic can apply to casting, publishing lists, advertising inventories, programming schedules, product portfolios, and institutional attention.

## Non-examples

A studio listening closely to its existing customers because retention is fragile exhibits the opposite incentive.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The mechanism requires some belief in incumbent stickiness; without evidence, it should remain a hypothesis rather than inferred motive.

Do not treat all cultural attention as strictly zero-sum. Budgets, roster sizes, and markets can expand. The relevant question is which resources are actually scarce in the decision being analyzed.

## Operationalization / evidence

Identify the scarce resource, the before/after allocation, the constituencies being targeted, and whether change is additive, substitutive, or mixed. Where possible, separate absolute quantity from portfolio share.
