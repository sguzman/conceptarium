# defamilialization

## Definition

**Defamilialization** means reducing the degree to which a person's survival or adult autonomy is compulsory dependent on family, spouse, household, or kin.

It can involve access to:

- independent income;
- childcare;
- housing;
- healthcare;
- education;
- legal status;
- care services;
- mobility.

The goal is not necessarily hostility to family. It is to make family support **less coercively irreplaceable**.

## Problem pressure

The anti-domination framework treats dependency as dangerous when one gatekeeper can turn necessity into control.

Family can provide extraordinary care and continuity. It can also become a source of captivity when leaving means losing housing, money, care, transport, or social identity.

Defamilialization names the institutional strategy of creating credible alternatives.

## Core model

~~~text
basic need
   ↓
only family / spouse / kin can provide it
   ↓
high domination risk

defamilialization:
alternative income / care / housing / rights
   ↓
credible exit
   ↓
family relation becomes less compulsory
~~~

## Claims and implications

Defamilialization can increase individual sovereignty and bargaining power inside families.

But it can also accelerate social disembedding and replace reciprocal care with bureaucratic or market dependency.

The local framework therefore refuses the simple equation “less family dependence = more freedom.” The new dependency must also be evaluated.

## Examples

### Childcare

Public childcare makes employment or education possible without total reliance on relatives.

### Independent income

Benefits or wages give an adult the ability to leave a controlling household.

### Legal recognition

Rights travel with the individual rather than being mediated exclusively through spouse or family status.

## Non-examples

Choosing to live alone is not necessarily defamilialization if practical survival still depends heavily on kin.

Nor is family estrangement itself a policy or institutional structure.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Defamilialization can destroy useful kin capacities if institutions displace rather than supplement them.

It can also produce loneliness or care gaps if formal systems are too thin.

The target is compulsory dependency, not relationship itself.

## Operationalization / evidence

Indicators include:

- share of care available outside kin;
- independent access to income and housing;
- rights attached to individuals rather than household status;
- ability to exit abusive households;
- institutional replacement versus supplementation of kin support;
- administrative burden of alternatives.
