# decommodification

## Definition

**Decommodification** means reducing the extent to which basic survival and security depend on successful participation in commodity and labor markets.

The relevant goods can include:

- food;
- housing;
- healthcare;
- education;
- income support;
- utilities;
- care.

The Conceptarium use is relational rather than utopian: decommodification does not make a person independent. It **changes which institutions or relationships carry dependency**.

## Problem pressure

The local liberalism analysis rejected the equation:

~~~text
freedom from kin
      =
freedom from dependency
~~~

A person who can leave family control may become dependent on wages, rent, credit, insurance, or employers.

Decommodification became useful as one design strategy for reducing the coercive force of market dependency over basic survival.

## Core model

~~~text
basic need
   ↓
market purchase / wage access required
   ↓
high exit cost from market gatekeepers

decommodification:
public / social / guaranteed provision
   ↓
reduced survival dependence on market purchase
   ↓
dependency shifts to institutions with different rules
~~~

## Claims and implications

Decommodification can increase exit capacity from employers, abusive households, or volatile markets.

It does not automatically reduce domination. If public provision is opaque, arbitrary, stigmatizing, or monopolistic, dependency may simply be transferred to a bureaucracy.

The anti-domination question remains: are rules visible, contestable, and appealable?

## Examples

### Healthcare

Access is guaranteed independently of current employment.

### Housing floor

A person retains a basic housing entitlement even after losing wage income.

### Education

Core access does not depend entirely on purchasing power.

## Non-examples

A private market with lower prices is not necessarily decommodified if access still depends on purchase.

Nor does every public subsidy count equally; a voucher may preserve commodity dependence while reducing its cost.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The concept can obscure scarcity.

Removing price at point of access does not eliminate production constraints, queues, rationing, or institutional discretion.

The question is how access is governed, not whether resources become infinite.

## Operationalization / evidence

Measure:

- share of access independent of market income;
- employment-linkage of basic benefits;
- out-of-pocket exposure;
- ability to maintain essential goods during unemployment;
- administrative discretion;
- appeals and portability.

Compare dependency and exit conditions before and after decommodifying reforms.

## Relations

Decommodification is associated with **Shift dependency**, supports **Anti-domination** when it increases exit from market gatekeepers, is enabled by **Institutional mediation**, and contrasts with **Defamilialization**, which targets compulsory family dependence rather than market purchase.
