# Moral equality / moral non-equivalence

## Definition

Two people may possess equal human worth while occupying radically different positions of capacity, dependence, reflexivity, mobility, responsibility, and risk.

Equality and equivalence answer different questions and should not be forced into one scale.

## Problem pressure

The thread needed a two-axis formulation that could defend equal dignity while still describing real asymmetries in agency, formation, and hazard.

The broader thread was trying to preserve exceptional intellectual capacity without either converting it into jurisdiction over others or demanding that the person erase the ego that carries the capacity.

## Core model

~~~text
AXIS 1: human worth → equal
AXIS 2: capacity / dependence / mobility / risk → may differ radically
~~~

## Claims and implications

Responsibility and institutional safeguards can vary with actual position while basic moral standing remains equal.

This entry belongs to the larger architecture of **psychic sovereignty, epistemic submission, and social non-domination**.

## Examples

### Clean example

A powerful executive and an employee have equal human worth but radically different ability to impose costs, exit a relationship, and shape institutional rules.

### Borderline example

A case in which the same outward condition exists but exit capacity, motive, actual power, or scope of authority differs.

## Non-examples

Using capability differences to justify unequal basic rights.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Descriptive categories can be biased or mistaken. Non-equivalence must be evidenced rather than presumed from status labels.

Do not treat Fallen/Village vocabulary as a biological caste or proof of moral superiority.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track the concrete variables relevant to the concept: actual exit options, retained capacities, domain-specific authority, response to correction, consent, contestability, and whether a representational or status advantage is converted into claims over others.
