# Psychological cartography

## Definition

**Psychological cartography** is the practice of constructing explicit maps of inner life.

A map may represent:

- recurring states;
- motives;
- identities;
- conflicts;
- boundaries;
- transitions;
- triggers;
- internal authorities;
- regions of uncertainty;
- relations among these elements.

The goal is not to discover one final true diagram of the psyche.

It is to create a **usable, revisable representation**.

## Problem pressure

Once psychological territory becomes more legible, isolated observations become difficult to manage.

A person may have many local discoveries:

- shame appears in one context;
- a punitive tribunal appears in another;
- one self-state has different values;
- certain transitions reliably follow status threat;
- some motives remain opaque.

Cartography turns scattered findings into a relational structure.

~~~text
observations
   ↓
stable labels
   ↓
relations / boundaries / transitions
   ↓
map
   ↓
comparison and revision
~~~

## What a psychological map can contain

### Regions

Broad classes of experience.

### Boundaries

Conditions under which one state is admitted, suppressed, or unavailable.

### Paths

Recurring transitions between states.

### Agents or roles

Investigator, tribunal, competing self-states, or other functional units.

### Terrain difficulty

Areas that are poorly legible, unstable, or state-dependent.

### Unknown territory

Explicitly represented uncertainty.

## Map versus territory

The central anti-collapse rule is:

~~~text
psychological map
        ≠
psychological territory
~~~

A map is:

- selective;
- purpose-dependent;
- revisable;
- compressed;
- built from limited evidence.

The fact that one representation is useful does not give it ontological monopoly.

## Relation to legibility of the self

[Legibility of the self](./legibility-of-the-self.md) supplies the representational raw material.

Cartography arranges that material spatially or relationally.

~~~text
legibility
→ stable distinctions

cartography
→ structured relations among distinctions
~~~

## Relation to reflective administration

[Reflective administration](./reflective-administration.md) can use a psychological map to:

- anticipate transitions;
- design boundaries;
- compare past episodes;
- decide where further inquiry is valuable;
- preserve unresolved contradictions.

The map informs administration without becoming sovereign over it.

## Relation to self-opacity

A good map must include uncertainty.

Self-opacity should appear as:

- incomplete regions;
- confidence ranges;
- disputed edges;
- unknown causes;
- alternative models.

Erasing uncertainty to make the map aesthetically complete is a cartographic failure.

## Examples

A person maps recurring self-states and the triggers most associated with transitions among them.

Someone diagrams how shame changes tribunal standards and where evidence becomes asymmetrical.

A person records several competing explanations for one recurring desire rather than forcing a single causal story.

## Non-examples

Psychological cartography is not:

- a literal brain map;
- a fixed personality taxonomy;
- one true ontology of self;
- diagnostic certainty;
- complete self-transparency;
- merely journaling without relational structure.

## Boundaries and failure modes

### Map-territory collapse

The representation becomes treated as the self itself.

### Premature ontology

Provisional roles become reified into permanent entities.

### Aesthetic completion pressure

The desire for a beautiful system can erase uncertainty.

### Surveillance overload

Mapping every fluctuation can become exhausting.

### Cartographic colonialism

One internal authority may define other parts entirely on their behalf.

## Operationalization

A useful psychological map should expose:

- units;
- relations;
- confidence;
- provenance;
- transitions;
- unresolved areas;
- revision history.

It should also make it easy to answer:

> What evidence would make me redraw this part?

## Relations

**Psychological territory** is what the map represents.

**Legibility of the self** supplies explicit distinctions.

**Reflective administration** can use the map.

**Self-opacity** constrains claims of completeness.
