# Confession without submission

## Definition

**Confession without submission** permits radical disclosure while retaining final authorship over interpretation.

~~~text
I disclose
    ↓
you may question / compare / challenge / propose
    ↓
your interpretation gains evidentiary weight
        ≠
automatic sovereignty
~~~

The principle separates **epistemic openness** from **jurisdictional surrender**.

## Why the distinction matters

Confession creates vulnerability.

A listener, clinician, priest, institution, ideology, or analytical system can convert access into authority:

> “Because you disclosed this to me, I now know what it ultimately means.”

Confession without submission rejects that conversion.

## External input remains real

Retained sovereignty is not autarky.

Outside interpretation can:

- reveal blind spots;
- supply concepts;
- identify contradictions;
- challenge self-serving narratives;
- provide historical or clinical evidence.

The self does not need to reject external correction in order to preserve authorship.

## Relation to interpretive sovereignty

The principle preserves [Interpretive sovereignty](./interpretive-sovereignty.md).

An interpretation may be compelling enough to adopt.

But adoption should occur because it survives scrutiny, not because the interpreter possesses status.

## Failure modes

### Defensive sovereignty

Rejecting all external correction in the name of authorship.

### Authority capture

Treating credentials, intimacy, or access as sufficient for final interpretation.

### Performative confession

Disclosing only material that preserves control over the expected verdict.
