# Abundance discipline

## Definition

**Abundance discipline** is self-regulation that remains functional after price, shortage, prohibition, effort, or limited access stops doing the restraining. It is discipline under conditions in which one *could* consume more and must therefore generate the stopping rule internally or through chosen commitments.

Its significance lies in separating “I did not overconsume because I could not” from “I could have consumed indefinitely and remained governed.”

The compact originating definition was:

> The capacity to self-impose limits when external scarcity no longer forces restraint. Contrasts with scarcity discipline.

## Problem pressure

The original puzzle was that a person may appear disciplined in a low-access environment without ever having developed the capacity needed for a high-access environment. Rural scarcity, price, household constraints, or simple nonavailability can suppress consumption automatically. Remove those constraints and the underlying self-regulatory problem is exposed.

## Core model / mechanism

```text
external limit disappears
        ↓
pleasure remains available
        ↓
self must generate stopping rule
        ↓
urge is tolerated / redirected
        ↓
consumption remains chosen rather than automatic
```

## Claims and implications

Abundance discipline is not reducible to willpower in the heroic sense. It can include habits, identity, planning, environmental design, self-binding, rituals, or chosen friction. The key is that restraint is no longer merely an accidental consequence of deprivation.

## Examples

Living next to unlimited cheap food while maintaining a chosen diet; having infinite streaming content while reliably stopping at a planned time; having abundant money but keeping spending within self-authored priorities.

## Non-examples

Eating little because there is no food; abstaining because a parent controls access; being unable to purchase the tempting good; losing appetite entirely and therefore facing no temptation.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Do not moralize every refusal as discipline. A person may abstain because the good is unwanted. Do not infer abundance discipline from one visible outcome without knowing access and temptation. Excessive self-denial can itself become compulsive or status-driven.

## Operationalization / evidence

Evidence requires observing high access together with meaningful temptation and repeated successful self-regulation. Natural experiments where access changes are especially informative. Distinguish deliberate restraint from lack of desire.

## Relations

It contrasts with **scarcity discipline**, enables **sovereignty over abundance**, depends conceptually on an **internalized governor**, and can be supported by **synthetic friction** without being identical to it.
