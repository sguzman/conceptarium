# Self-correcting dilution

## Definition

**Self-correcting dilution** is a temporary broadening of franchise identity that fails to reproduce itself. The offending or experimental work is poorly received, ignored, decanonized, quarantined as a spin-off, or simply ceases to influence later design. The category boundary therefore tends to rebound.

## Problem pressure

This emerged as the mirror image of good dilution. Bad departures may damage a release but preserve the franchise's longer-term identity because rejection supplies a negative feedback signal.

The immediate case was franchise evolution around *Silent Hill*, especially the possibility that successful, acclaimed departures may change the category more effectively than obvious failures.

## Core model

```text
diluting departure
        ↓
poor reception / weak attachment
        ↓
low imitation + low precedent value
        ↓
entry marginalized
        ↓
older identity conditions recover
```

## Claims and implications

Failure can paradoxically be identity-preserving. The mechanism also shows why franchise health cannot be measured only by whether any deviant installment exists; what matters is whether the deviation acquires descendants.

This family deliberately separates two judgments: **is the work good?** and **what does admitting this work do to the meaning of the franchise?**

## Examples

### Clean example

A poorly received installment radically broadens the premise, but later games ignore its innovations and audiences treat it as an aberration.

### Borderline example

A well-received installment introduces several new assumptions but also preserves much of the older grammar; the key dispute is whether the change is ordinary evolution or category drift.

### Cross-domain example

The same logic applies to institutions, political labels, genres, product categories, and intellectual traditions whose names persist while membership criteria change.

## Non-examples

A bad installment whose new conventions are nevertheless copied repeatedly is not self-correcting.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Audience rejection is not the only correction mechanism. Corporate strategy, creator turnover, canon policy, or technical constraints can also prevent replication. Conversely, a once-rejected work can later be rehabilitated and become influential.

“Dilution” here is semantic, not automatically qualitative. A broader category can contain better works than the narrower one it replaced.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track changes in category extension across time: which works are admitted, which traits cease to be necessary, what audiences expect from the label, what creators imitate, and whether formerly exceptional features become normal. Separate quality measures from identity measures.

## Relations

Self-correcting dilution **specializes** semantic dilution and **contrasts with** non-self-correcting dilution and good dilution. Canonical adhesion works against self-correction by making departures harder to marginalize.

## Provenance

### First known appearance

### Immediate context

The discussion distinguished ordinary franchise failure from the more subtle case where good work makes a broader or altered franchise definition harder to resist.

### Problem being solved

“Decline,” “bad sequel,” and “selling out” were inadequate because they assume aesthetic failure. The needed vocabulary had to describe identity change that can occur through success.

## Open questions

- How long must a departure remain uninfluential before calling correction successful?
- Can fan rejection correct identity even when the rights-holder keeps pursuing the same direction?
- What causes previously self-corrected experiments to return decades later?
