# Accumulated-precedent redefinition

## Definition

**Accumulated-precedent redefinition** is category change through repetition. Each individual departure may be arguable, exceptional, or modest; together they produce enough precedent that the historical exception becomes the contemporary rule.

## Problem pressure

Franchise debates often evaluate each new installment one at a time. This misses a cumulative effect: five individually defensible expansions can transform the category even if no single installment clearly crosses the line.

This vocabulary emerged from trying to explain how *Silent Hill 2* can be both a great installment and a dangerous template for understanding what the franchise as a whole “really is.”

## Core model

```text
departure 1 → admitted precedent
      +
departure 2 → admitted precedent
      +
departure 3 → admitted precedent
      ↓
new distribution of franchise traits
      ↓
old boundary no longer looks normal
```

## Claims and implications

Redefinition can occur without manifesto, reboot, or explicit retcon. The corpus itself changes the empirical basis from which audiences infer identity conditions.

The framework distinguishes historical influence from normative legitimacy: becoming the dominant prototype does not prove that the prototype was originally representative.

## Examples

### Clean example

After enough games outside the original place or using a different narrative ontology, the claim that the place was once constitutive begins to sound artificially restrictive even if it accurately described the early series.

### Borderline example

A famous installment introduces a feature that later becomes common, but earlier entries also contain weaker versions of it; whether this is capture or genuine clarification remains disputed.

### Cross-domain example

The same mechanism can occur in superhero series, film franchises, bands, product families, institutions, or intellectual traditions when one celebrated member becomes the template through which the whole category is remembered.

## Non-examples

A single experimental spin-off that remains isolated does not create accumulated precedent.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Counting installments mechanically is insufficient. Cultural salience, canonical status, quality, and perceived centrality determine how much precedent each work contributes.

Do not treat popularity itself as distortion. A prototype may be famous because it genuinely crystallized the franchise. The analytical burden is to compare the prototype with the preexisting distribution of traits.

## Operationalization / evidence

Compare the prototype with installments before and after it. Track which traits become more frequent, which are copied in marketing and design, which are cited by creators, and whether descriptions of older entries shift after the prototype's success.

## Relations

Semantic drift can **cause** accumulated-precedent redefinition; canonical adhesion **supports** it. Once established, it **produces** retroactive franchise rewriting and can **stabilize** ontological migration.

### Immediate context

The discussion focused on the possibility that *Silent Hill 2*, despite being exceptional and atypical in some respects, became the cultural model for what later audiences and creators expected from *Silent Hill*.

### Problem being solved

“Influential sequel” was too weak. The needed vocabulary had to describe a prototype changing the interpretation of both later and earlier members of the category.

### Conceptual ancestors

Prototype effects, canon formation, path dependence, cultural memory, sequel imitation, and category learning.
