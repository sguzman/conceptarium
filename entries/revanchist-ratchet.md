# Revanchist ratchet

## Definition

A **Revanchist ratchet** is a directional dynamic in which concessions or successful remedies do not reduce an outstanding grievance claim. Instead, they make the next expansion easier, more legitimate, or more demanding.

The metaphor is mechanical:

~~~text
redress
  ↓
new baseline
  ↓
broader claim
  ↓
further redress
  ↓
new baseline
  ↓
broader claim
~~~

The system moves toward greater asymmetry more easily than back toward ordinary reciprocal standing.

## Problem pressure

The conversation needed to explain how an unbounded grievance can remain active even when concrete demands are met.

The key observation was that successful redress can sometimes be interpreted not as payment of a debt but as **validation of the entire grievance framework**, creating political capital for a broader claim.

## Core model

A ratchet requires at least three moves:

1. a claim is justified by grievance;
2. a remedy is granted;
3. the grant is interpreted as evidence for expanding rather than discharging the warrant.

This differs from discovering a genuinely separate harm. The ratchet concerns the **effect of satisfaction on claim scope**.

## Claims and implications

The mechanism is empirical. It should not be inferred merely because a movement changes demands.

New information, partial compliance, strategic sequencing, or genuinely independent harms can produce escalating demands without a ratchet.

Evidence is strongest when actors explicitly treat prior concessions as proof that further asymmetrical claims are deserved even after earlier stated objectives are complete.

## Examples

### Clean example

A settlement grants the exact remedy originally demanded. Rather than reducing the claim, leaders declare the concession proof that the opposing side admits permanent moral inferiority and therefore owes broader deference.

### Social example

Repeated apologies become evidence that the apologizing party must accept increasingly unilateral rules, with no mechanism for restoration of ordinary standing.

### Borderline example

A first reform exposes a second, previously hidden harm. The second claim may be new evidence rather than ratcheting.

## Non-examples

Sequential reform is not inherently a revanchist ratchet.

Neither is increasing a remedy because the first remedy demonstrably failed to repair the stated harm.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The term can become an accusation that freezes political programs at their first demand. That would be intellectually useless.

The analyst must distinguish **claim expansion because the warrant is being unbounded** from **claim revision because facts changed**.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track demands over time, stated satisfaction conditions, actual concessions, reasons given for subsequent expansion, and whether earlier completion conditions are acknowledged.

A useful indicator is whether every successful remedy systematically increases the claimant’s exceptional jurisdiction rather than narrowing the unresolved remainder.

## Relations

Revanchist ratchet intensifies **Unbounded revanchism**, can be enabled by **Settlement refusal**, and is often associated with **Closureless grievance**.
