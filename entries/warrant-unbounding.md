# Warrant unbounding

## Definition

**Warrant unbounding** is the mechanism by which a justification that originally supports a limited claim is expanded so that its **scope, duration, target, or strength no longer has a clear limiting principle**.

The starting warrant can be positive or negative:

- **achievement:** “I proved competence.”
- **injury:** “I was wronged.”
- **sacrifice:** “I paid the cost.”
- **victory:** “I saved the institution.”
- **credential:** “I passed the relevant tests.”

None of these is automatically illegitimate. The mechanism begins when the inference changes from:

> this fact justifies **this bounded claim**

to:

> this fact gives me a **standing entitlement whose limits need no further proof**.

## Problem pressure

The conversation discovered that Infinite license and Unbounded revanchism looked politically opposite but shared a deep formal structure.

One begins from merit:

~~~text
I achieved something
→ therefore I may rule
~~~

The other begins from injury:

~~~text
I suffered something
→ therefore I may keep claiming
~~~

The common failure is not the content of the warrant. It is the disappearance of the **stopping rule**.

## Core model

~~~text
finite warrant
     ↓
legitimate local claim
     ↓
scope / time / target / remedy expands
     ↓
no renewed justification required
     ↓
unbounded entitlement
~~~

Possible axes of unbounding:

- **scope:** one domain → unrelated domains;
- **time:** temporary → indefinite;
- **target:** responsible party → wider categories;
- **remedy:** repair → punishment → status reversal → standing jurisdiction;
- **inheritance:** original claimant → successors without a new limiting rule.

## Claims and implications

Warrant unbounding does not imply that large claims are illegitimate. A large remedy can remain bounded if the rule tells us what discharges it.

Likewise, a centuries-old claim can be bounded if the legal or moral theory specifies who holds it, against whom, for what, and under what termination condition.

The concept is therefore about **structure**, not size or age.

## Examples

### Clean example

A professional credential initially justifies signing off on a specific technical decision. Over time the credential is treated as evidence that the profession should control unrelated social policy.

### Revanchist example

A compensatory claim for a specific historical injury evolves into a standing claim to asymmetrical status without a stated endpoint.

### Borderline example

A court extends a precedent to a closely analogous case. Expansion alone is not unbounding if the new scope is justified by an explicit rule.

## Non-examples

A claim that grows because new harms are independently established is not necessarily warrant unbounding.

A temporary emergency authority renewed after a fresh legislative finding is not automatically unbounded; the key question is whether new justification is genuinely required.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The analyst must identify the **original warrant** and the **new claim** precisely. Otherwise every development in law or morality can look like expansion.

The concept should not assume that only the original literal scope is legitimate. Generalization can be rational. Unbounding occurs when the process loses principled constraints.

## Operationalization / evidence

Record:

1. original warrant;
2. original claim;
3. later expanded claim;
4. explicit rule authorizing the expansion;
5. satisfaction or expiration condition;
6. parties entitled to invoke it;
7. mechanism for review or revocation.

A case is stronger when steps 4–7 become increasingly vague while the claimed entitlement becomes stronger.

## Relations
