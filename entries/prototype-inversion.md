# Prototype inversion

## Definition

**Prototype inversion** is the reversal by which an atypical but famous member becomes the standard against which formerly typical members are judged. Historical centrality and cultural centrality trade places.

## Problem pressure

If *Silent Hill 2* was once an unusual entry but later becomes the default image of *Silent Hill*, then works closer to the original series structure can paradoxically be perceived as deviations from “what Silent Hill is.” A term was needed for that inversion of normal and exceptional.

This vocabulary emerged from trying to explain how *Silent Hill 2* can be both a great installment and a dangerous template for understanding what the franchise as a whole “really is.”

## Core model

```text
historically unusual member
        ↓ prototype capture
culturally central member
        ↓ later imitation
new norm
        ↓
older distribution now looks anomalous
```

## Claims and implications

Prototype inversion demonstrates that category memory is not a neutral record of history. A later prototype can reorganize salience so strongly that audiences experience historical precedence as deviation.

The framework distinguishes historical influence from normative legitimacy: becoming the dominant prototype does not prove that the prototype was originally representative.

## Examples

### Clean example

Fans treat personal psychological symbolism as the defining franchise norm because the most famous installment centered it, even though earlier entries gave other structures greater weight.

### Borderline example

A famous installment introduces a feature that later becomes common, but earlier entries also contain weaker versions of it; whether this is capture or genuine clarification remains disputed.

### Cross-domain example

The same mechanism can occur in superhero series, film franchises, bands, product families, institutions, or intellectual traditions when one celebrated member becomes the template through which the whole category is remembered.

## Non-examples

A series deliberately announcing a new era and openly replacing its old premise is redefinition, but not necessarily prototype inversion.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The claim requires historical comparison. It is easy to misremember an installment as atypical merely because one personally prefers another era.

Do not treat popularity itself as distortion. A prototype may be famous because it genuinely crystallized the franchise. The analytical burden is to compare the prototype with the preexisting distribution of traits.

## Operationalization / evidence

Compare the prototype with installments before and after it. Track which traits become more frequent, which are copied in marketing and design, which are cited by creators, and whether descriptions of older entries shift after the prototype's success.

## Relations

Prototype inversion is **produced by** prototype capture, **causes** retroactive franchise rewriting, and can **support** accumulated-precedent redefinition.
