# Diegetization

## Definition

In Conceptarium, **diegetization** means taking something that would otherwise float above a fictional or represented world and giving it an **address inside the world or in a defined channel attached to it**.

The most important case is knowledge.

Instead of allowing narration to know things for no articulated reason, a work can give information a carrier:

- a narrator;
- witness;
- diary;
- letter;
- chronicler;
- recovered manuscript;
- recording;
- archive;
- memory;
- testimony;
- institutional record;
- bounded point of view.

The compressed formulation is:

> **Forcing the transcendent to acquire an address inside the world.**

And the even shorter mnemonic is:

> **Knowledge needs a return address.**

## Problem pressure

The concept crystallized from a sudden recognition about **framing devices**.

A framing device is often taught as a stylistic ornament: a story inside a story, a diary conceit, found footage, a manuscript, an elderly narrator remembering youth.

But these devices can solve a deeper problem.

Third-person narration—especially omniscient narration—quietly presents an impossible amount of information. The reader usually accepts this because narration conventionally occupies a privileged position.

Once that privilege becomes intellectually uncomfortable, a question appears:

> **How does the narration know this?**

That is an epistemic problem, not merely an aesthetic one.

A framing device can answer it diegetically by locating the information in a witness, record, memory, archive, or transmission chain.

The insight was that modern fiction often seems interested in **paying off the epistemic debt of narration**.

## Core model

Without diegetization:

```text
author/narrative authority
        ↓
     assertion
        ↓
      reader
```

The knowledge channel is transcendent or conventionally invisible.

With diegetization:

```text
event
  ↓
perception / witness / measurement
  ↓
memory / record
  ↓
transmission
  ↓
narrating position
  ↓
reader
```

The second model exposes an **epistemic chain of custody**.

Every link can now become narratively meaningful:

- witnesses can lie;
- memories can decay;
- documents can be forged;
- archives can be incomplete;
- narrators can misunderstand;
- editors can omit;
- institutions can classify;
- recordings can have blind spots.

Diegetization therefore generates not only realism but **structured uncertainty**.

## Claims and implications

### Narratological claim

Framing devices can be understood as mechanisms for grounding narrative access rather than as mere decorative framing.

### Epistemological claim

Once knowledge is given a route, reliability can be analyzed locally at each transmission step.

### Great Subjectification claim

Diegetization is a small-scale instance of a larger move from self-authorizing knowledge to positioned claims with explicit provenance.

## Examples

### Diary novel

The text exists because a character wrote it. The diary explains both access and limitation.

### Found footage

The camera is physically present in the world. What cannot enter its sensorium cannot be shown without changing the epistemic setup.

### Recovered manuscript

The existence of the story depends on a document surviving and reaching an editor or reader.

### Retrospective narrator

The narration is tied to a person remembering. This gives extraordinary access to one life while creating memory distortions and temporal hindsight.

### Institutional archive

A story told through case files or reports makes the institution itself part of the epistemic machinery.

## Non-examples

Not every in-world detail is diegetization.

A lamp existing in the fictional room is diegetic, but it is not performing the operation described here unless it grounds something that would otherwise sit outside the world.

Likewise, first-person narration is not automatically epistemically complete. A first-person narrator can still report information they could not plausibly possess.

## Boundaries and failure modes

### Mistaking convention for error

Omniscient narration does not *need* to apologize for existing. Diegetization is a useful lens, not a rule that all fiction must obey.

### Infinite regress

If every source requires another source, one can demand an absurdly complete chain. Fiction and real knowledge both eventually rely on accepted primitives, conventions, or inaccessible links.

### False realism

An elaborate document frame can create the *appearance* of epistemic rigor while the underlying information remains impossible.

### Reductionism

A framing device may serve many functions simultaneously: voice, irony, distance, suspense, authenticity, genre play, censorship, or historical imitation. Its epistemic role is one layer.

## Relations

### Epistemic debt

Diegetization can pay the debt created when narration asserts knowledge.

### Epistemic chain of custody

Diegetization gives the chain explicit links.

### The Great Subjectification

It exemplifies the movement from transcendent authority toward a positioned model with explicit access conditions.

### Diegetic truth

A useful distinction is between what is true in the fictional world and what a particular narrator can know about it.

### Immediate context

The user independently realized that framing devices can **contextualize omniscient third-person narration** by solving the narrator’s access problem from inside the fiction.

This connected immediately to a broader observation about modern novels: an unusual hunger for explaining *how knowledge gets transmitted*.

### Problem being solved

Why does modern narration so often bother to justify who is speaking, where records came from, who witnessed what, and how a text reached us when older narrative conventions could simply state events?

The answer proposed here is that narration had accumulated **epistemic debt** and increasingly sought diegetic ways to pay it.

### External antecedents

“Diegesis” and “diegetic” are established narratological terms. The local contribution is the specific emphasis on **diegetization as epistemic localization**: forcing previously transcendent information channels to acquire an in-world address.

This provenance should eventually be compared carefully with existing narratological literature rather than assuming novelty.

## Open questions

- When historically does explicit epistemic grounding become unusually common in prose fiction?
- Does the rise of documentary culture, print, journalism, historiography, or scientific source practice track this shift?
- Are framing devices more common during periods of epistemic distrust?
- Can “diegetization” usefully describe non-narrative systems, or should the term remain primarily narratological?
