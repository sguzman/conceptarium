# Impossibility of de-Fallening

## Definition

The principle that once abstraction, reflexivity, mobility, system-building capacity, or elite cognitive formation has developed, voluntary simplicity cannot restore the person to the original Village condition. Conduct can change; formation cannot simply be erased.

The claim is about persistent or irreversible formation, not an immutable personality essence.

## Problem pressure

The thread used Simone Weil-like self-abnegation to test whether a Fallen person could simply choose Village life. The answer developed here was that behavior may imitate simplicity while formation and optionality persist.

The broader thread was trying to preserve exceptional intellectual capacity without either converting it into jurisdiction over others or demanding that the person erase the ego that carries the capacity.

## Core model

~~~text
developed abstraction / reflexivity / mobility
        ↓
voluntary simplicity
        ↓
conduct changes
formation + capacities + exit options persist
~~~

## Claims and implications

Ethics should focus on governing developed capacities rather than fantasizing about returning to a pre-developmental social position.

This entry belongs to the larger architecture of **psychic sovereignty, epistemic submission, and social non-domination**.

## Examples

### Clean example

An elite-educated intellectual performs manual labor and lives austerely but continues to possess abstract cognition, literacy, mobility, and the ability to exit.

### Borderline example

A case in which the same outward condition exists but exit capacity, motive, actual power, or scope of authority differs.

## Non-examples

A person changing values, habits, or loyalties after elite formation; those changes are compatible with the principle.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Some capacities can atrophy and institutions can strip options. The claim is not that every acquired advantage is literally permanent.

Do not treat Fallen/Village vocabulary as a biological caste or proof of moral superiority.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track the concrete variables relevant to the concept: actual exit options, retained capacities, domain-specific authority, response to correction, consent, contestability, and whether a representational or status advantage is converted into claims over others.
