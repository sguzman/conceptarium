# Shift dependency

## Definition

**Shift dependency** is the claim that many apparent transitions from dependence to independence are better understood as **transfers of dependence**.

A person may become less dependent on:

- parents;
- spouse;
- village;
- landlord;
- patron;
- local church;
- kin network;

while becoming more dependent on:

- wages;
- markets;
- landlords at scale;
- banks;
- credentials;
- state benefits;
- insurance;
- platform access;
- bureaucratic identity.

The normative question is therefore not simply:

> Are people dependent?

but:

> **On whom, through what mechanism, with what exit rights, and under what degree of contestability?**

## Problem pressure

The concept emerged from dissatisfaction with a triumphalist account of liberal freedom.

Formal personal independence can be real and morally important.

But if the analysis stops there, it misses the infrastructure required to make independent life possible.

The user’s broader anti-domination concern made this especially salient: dependency can become less visible when it is distributed across institutions rather than embodied in one controlling person.

## Core model

```text
personal/local dependency
        ↓
disembedding
        ↓
portable formal freedom
        ↓
market/institutional dependency
```

This can be progress if the new dependency is more contestable, plural, rights-protected, and escapable.

It can be regress if the new system is opaque, monopolistic, or impossible to exit.

## Contestability test

A useful evaluation asks:

- Are there multiple providers?
- Can rules be challenged?
- Are rights explicit?
- Can dependency be exited?
- Is failure catastrophic?
- Does one institution control several necessities at once?
- Can the dependent person understand the rules?
- Can collective action alter them?

This turns shift dependency from a slogan into a comparative institutional question.

## Relations

### Social disembedding

Disembedding is one major mechanism by which dependencies shift.

### Institutional legibility

Impersonal support systems need standardized ways to recognize and process people.

### Decommodification

Decommodification can reduce dependence on markets for basic survival.

### Defamilialization

Defamilialization can reduce coercive dependence on family or household members.

These strategies can themselves create institutional dependencies, which should be evaluated rather than denied.

## Provenance

Developed within conversations about liberalism, markets, family, and the idea that modernity often changes the *location* of dependence.

## Open questions

- What forms of dependency are least dominating?
- Is distributed dependency across many institutions safer than concentrated personal dependency?
- Which basic goods should be decommodified specifically to improve exit?
- Can Village support and institutional portability coexist?

- **2026:** Stabilized as a core liberalism/dependency concept.
