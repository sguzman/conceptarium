# Recognition hunger / recognition terror

## Definition

The simultaneous desire to have exceptional achievement accurately recognized and the fear that recognition will mutate into status, generalized authority, entitlement, and domination.

The two poles can coexist in the same person and make both obscurity and prestige psychologically costly.

## Problem pressure

The thread identified an ambivalence toward recognition: invisibility feels like falsification of real achievement, yet social recognition can create precisely the authority structure the anti-domination ethic fears.

The broader thread was trying to preserve exceptional intellectual capacity without either converting it into jurisdiction over others or demanding that the person erase the ego that carries the capacity.

## Core model

~~~text
achievement
   ├─ hunger: 'recognize this accurately'
   └─ terror: 'do not turn recognition into sovereignty'
~~~

## Claims and implications

Prestige without sovereignty becomes attractive because it attempts to satisfy recognition hunger while containing recognition terror.

This entry belongs to the larger architecture of **psychic sovereignty, epistemic submission, and social non-domination**.

## Examples

### Clean example

A creator wants serious acknowledgment of exceptional work but recoils when admirers begin treating that achievement as proof of general superiority or entitlement.

### Borderline example

A case in which similar surface behavior exists but the underlying status, optionality, or jurisdictional structure differs.

## Non-examples

Simple desire for fame without concern about authority or domination.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Recognition terror can sometimes mask ordinary fear of scrutiny or failure. The concept is specific to fear of status conversion into sovereignty.

Do not treat Fallen/Village vocabulary as a biological caste or proof of moral superiority.

## Operationalization / evidence

Where empirical, examine actual options, observable status dynamics, reflexive behavior, institutional authority, and whether prestige or representation is converted into external constraint. Where normative, ask whether the rule preserves equal standing without falsifying real asymmetries.
