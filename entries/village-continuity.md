# Village continuity

## Definition

**Village continuity** is the principle that the successful reproduction of a social world across time can itself be a legitimate objective.

The relevant continuities can include:

- kin;
- place;
- language;
- household;
- custom;
- reciprocal institutions;
- land access;
- livelihood;
- local memory;
- ordinary competence;
- transmission across generations.

The measure is not stasis. It is recognizability and reproduction.

## Problem pressure

Modernization and elite histories often evaluate communities using external metrics:

- growth;
- mobility;
- national integration;
- productivity;
- ideological victory;
- state capacity;
- prestige.

Village continuity asks a different question:

> **Did the people retain the relationships and capacities that made their ordinary social world viable and theirs?**

## Core model

~~~text
social world
   ↓
shock / migration / market / state / technology
   ↓
adaptation
   ↓
are kin, reciprocity, livelihood, local institutions,
memory, and transmission still reproducing?
   ↓
Village continuity or dissolution
~~~

## Claims and implications

Continuity is not passivity.

Communities may litigate, rebel, adopt technology, migrate, reorganize institutions, or form coalitions precisely to preserve continuity.

The best normative version is **Adaptive continuity**, because literal preservation of every custom would turn living communities into museums.

## Examples

### Language

A community adopts bilingual institutions while retaining intergenerational transmission.

### Migration

Members work elsewhere but maintain durable return pathways, care obligations, and local institutions.

### Agrarian adaptation

Production changes while land control and reciprocal community organization remain locally viable.

## Non-examples

Demographic survival alone is not Village continuity if the constitutive social relations disappear.

Touristic preservation of costumes without living transmission is also insufficient.

Nor does continuity require every individual to remain in place.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Continuity can be used to justify coercive conformity.

A community cannot preserve itself by denying individual exit, treating members as reproductive property, or suppressing difference without limit.

That is why Village continuity remains constrained by anti-domination even when anti-domination is not stored here as a direct edge.

## Operationalization / evidence

Assess:

- intergenerational transmission;
- durability of local institutions;
- land and livelihood control;
- reciprocal networks;
- language or customary practice;
- return and care pathways;
- community-defined continuity;
- individual exit and contestability.

Measure both social reproduction and the costs imposed on members.

## Relations

Village continuity prioritizes the **Village** success criterion, is realized through **Adaptive continuity**, is supported by **Local embeddedness** and **reciprocal morality**, and is threatened by **Social disembedding** when local relations are replaced without preserving their functions.
