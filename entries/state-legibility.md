# state legibility

## Definition

**State legibility** is the extent to which social reality has been translated into categories and records that a state can reliably perceive and act upon.

Typical legibility devices include:

- censuses;
- cadastral maps;
- standardized names;
- property titles;
- identity documents;
- tax categories;
- occupational classifications;
- registries;
- standardized measures.

In Conceptarium, state legibility is explicitly a **subtype of Institutional legibility**.

## Problem pressure

Large states cannot govern dense social reality directly.

They require simplification.

That simplification can enable portable rights, taxation, public provision, planning, and accountability. It can also erase customary arrangements that do not fit the schema.

The Village side makes this tension especially visible because local rights and obligations are often carried in memory, repetition, and relational context.

## Core model

~~~text
dense lived reality
      ↓
state-relevant selection
      ↓
standardized records / categories
      ↓
state legibility
      ↓
tax / allocation / regulation / policing / provision
~~~

## Claims and implications

State legibility is not inherently oppressive.

Without records, rights can depend on local power or memory and become impossible to enforce at scale.

But legibility changes what institutions can see. What cannot be represented may become administratively nonexistent.

The strongest danger appears when the state then restructures reality to fit the simplified map.

## Examples

### Cadastre

Land is converted from customary use relations into standardized parcels and titles.

### Census

Fluid social identities are placed into official categories used for allocation and governance.

### Benefits register

Households become visible through identifiers and standardized eligibility data.

## Non-examples

A state possessing general knowledge about a population is not enough.

Legibility requires representation in forms usable for routine administration and comparison.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The concept can romanticize illegibility.

Being invisible to the state can protect autonomy, but it can also block rights, public services, legal claims, and accountability.

The question is not “legible or illegible?” but who controls the categories and whether the map can be contested.

## Operationalization / evidence

Indicators include:

- registration coverage;
- standardized identifiers;
- cadastral completeness;
- census classification;
- tax visibility;
- administrative interoperability;
- proportion of rights dependent on formal records;
- mechanisms for correcting records.

Study mismatches between official categories and local lived arrangements.

## Relations

State legibility specializes **Institutional legibility**, enables **Institutional mediation** and **political domestication**, supports some forms of **ethnographic engineering**, and can threaten **Village continuity** when standardized categories erase locally carried rights and relations.
