# Correction without self-abnegation

## Definition

The possibility of conceding error while preserving ego, ambition, authorship, and intellectual force. One need not become humble in order to become corrigible.

The principle distinguishes humility of claims from humiliation or abolition of the claimant.

## Problem pressure

The thread rejected a hidden equation between being corrigible and becoming psychologically small.

The broader thread was testing whether Fallen danger is better solved by self-erasure or by a constitutional architecture that survives pride, status pressure, and human fallibility.

## Core model

~~~text
error discovered
      ↓
model yields
      ↓
ego / ambition / authorship persist
      ↓
next construction remains possible
~~~

## Claims and implications

Corrigibility can be designed as a model-management discipline rather than a personality-purification program.

This entry belongs to the larger architecture of **psychic sovereignty, epistemic submission, and social non-domination**.

## Examples

### Clean example

A proud theorist accepts decisive criticism, rewrites the theory, and remains equally ambitious about building the next one.

### Borderline example

A case that contains one element of the pattern—humiliation, correction, asceticism, ambition, or prestige—without the full mechanism required by the concept.

## Non-examples

Preserving ego by refusing to concede a demonstrably false claim.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Sometimes correction should alter self-conception when the error reveals a genuine character or competence problem. Persistence is not immunity from learning about oneself.

The concept should not be used to infer violence, domination, or pathology from pride, humiliation, or ambition alone.

## Operationalization / evidence

For humiliation and status mechanisms, trace mediating conditions rather than relying on a single cause. For constitutional principles, inspect whether checks remain effective when the actor is trusted, admired, angry, or wrong. For corrigibility, observe behavior under high-identity-cost correction rather than only trivial mistakes.
