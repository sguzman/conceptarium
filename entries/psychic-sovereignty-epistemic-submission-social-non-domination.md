# Psychic sovereignty, epistemic submission, social non-domination

## Definition

The tight synthesis of the thread’s architecture: maximal freedom within private interiority, maximal submission of models to reality, and strong prohibition on converting internal grandeur into domination over other persons.

Each norm applies to a different jurisdiction; collapsing them creates the pathologies the thread was trying to avoid.

## Problem pressure

The full theory needed a three-part formula that identifies exactly where freedom, submission, and restraint belong.

The phrase preserves the architecture in compressed form so that later theory does not dissolve the original insight into generic language.

## Core model

~~~text
PSYCHIC: sovereignty
EPISTEMIC: submission
SOCIAL: non-domination
~~~

## Claims and implications

The architecture permits a strong ego, a weakly protected model, and strongly protected other persons.

## Examples

### Clean example

A thinker indulges private grandiosity, lets evidence destroy a favored theory, and refuses to impose the theory on unwilling others.

## Non-examples

Demanding humility in all three domains or sovereignty in all three domains.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The triad is a synthesis, not a complete institutional specification.

## Operationalization / evidence

Treat the phrase as compression of the linked framework rather than independent empirical evidence. Applications should be unpacked into the underlying claims about psychic sovereignty, epistemic correction, jurisdiction, and anti-domination.
