# Internal tribunal

## Definition

The **internal tribunal** is the psychological adjudication structure by which a person decides what inner claims are legitimate, illegitimate, admissible, dangerous, false, shameful, binding, or dismissible.

The tribunal can be implicit.

A person need not hear an inner voice saying “court is now in session.”

The structure is visible whenever inner material is treated as making a claim that must receive standing.

~~~text
impulse / memory / interpretation / identity claim
        ↓
investigation
        ↓
standards of evidence and legitimacy
        ↓
judgment
        ↓
admitted / rejected / constrained / deferred
~~~

## Problem pressure

The psychological-opening model already distinguished visibility from admissibility.

That creates a new question:

> Who or what decides what becomes admissible?

The tribunal is the name for that decision architecture.

It lets Conceptarium talk about the **governance of self-interpretation** rather than only access to inner material.

## Tribunal functions

The internal tribunal can:

- decide whether an impulse counts as “really mine”;
- determine how much evidential weight one event receives;
- judge whether a motive is acceptable;
- impose moral constraints;
- reject or admit identity hypotheses;
- arbitrate contradictions between self-states;
- decide whether provenance invalidates legitimacy;
- distinguish recognition from permission to act.

## Tribunal versus investigator

The [Investigator](./the-investigator.md) gathers, interprets, and questions.

The tribunal adjudicates standing.

~~~text
INVESTIGATOR
"What happened, and what does it mean?"

TRIBUNAL
"What status should this claim have inside the self-model?"
~~~

In practice, the same internal process may perform both roles.

## Tribunal versus conscience

Conscience is usually moral.

The tribunal is broader.

It can adjudicate:

- truth;
- authenticity;
- identity;
- coherence;
- relevance;
- legitimacy;
- evidence;
- permission.

Moral judgment is only one jurisdiction.

## Tribunal versus admissible self-model

The [Admissible self-model](./admissible-self-model.md) is the resulting boundary of what can count as self.

The internal tribunal is one mechanism that helps produce and revise that boundary.

## Fairness as a procedural question

A tribunal can reach a correct conclusion through bad procedure.

It can also reach a mistaken conclusion through sincere analysis.

This motivates [Procedural self-analysis](./procedural-self-analysis.md):

> Was the case judged fairly?

That question is separate from whether the verdict feels emotionally comforting.

## Examples

A person recognizes an aggressive impulse, admits that it genuinely belongs to them, but judges acting on it unacceptable.

Someone interprets a one-off state as evidence but gives it limited weight because it does not generalize across contexts.

A person dismisses a recurring identity possibility because the tribunal has a standing rule that “people like me cannot be that.”

The first two may be ordinary adjudication.

The third may reveal a biased procedure.

## Non-examples

The internal tribunal is not:

- literal multiple personalities;
- every conscious thought;
- conscience alone;
- automatic repression;
- proof that the self has one central executive;
- an argument that all inner claims deserve equal standing.

## Boundaries and failure modes

### Courtroom over-literalization

The metaphor should clarify procedure, not force mental life into legal categories.

### Tribunal absolutism

The tribunal can be wrong, biased, cruel, or historically contingent.

### Procedural fetish

Formal fairness does not guarantee substantive truth.

### No-verdict paralysis

Refusing every provisional judgment can become another failure.

## Operationalization

A tribunal becomes visible by asking:

- What inner claim is being judged?
- What standard is being applied?
- Who gets the burden of proof?
- Which evidence is admissible?
- What would reverse the judgment?
- Is the same standard applied across internal parties?
- What consequences follow from the verdict?

## Relations

**The investigator** supplies much of the evidence and interpretation.

**Procedural self-analysis** tests the fairness of the adjudication.

**Cruelty in the tribunal** threatens the architecture when punishment contaminates inquiry.

The constitutional phrase **No part of me gets to say: do not investigate me** constrains the tribunal by denying permanent exemption to any internal authority.
