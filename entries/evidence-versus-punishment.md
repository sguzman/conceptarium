# Evidence versus punishment

## Definition

**Evidence versus punishment** is the rule that an internal fact can be informative without being self-sentencing.

~~~text
"I noticed this motive."
        ↓
evidence about psychology

        ≠

"Therefore I deserve punishment."
~~~

The distinction applies even to morally disturbing or socially undesirable material.

## Why it matters

Confessional epistemology deliberately increases exposure.

Without this rule, greater honesty can perversely increase punishment.

That creates pressure to hide exactly the material the method needs to inspect.

## Three separate questions

1. **What is present?**
2. **What does it mean?**
3. **What response is justified?**

Collapsing those questions creates prosecutorial reasoning.

## Relation to automatic condemnation

[Nothing in me is automatically condemned by examination.](./nothing-in-me-is-automatically-condemned-by-examination.md) is the constitutional axiom.

Evidence versus punishment is the procedural distinction used in concrete cases.

## Relation to containment

Evidence can still justify boundaries.

A motive may be real, informative, and behaviorally dangerous.

[Containment without cruelty](./containment-without-cruelty.md) supplies the response when restriction is needed without punitive surplus.

## Anti-collapse

- evidence ≠ innocence;
- evidence ≠ guilt;
- explanation ≠ permission;
- disturbing ≠ punishable;
- containment ≠ punishment.
