# Confessional epistemology

## Definition

**Confessional epistemology** is a method for learning about oneself by making interior material available, interrogating it, tracing its history, testing interpretations, and revising the self-model.

Its basic machinery is:

~~~text
DISCLOSURE
    ↓
NARRATION
    ↓
INTERROGATION
    ↓
PROVENANCE
    ↓
ADVERSARIAL TESTING
    ↓
RECURSIVE SCRUTINY
    ↓
PROCEDURAL REVIEW
    ↓
REINTERPRETATION
~~~

The method is constitutional rather than merely expressive.

## Constitutional pair

Confessional epistemology requires two simultaneous rules.

### Anti-immunity

Nothing receives permanent exemption merely because it is identity-bearing, emotionally protected, morally prestigious, or performing the analysis.

### Anti-cruelty

Examinability does not create permission for humiliation, punishment, or psychological brutality.

~~~text
UNIVERSAL ANALYTICAL JURISDICTION
        +
NO TORTURE AUTHORITY
~~~

## Confession without surrender

The method may use external audiences, texts, experts, or AI.

But [Confession without submission](./confession-without-submission.md) prevents disclosure from automatically transferring interpretive sovereignty.

External interpretation is evidence-bearing, not self-authenticating.

## Relation to recursive introspection

Confessional epistemology inherits the Batch 13 architecture:

- recursive introspection;
- investigating the investigator;
- internal tribunal;
- procedural self-analysis;
- anti-cruelty constraints.

It adds an explicit **disclosure and evidence-production layer**.

## Relation to psychological archaeology

The method can be used synchronically or historically.

When it investigates where internal rules came from, it becomes psychological archaeology.

That neighboring branch remains queued for later materialization.

## What the method seeks

Not purity.

Not total self-transparency.

Not complete conscious sovereignty.

It seeks:

- more legible evidence;
- better explanations;
- greater procedural symmetry;
- revisability;
- fewer protected blind spots;
- less punitive distortion.

## Failure modes

- confession as performance;
- internal inquisition;
- infinite recursion;
- theory capture;
- external sovereignty transfer;
- cruelty disguised as honesty;
- compassion disguised as evidence;
- overconfidence in autobiographical memory.

## Operational sequence

A practical case can ask:

1. What happened?
2. What did I feel or want?
3. What interpretation did I form?
4. Where did that interpretation come from?
5. What evidence supports it now?
6. What part of me is judging it?
7. Is the procedure symmetric?
8. What would change the conclusion?
9. Is any punitive surplus present?
10. What revised model best fits the evidence?
