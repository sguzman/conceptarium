# The dragon and the property line

## Definition

Metaphor for the whole architecture: allow the internal dragon unlimited psychic territory, but make the external borders extremely strong. The problem is not that the dragon exists; the problem begins when it crosses into someone else’s domain.

The dragon stands for ego, ambition, grandiosity, system-building, and aggressive conceptual energy.

## Problem pressure

The thread wanted an image vivid enough to preserve the architecture without translating it back into self-domestication.

The phrase preserves the architecture in compressed form so that later theory does not dissolve the original insight into generic language.

## Core model

~~~text
YOUR PROPERTY: dragon may roam
================ PROPERTY LINE ================
OTHER PERSON'S DOMAIN: crossing requires permission
~~~

## Claims and implications

The locus of control is the border: strengthen the property line rather than killing the dragon.

## Examples

### Clean example

A person keeps expansive fantasies and theories but treats another person’s project as a property line that cannot be crossed without consent.

## Non-examples

Calling destructive external behavior acceptable because it expresses an authentic inner dragon.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Property metaphors can overstate separateness where actions have diffuse social effects. Some crossings are indirect and require more than literal consent analysis.

## Operationalization / evidence

Treat the phrase as compression of the linked framework rather than independent empirical evidence. Applications should be unpacked into the underlying claims about psychic sovereignty, epistemic correction, jurisdiction, and anti-domination.
