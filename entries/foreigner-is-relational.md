# foreigner is relational

## Definition

**Foreigner is relational** means that foreignness is defined relative to a bounded network of belonging and obligation.

A person can be:

- insider to a household;
- outsider to another kin network;
- insider to a village;
- outsider to a neighboring village;
- insider to a region;
- outsider to a nation;
- insider to a nation relative to an external state.

The category changes with the social scale that defines reciprocal membership.

## Problem pressure

The Village model emphasizes bounded reciprocity and local embeddedness.

Without a relational account, that could slide into an essentialist story in which some people are permanently “foreign” by blood or immutable category.

The relational principle instead asks:

> **Outside which network, with respect to which obligations, at what scale?**

## Core model

~~~text
person
   +
reference network
   ↓
inside reciprocal boundary → insider
outside reciprocal boundary → foreigner

change reference scale
   ↓
category may reverse
~~~

## Claims and implications

Foreignness can be socially consequential without being essential.

A neighboring community may be outside one reciprocal network while becoming an ally inside a broader coalition.

This helps explain nested identities and why political boundaries can expand or contract under pressure.

## Examples

### Neighboring villages

Two villages maintain distinct obligations locally but treat one another as insiders relative to an external state intervention.

### Migration

A migrant is an outsider to one local network while remaining fully embedded in a translocal kin network.

### Coalition

Groups previously foreign to one another construct a new scale of reciprocity through repeated alliance.

## Non-examples

Legal citizenship alone does not determine relational foreignness.

Nor does ancestry automatically determine whether someone is inside a living network of obligation.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The principle should not deny that states and institutions create legally consequential outsider categories.

It also should not romanticize bounded reciprocity: relational foreignness can still support exclusion, hostility, or domination.

## Operationalization / evidence

Identify:

- the relevant network of obligation;
- repeated interaction;
- kin or local membership rules;
- who receives reciprocal claims;
- how boundaries shift across scale;
- whether coalition or migration changes insider status.

Avoid coding “foreigner” without specifying the reference network.
