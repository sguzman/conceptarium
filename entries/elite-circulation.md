# Elite circulation

## Definition

**Elite circulation** is the process by which consequential institutions regularly absorb, replace, promote, or rotate ambitious outsiders rather than allowing elite membership to harden into permanent closure.

Within Fallen/Village theory, the central sequence is:

```text
Rising Fallen
      ↓
incorporation / promotion / succession
      ↓
Established Fallen
```

Healthy circulation does not abolish hierarchy.

It keeps hierarchy from becoming completely impermeable.

## Problem pressure

The theory of Rising Fallen creates a stability problem.

If societies continuously produce ambitious, capable, high-expectation people but elite positions remain inherited or tightly closed, status discrepancy accumulates.

Eventually the system may generate counter-elites whose easiest route upward is institutional rupture.

This suggested that some amount of circulation functions as a **pressure valve**.

The question is not simply whether elites exist.

It is whether the system can absorb new talent without requiring the new cohort to destroy the ladder.

## Forms of circulation

- open examination;
- promotion by demonstrated competence;
- electoral succession;
- professional recruitment;
- institutional founding;
- patronage that genuinely incorporates outsiders;
- geographic or sectoral mobility;
- creation of new consequential fields.

Different mechanisms have different risks.

## Circulation versus openness

A system can be formally open while circulating poorly.

If everyone may apply but almost nobody outside a narrow network succeeds, the ladder remains effectively closed.

Conversely, a system can have explicit standards and still circulate well if outsiders regularly enter through predictable routes.

The important variable is **real incorporation**.

## Stabilizing effect

The working hypothesis is:

```text
ambitious outsider
      +
credible route to incorporation
      ↓
lower incentive for total institutional attack
```

This is not guaranteed.

Some actors prefer transformation for substantive reasons even when offered status.

But blocked status should become less systematically explosive when routes upward are real.

## Risks

### Co-optation

Circulation can neutralize critics by absorbing them individually without fixing broader injustice.

### Oligarchic renewal

An elite can refresh itself just enough to remain dominant.

### Credential capture

Nominal circulation mechanisms can become new forms of closure.

### Village extraction

A system may circulate talent by continuously removing capable people from local communities and concentrating them in central institutions.

## Operationalization / evidence

Measure:

- social origins of new elite entrants;
- intergenerational mobility;
- turnover in consequential offices;
- permeability of professional fields;
- concentration of recruitment institutions;
- rates at which outsider movements become institutionalized;
- whether circulation preserves or destroys source communities.

## Provenance

Adopted into the framework during discussions of pressure valves, ladder closure, and how societies can manage high-Fallen competition without permanent revolution.

## Open questions

- How much circulation is enough?
- When does circulation become mere co-optation?
- Can systems circulate elites while returning capacity to Village communities?
- Do plural ladders substitute for circulation or complement it?
