# Social disembedding

## Definition

**Social disembedding** is the process by which people, rights, obligations, production, care, and identity become less dependent on dense inherited/local relations and more mediated by impersonal systems.

The relevant contrast is not “society versus no society.”

It is a shift from relations such as:

- kinship;
- customary right;
- household;
- guild;
- village;
- patronage;
- local reputation;

toward relations such as:

- contract;
- wage;
- credential;
- account;
- benefit eligibility;
- legal status;
- standardized service;
- market exchange;
- bureaucratic record.

## Problem pressure

The concept matters because **freedom from one dependency often creates another dependency**.

A person freed from family control may gain genuine autonomy.

But they may now require:

- wage income;
- rent payments;
- legal documentation;
- market access;
- childcare services;
- credentials;
- institutional healthcare;
- financial systems.

The vocabulary of liberation can hide this transfer if it treats only personal dependence as dependency.

## Core mechanism

```text
dense inherited relation
        ↓
right/obligation extracted from local context
        ↓
standardized portable form
        ↓
institutional or market mediation
        ↓
new dependencies + new freedoms
```

Disembedding often increases portability and contestability.

It can also dissolve tacit support structures.

## Productive side

Potential gains include:

- exit from abusive kin;
- portability of rights;
- occupational mobility;
- impersonal enforcement;
- access to strangers;
- reduced dependence on local gatekeepers.

## Destructive side

Potential losses include:

- local knowledge;
- reciprocal care;
- customary rights;
- household resilience;
- social memory;
- low-cost trust;
- continuity.

The concept is therefore descriptive before it is normative.

## Relations

### Institutional legibility

Portable impersonal systems generally require standardized records and categories.

### Shift dependency

Disembedding often changes the object of dependency rather than abolishing dependency.

### Village

Village systems are particularly vulnerable because much of their coordination is relational and tacit.

## Provenance

The term is pre-existing in social theory. It became part of the user’s local research vocabulary while analyzing liberalism as a process that can increase personal freedom by transferring dependence from kin and village into markets and institutions.

## Open questions

- Which dependencies become more contestable after disembedding?
- Which forms of local support can be preserved without restoring coercive kin control?
- Can public systems provide portability without demanding destructive standardization?
