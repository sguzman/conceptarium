# Punitive anti-fanservice

## Definition

**Punitive anti-fanservice** is anti-fanservice whose value partly lies in the frustration itself. Refusal is not merely a byproduct of another design goal; denying the targeted preference is treated as morally corrective, resistant, or symbolically virtuous.

## Problem pressure

The term distinguishes ordinary non-fanservice and prestige anti-fanservice from the strongest adversarial case, where audience disappointment is itself interpreted positively.

The immediate problem was how abstract ideology, prestige, corporate incentives, and audience judgments can become concrete design choices without requiring a single centralized command.

## Core model

```text
target preference judged undesirable
      ↓
design refuses satisfaction
      ↓
frustration itself acquires corrective / resistant meaning
```

## Claims and implications

Punitive anti-fanservice can be driven by adversarial governance, animus, or prestige, but those causes should not be presumed without evidence.

The entry is a mechanism for inquiry, not a presumption of motive. A specific application should identify which stages of the mechanism are evidenced and which remain hypothetical.

## Examples

### Clean example

A creator explicitly celebrates denying a male audience the sexualized heroine it wants because the desire itself is framed as entitled or morally backward.

### Borderline example

A design decision fits the predicted mechanism but could also be explained by story needs, technology, individual artist taste, market research, or production constraints.

### Cross-domain example

Comparable governance and allocation mechanisms can operate in advertising, fashion, film, publishing, social platforms, and institutional communication.

## Non-examples

A nonsexualized character designed for narrative realism is not punitive merely because some fans dislike it.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The concept has a high evidentiary burden because motive matters. Outcome alone cannot establish punishment.

Avoid monocausal explanations. Corporate strategy, sincere moral beliefs, professional prestige, audience research, and personal aesthetics can align without being identical causes.

## Operationalization / evidence

Map the proposed causal chain stage by stage. Seek evidence for doctrine, status incentives, decision rules, allocation changes, and concrete output changes separately rather than inferring the whole pipeline from the final artifact.

## Relations
