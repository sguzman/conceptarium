# Semantic dilution

## Definition

**Semantic dilution** is a loss of informational density in a category label. The franchise name continues to denote a set of works, but the set becomes so heterogeneous that knowing the label rules out fewer possibilities and predicts fewer concrete properties.

## Problem pressure

The issue was not simply that franchise meaning changes. Some changes replace one precise meaning with another. Dilution is different: the label becomes broader and less discriminating while often retaining emotional and commercial force.

The immediate case was franchise evolution around *Silent Hill*, especially the possibility that successful, acclaimed departures may change the category more effectively than obvious failures.

## Core model

```text
narrow category + strong exclusions
        ↓
heterogeneous admissions
        ↓
more traits become optional
        ↓
label predicts less
        ↓
semantic dilution
```

## Claims and implications

Dilution explains the coexistence of strong branding and weak specificity. A label can become culturally louder while becoming semantically quieter.

This family deliberately separates two judgments: **is the work good?** and **what does admitting this work do to the meaning of the franchise?**

## Examples

### Clean example

A franchise that once strongly predicted a setting, ontology, and narrative structure comes to include many unrelated stories sharing only broad psychological-horror cues.

### Borderline example

A well-received installment introduces several new assumptions but also preserves much of the older grammar; the key dispute is whether the change is ordinary evolution or category drift.

### Cross-domain example

The same logic applies to institutions, political labels, genres, product categories, and intellectual traditions whose names persist while membership criteria change.

## Non-examples

Replacing one strict franchise ontology with another strict ontology is semantic shift, not necessarily semantic dilution.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Heterogeneity alone does not prove dilution. A franchise can support diverse surface forms under a strong abstract grammar. The test is whether meaningful prediction and exclusion have actually weakened.

“Dilution” here is semantic, not automatically qualitative. A broader category can contain better works than the narrower one it replaced.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track changes in category extension across time: which works are admitted, which traits cease to be necessary, what audiences expect from the label, what creators imitate, and whether formerly exceptional features become normal. Separate quality measures from identity measures.

## Relations

Semantic drift can **cause** dilution. Dilution **threatens** franchise specificity and can **produce** prestige without specificity or, at the limit, franchise identity collapse.

### Problem being solved

“Decline,” “bad sequel,” and “selling out” were inadequate because they assume aesthetic failure. The needed vocabulary had to describe identity change that can occur through success.
