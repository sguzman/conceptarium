# Ontological migration

## Definition

**Ontological migration** is a change in the kind of continuity that defines a franchise. The series may migrate from place to method, protagonist lineage to shared mythology, fictional universe to anthology format, or another ontological basis. The important change is not simply new content but a new answer to “what sort of thing is this franchise?”

## Problem pressure

Moving *Silent Hill* outside its historically central American-coded town raises a deeper issue than setting variation if the town itself was part of the franchise ontology. If the new rule is instead that any psychologically symbolic horror story can be a *Silent Hill* story, the category has migrated.

The concept arose while separating the artistic identity of *Silent Hill* from the commercial and cultural strength of the *Silent Hill* name.

## Core model

```text
ontology A
(place / world / lineage)
        ↓ accumulated reinterpretation
ontology B
(method / anthology / genre)
        ↓
new identity conditions become possible
```

## Claims and implications

Ontological migration can retroactively make older unusual entries appear central and older central features appear contingent. It therefore changes both future possibility space and historical interpretation.

Quality, popularity, and franchise fit are separate variables. None should be silently substituted for another.

## Examples

### Clean example

A franchise once understood as stories generated by one haunted place becomes understood as an anthology framework capable of generating unrelated locations united by psychological-symbolic horror.

### Borderline example

A new installment preserves several familiar cues and is marketed as a major franchise entry, but whether its deeper organizing logic belongs to the older lineage remains disputed.

### Cross-domain example

Comparable dynamics can occur whenever a powerful label becomes portable across products whose internal similarity is weaker than their shared branding.

## Non-examples

Changing from fixed camera to over-the-shoulder play does not by itself change what kind of entity the franchise is.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Not every broadening is ontological. The term should be reserved for changes in the *basis of continuity*, not ordinary innovation within a stable basis.

The concept should not assume that only the earliest formulation is authentic. Franchises can legitimately broaden; the analytical task is to name what changed.

## Operationalization / evidence

Compare what the franchise label predicts at different historical moments. Track marketing descriptors, repeated motifs, setting and ontology, audience expectations, creator rationales, and whether a work stripped of names and icons would still be classified as belonging to the same creative lineage.

## Relations

Ontological migration **changes** franchise ontology and identity conditions, can **enable** genre-label conversion, and is a specialized form of semantic drift.
