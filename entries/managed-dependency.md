# managed dependency

## Definition

**Managed dependency** is dependency that has been reorganized through formal systems rather than eliminated.

The dependent relationship may be distributed across:

- markets;
- bureaucracies;
- benefits systems;
- employers;
- banks;
- insurers;
- landlords;
- credential institutions;
- platforms.

The word **managed** matters because rules, classifications, eligibility, records, and administrative interfaces become part of the dependency itself.

## Problem pressure

The local liberalism analysis kept encountering a false story:

~~~text
personal dependence
      ↓ modernization
individual independence
~~~

A better description was often:

~~~text
personal / kin dependence
      ↓
institutional and market mediation
      ↓
managed dependency
~~~

This may still be progress—but only if the new dependencies are less arbitrary and more contestable.

## Core model

~~~text
necessity
   ↓
formal gatekeeper or market
   ↓
classification / contract / eligibility
   ↓
managed dependency
   ↓
access conditioned on institutional rules
~~~

## Claims and implications

Managed dependency can be safer than personal domination because rules may be explicit, distributed, appealable, and subject to law.

It can also become more total because many necessities depend on the same bureaucratic identity, market access, or platform infrastructure.

The normative comparison is therefore institutional, not rhetorical.

## Examples

### Welfare administration

Support no longer depends on a patron but on documented eligibility.

### Employment-linked life

Income, health insurance, housing eligibility, and credit all become indirectly dependent on formal employment.

### Platform dependency

A worker is formally independent but access to customers depends on one platform's rules.

## Non-examples

Ordinary interdependence is not necessarily managed dependency.

A friendship or reciprocal family relation without formalized gatekeeping is a different structure.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The phrase can sound pejorative and should not imply that unmanaged personal dependence is preferable.

The relevant comparison concerns arbitrariness, opacity, exit, concentration, rights, and error correction.

## Operationalization / evidence

Ask:

- which necessities depend on which institutions;
- how many alternative providers exist;
- whether rules are visible;
- whether denial can be appealed;
- whether multiple dependencies concentrate in one gatekeeper;
- what happens after identification or credential failure;
- whether benefits and rights are portable.

## Relations

Managed dependency generalizes **Shift dependency**, can be produced by **Institutional mediation**, requires **Institutional legibility** for large-scale administration, and should be constrained by **Anti-domination** through contestability, appeal, and exit.
