# media appetite

## Definition

**Media appetite** is the momentary or medium-term configuration of what kinds of media currently feel **available to desire**.

It is broader than stable taste. A person may have broad long-term preferences while, for a period, only certain combinations of genre, mood, pacing, visual texture, narrative structure, or emotional tone feel usable.

Media appetite is therefore a state variable rather than an identity claim.

## Problem pressure

The canonical **Genre lock** entry showed that “I want science fiction” was often too coarse. The actual desired package could include brooding mood, nocturnal environments, high-contrast lighting, technological saturation, bleakness, and small signals of hope.

That suggested a broader concept above genre lock: the changing profile that determines what representations are motivationally accessible at a given time.

## Core model

~~~text
current mood
+ fatigue / energy
+ recent exposure
+ novelty demand
+ intellectual preoccupation
+ sensory / aesthetic preference
        ↓
MEDIA APPETITE
        ↓
some works become easy to want
others become motivationally inaccessible
~~~

## Claims and implications

Media appetite can vary independently from quality judgments. A person can recognize that a work is excellent while being unable to want it right now.

It can also be more specific than conscious labels. “I need sci-fi” may be shorthand for a latent feature bundle that crosses formal genre boundaries.

The concept is intentionally descriptive. It does not imply that every temporary appetite should be indulged or optimized.

## Examples

### Clean example

A player with broad taste temporarily wants only dark, urban, technologically saturated games with brooding music and strong atmosphere.

### Cross-medium example

A reader who normally enjoys many genres temporarily wants only tightly plotted mysteries because unresolved epistemic tension is the active appetite.

### Borderline example

Someone simply prefers fantasy over science fiction across many years. That is stable taste unless the preference becomes temporarily restrictive in a state-dependent way.

## Non-examples

A permanent favorite genre is not by itself media appetite in the present sense.

A recommendation algorithm’s inferred user profile is also not the appetite itself; it is a model of it.

## Boundaries and failure modes

The phrase is intentionally broad and risks becoming a catch-all for preference. Its usefulness depends on preserving the temporary, motivational, and state-dependent aspects.

Media appetite should not be pathologized merely because it narrows. Narrowness can be useful and self-limiting.

## Operationalization / evidence

Possible indicators include rapid changes in what the person starts, unusually high abandonment of nonmatching media, repeated convergence on shared aesthetic features, and explicit descriptions such as “nothing else works right now.”

A useful probe is to compare stable liking with current startability: **what do I generally like, and what can I actually want today?**

## Relations

Media appetite contains the narrower problem of **Motivational admissibility**, shapes **Mood-congruent selection**, and may be represented by a **Latent feature bundle**. **Genre lock**, **Tone lock**, and **Aesthetic lock** are different forms of temporary appetite narrowing.

## Open questions

- How quickly can media appetite change?
- Which factors—mood, fatigue, novelty, recent exposure, or current intellectual concerns—have the largest effect?
- Can an appetite be represented as a feature vector without flattening its phenomenology?
