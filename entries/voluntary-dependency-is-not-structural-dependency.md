# Voluntary dependency is not structural dependency

## Definition

Choosing to live under constrained conditions is not equivalent to being unable to leave them.

Surface similarity of living conditions does not establish equivalence of power or vulnerability.

## Problem pressure

The thread needed to distinguish an ascetic or intellectual choosing dependence from a person whose material position gives them no credible alternative.

The broader thread was trying to preserve exceptional intellectual capacity without either converting it into jurisdiction over others or demanding that the person erase the ego that carries the capacity.

## Core model

~~~text
same visible constrained condition
      ├─ chosen + credible exit → voluntary dependency
      └─ no credible exit      → structural dependency
~~~

## Claims and implications

Dependency analysis should include counterfactual mobility and fallback options, not merely current consumption or employment status.

This entry belongs to the architecture of **psychic sovereignty, epistemic submission, and social non-domination**.

## Examples

### Clean example

Two workers live in the same dormitory; one can leave tomorrow using savings and credentials, while the other would lose housing, legal status, and income.

### Borderline example

A case in which similar outward behavior exists but the underlying motive, exit option, or constitutional status differs.

## Non-examples

A person calling a coercive dependency voluntary because they once consented before exit became impossible.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Voluntary dependencies can become structural over time when exit options decay.

## Operationalization / evidence

Track the concrete features named by the concept rather than inferring equivalence from appearances alone. For dependency claims, inspect credible exit and fallback options. For epistemic claims, inspect actual response to correction. For psychic-governance claims, distinguish internal desire from external imposition.
