# Beauty-diversity norm

## Definition

A **beauty-diversity norm** treats aesthetic concentration itself as a portfolio problem. The target is not merely demographic diversity but diversity in facial structure, body type, age, styling, conventional attractiveness, and relation to dominant beauty ideals.

## Problem pressure

The term separates two different claims: that no individual woman owes beauty, and that a portfolio containing many conventionally beautiful women is itself an imbalance that designers may deliberately correct.

The immediate context was analysis of character design, fanservice, beauty norms, and the possibility that institutions assign different legitimacy to different audience preferences.

## Core model

```text
portfolio concentrated around narrow beauty ideal
        ↓ identified as representational imbalance
broaden morphology / age / styling / attractiveness
        ↓
beauty-diversity norm
```

## Claims and implications

The norm can be implemented additively, by adding new types, or substitutively, by reducing the share of previously common beauty types. Those implementation choices have different audience-allocation consequences.

This entry names an analytical variable or mechanism. It does **not** establish by itself that a particular studio, industry, or demographic exhibits the pattern; application requires evidence about actual decisions, discourse, incentives, and outcomes.

## Examples

### Clean example

A character roster is intentionally diversified across age, body structure, facial morphology, styling, and attractiveness rather than clustering around one glamour ideal.

### Borderline example

A creator changes a character design for several overlapping reasons—art direction, realism, audience diversification, employee taste, and reputational concerns—making it difficult to isolate any one mechanism.

### Cross-domain example

Comparable allocation problems can occur in advertising, film, television, fashion, publishing, and other cultural industries where aesthetic choices serve multiple audiences and prestige systems.

## Non-examples

A naturally varied cast created without any norm that concentration is problematic may produce beauty diversity without instantiating a beauty-diversity norm.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Aesthetic diversity is difficult to measure without reifying a single beauty scale. Analysts should distinguish creators' stated categories from their own attractiveness judgments.

Avoid inferring motive from morphology alone. A design outcome can be compatible with several causal explanations, and similar-looking outcomes may arise from very different production processes.

## Operationalization / evidence

Useful evidence can include art-direction documents, interviews, model sheets, before/after designs, audience research, internal or public design rationales, portfolio comparisons, marketing segmentation, and systematic comparison across works. Distinguish direct evidence of motive from inference based on patterns.

## Relations

The norm can be **supported by** anti-prescriptive femininity and implemented through either additive diversity or substitutive diversity.
