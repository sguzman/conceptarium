# Moral coefficient of preference

## Definition

The **moral coefficient of preference** is a conceptual weighting factor representing how morally favored or disfavored an institution treats a preference before combining that preference with market size, artistic goals, cost, and other decision variables.

## Problem pressure

The term was needed to formalize the intuition that not all equally strong consumer desires enter decision-making with equal normative weight.

The immediate problem was how abstract ideology, prestige, corporate incentives, and audience judgments can become concrete design choices without requiring a single centralized command.

## Core model

```text
raw preference demand
      × moral coefficient
      × commercial / artistic / strategic factors
      ↓
effective institutional weight
```

## Claims and implications

The coefficient is not assumed to be numerical in practice. It is a way of asking whether some desires receive a positive moral premium while others carry a penalty.

The entry is a mechanism for inquiry, not a presumption of motive. A specific application should identify which stages of the mechanism are evidenced and which remain hypothetical.

## Examples

### Clean example

Two equally sized audiences request different forms of attractive character design, but one request is treated as representationally valuable while the other enters review with an objectification concern.

### Borderline example

A design decision fits the predicted mechanism but could also be explained by story needs, technology, individual artist taste, market research, or production constraints.

### Cross-domain example

Comparable governance and allocation mechanisms can operate in advertising, fashion, film, publishing, social platforms, and institutional communication.

## Non-examples

Different allocation caused solely by one audience being ten times larger does not require different moral coefficients.

## Boundaries and failure modes

This is a modeling metaphor, not evidence that institutions literally calculate coefficients. It can create false precision if treated quantitatively without data.

Avoid monocausal explanations. Corporate strategy, sincere moral beliefs, professional prestige, audience research, and personal aesthetics can align without being identical causes.

## Operationalization / evidence

Map the proposed causal chain stage by stage. Seek evidence for doctrine, status incentives, decision rules, allocation changes, and concrete output changes separately rather than inferring the whole pipeline from the final artifact.

## Relations
