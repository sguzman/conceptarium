# Unbounded revanchism

## Definition

**Unbounded revanchism** is the grievance-driven subtype of an unbounded claim.

A real or perceived injury becomes not merely a warrant for repair, but a **renewable source of special entitlement** whose permissible remedies can expand across time, targets, and domains.

The characteristic transformation is:

~~~text
injury / humiliation / dispossession / defeat
        ↓
legitimate grievance
        ↓
claim to redress
        ↓
redress does not discharge grievance
        ↓
claim expands or changes form
        ↓
past injury remains standing warrant
        ↺
~~~

The endpoint can be restitution, punishment, exclusion, status inversion, or jurisdiction over parties not originally responsible.

## Problem pressure

The user wanted a term for cases where political or social grievance seems unable to reach settlement.

The concern was not revenge as an emotion. Nor was it any demand for historical repair. It was the **absence of a stopping rule**: injury becomes constitutionalized as a permanent source of asymmetrical rights.

The concept became especially important because it mirrors Infinite license:

~~~text
achievement → standing title
injury      → standing claim
~~~

Both can transform a finite warrant into indefinitely renewable authority.

## Core model

Unbounded revanchism typically requires several separable mechanisms:

1. **closureless grievance** keeps the injury normatively open;
2. **grievance-to-jurisdiction conversion** turns victim status into authority;
3. **revanchist ratchet** makes successful redress enlarge rather than reduce the claim;
4. **settlement refusal** prevents closure;
5. **inherited revanchist warrant** can transmit the claim to successors;
6. **status-reversal entitlement** can redefine justice as inversion rather than non-domination.

## Claims and implications

The concept does not imply that historical grievances are false, that descendants cannot inherit legitimate legal claims, or that severe redress is inherently revanchist.

The defining question is whether the theory contains **principled satisfaction conditions** and whether achieving them reduces the exceptional claim.

The empirical use of the concept must be especially careful because parties commonly accuse each other of endless grievance. Evidence should come from explicit doctrines, institutional rules, repeated expansion after satisfaction, or observable refusal of previously stated closure conditions.

## Examples

### Clean example

A movement initially demands restitution of a specific wrong, receives the stated remedy, and then treats the successful remedy as proof that broader punitive and status-reversal claims are now morally authorized, without specifying any final settlement.

### Social example

A family conflict transmits a grievance across generations, and descendants are expected to preserve hostility and special claims against descendants of the opposing side regardless of their conduct.

### Borderline example

A group continues seeking redress because only part of an agreed settlement was delivered. Continued grievance is bounded if full compliance would end the special claim.

## Non-examples

Ordinary legal restitution, truth commissions, reparations, or transitional justice are not unbounded revanchism merely because they address old harms.

A claim can also remain open legitimately when harm is ongoing or when the responsible party has not performed the agreed remedy.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Revanchism is politically loaded language. The concept should therefore be applied structurally, not as a synonym for “aggrieved politics.”

Analysts should distinguish inherited memory, inherited legal rights, cultural mourning, and ongoing inequality from an inherited **warrant to dominate or punish**.

The concept should also not be used to force reconciliation where safety or justice has not been achieved.

## Operationalization / evidence

Look for:

- explicit satisfaction conditions;
- whether completed remedies reduce claims;
- whether targets expand beyond responsible parties;
- whether descendant status creates automatic liability or authority;
- whether punitive asymmetry is framed as justice in itself;
- whether institutions benefit from keeping the grievance unsettled;
- whether previously offered closure terms are repeatedly displaced after fulfillment.

## Relations

Unbounded revanchism specializes **Unbounded claim**. It is enabled by **Grievance-to-jurisdiction conversion**, intensified by **Revanchist ratchet** and **Inherited revanchist warrant**, and supported by **Settlement refusal**.

It is associated with **Moralized grievance** and may intersect the **Fallen humiliation hazard**, but neither is sufficient to produce revanchism.
