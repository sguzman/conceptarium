# Fallen purification through self-erasure

## Definition

The rejected strategy of solving the danger of Fallen capacity by suppressing the ego, renouncing distinction, or attempting to become psychologically equivalent to the Village.

The strategy can be sincere and morally serious while still failing as a general constitutional solution.

## Problem pressure

The thread needed a name for the strategy it was rejecting: if Fallen capacities are dangerous, perhaps the person should abolish the capacities’ psychological carrier.

The broader thread was testing whether Fallen danger is better solved by self-erasure or by a constitutional architecture that survives pride, status pressure, and human fallibility.

## Core model

~~~text
Fallen danger diagnosed
      ↓
solution = suppress ego / distinction / ambition
      ↓
attempted self-erasure
      ↓
possible internal despotism + retained formation
~~~

## Claims and implications

The alternative is to constitutionalize power and correction rather than demand permanent psychic purification.

This entry belongs to the larger architecture of **psychic sovereignty, epistemic submission, and social non-domination**.

## Examples

### Clean example

A system-builder concludes that the only safe ethical life is to suppress ambition and imitate an idealized Village psychology.

### Borderline example

A case that contains one element of the pattern—humiliation, correction, asceticism, ambition, or prestige—without the full mechanism required by the concept.

## Non-examples

Temporarily stepping back from prestige competition to recover judgment.

## Boundaries and failure modes

Some forms of renunciation may be healthy or spiritually meaningful. The failure-mode claim concerns using self-erasure as the general solution to Fallen hazard.

The concept should not be used to infer violence, domination, or pathology from pride, humiliation, or ambition alone.

## Operationalization / evidence

For humiliation and status mechanisms, trace mediating conditions rather than relying on a single cause. For constitutional principles, inspect whether checks remain effective when the actor is trusted, admired, angry, or wrong. For corrigibility, observe behavior under high-identity-cost correction rather than only trivial mistakes.
