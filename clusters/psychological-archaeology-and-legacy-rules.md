## Governing chain

~~~text
EXPERIENCE
    ↓
MENTAL NOTE
    ↓
INHERITED INTERNAL RULE
    ↓
possible STALE PROPOSITION
    ↓
PSYCHOLOGICAL ARCHAEOLOGY
    ↓
PROVENANCE OF BELIEF
    ↓
EPISTEMIC REVIEW
    ↓
REOPENING THE CASE
    ↓
ADULT AUDIT OF CHILDHOOD CONCLUSIONS
    ↓
LEGACY PSYCHOLOGICAL CODE becomes explicit
    ↓
PSYCHOLOGICAL REFACTORING
~~~

The governing idea is:

> **Old psychological rules deserve provenance and review, not automatic repeal and not automatic obedience.**

## Historical layering

The present self contains structures authored under earlier conditions.

Those structures can include:

- danger estimates;
- identity conclusions;
- prohibitions;
- dependency rules;
- social expectations;
- shame rules;
- interpretations of what must never happen again.

The fact that the original authoring context has faded does not make the rule disappear.

## Mental notes

[Mental note](../entries/mental-note.md) is the smallest unit in the model.

A complicated experience can be compressed into a portable conclusion:

~~~text
complex event
    ↓
"X is dangerous"
    ↓
future fast-path decision
~~~

Compression is useful.

It also discards context.

## Inherited internal rules

An [Inherited internal rule](../entries/inherited-internal-rule.md) is a mental conclusion that has become governing machinery.

The present self inherits authority produced by an earlier self.

The key question is not whether the rule is old.

It is whether changed circumstances have invalidated, narrowed, or preserved its justification.

## Stale propositions

A [Stale proposition](../entries/stale-proposition.md) is not necessarily a proposition that was always wrong.

Its defining problem is temporal:

~~~text
once-supported proposition
        +
changed conditions
        +
no review
        =
possible staleness
~~~

This prevents the framework from humiliating earlier selves for making locally rational decisions.

## Psychological archaeology

[Psychological archaeology](../entries/psychological-archaeology.md) traces present governance backward.

It asks:

- which self authored this?
- under what conditions?
- with what evidence?
- against what danger?
- with what available exits?
- what function did the rule serve?

The excavation is explanatory rather than automatically debunking.

## Provenance of belief

[Provenance of belief](../entries/provenance-of-belief.md) is the inward version of epistemic chain of custody.

~~~text
origin
    ≠
legitimacy

origin
    =
evidence relevant to review
~~~

This cluster therefore respects the standing Conceptarium distinction between developmental provenance and present legitimacy.

## Epistemic review

[Epistemic review](../entries/epistemic-review.md) turns archaeology into present governance.

It asks whether an inherited conclusion should be:

- retained;
- narrowed;
- revised;
- contextualized;
- retired;
- refactored.

The shift is:

~~~text
INHERITED AUTHORITY
        ↓ review
REVIEWED AUTHORITY
~~~

## Reopening the case

[Reopening the case](../entries/reopening-the-case.md) supplies the judicial metaphor.

Old psychological verdicts can acquire precedent-like force.

The right to reopen a case preserves temporal contestability without implying that every old verdict should be overturned.

## Adult audit of childhood conclusions

[Adult audit of childhood conclusions](../entries/adult-audit-of-childhood-conclusions.md) specializes review to developmental asymmetry.

A child and an adult can inhabit radically different capability structures.

A rule that was rational under childhood dependency may become overgeneralized once the adult has:

- exit;
- income;
- vocabulary;
- physical capacity;
- social alternatives;
- institutional recourse.

The audit asks whether the old rule still maps the present.

## Legacy psychological code

[Legacy psychological code](../entries/legacy-psychological-code.md) captures the persistence mechanism using a software metaphor.

Old rules can keep "executing" even when the environment changed.

The metaphor is useful for:

- persistence;
- automatic triggering;
- branching;
- hidden assumptions;
- dependency between rules;
- revision.

It must not be mistaken for a literal computational theory of mind.

## Psychological refactoring

[Psychological refactoring](../entries/psychological-refactoring.md) is the preferred repair operation when an old rule contains both signal and obsolete implementation.

~~~text
OLD RULE
"Never depend on anyone."

VALID SIGNAL
"Dependency without exit creates domination risk."

REFACTORED RULE
"Accept interdependence when reciprocity, exit,
and retained agency remain available."
~~~

Refactoring preserves hard-won information without granting old implementation permanent sovereignty.

## Relation to historical inward instrumentation

[Historical Inward Instrumentation](./historical-inward-instrumentation.md) studies cultural techniques for self-examination.

The present cluster applies historical reasoning inside one life.

~~~text
intellectual history
    ↔

~~~

The analogy is methodological, not literal.

## Anti-cruelty toward earlier selves

A central constraint is:

> **Do not use greater present knowledge to humiliate a past self for lacking information, power, or vocabulary it did not possess.**

Earlier conclusions should be judged relative to their evidentiary environment before being judged for present applicability.

This preserves the Batch 16 anti-cruelty constitution across time.

## Core anti-collapse rules

- old ≠ false;
- childhood ≠ irrational;
- provenance ≠ legitimacy;
- explanation ≠ exoneration;
- explanation ≠ condemnation;
- review ≠ repeal;
- persistence ≠ conscious endorsement;
- software metaphor ≠ literal computational ontology;
- refactoring ≠ deletion;
- present superiority ≠ license to humiliate earlier selves.
