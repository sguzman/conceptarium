# Attention, Absorption, and Completion

This cluster develops the machinery already implicit in two canonical entries:

- [Genre lock](../entries/genre-lock.md)
- [Closure pressure](../entries/closure-pressure.md)

The first concerns **what becomes available to desire**.

The second concerns **what remains mentally open after partial engagement**.

The promoted concepts connect those two sides into one attention-and-consumption model.

## I. Media appetite

[media appetite](../entries/media-appetite.md) is the broad state variable.

~~~text
mood
+ fatigue
+ recent exposure
+ novelty demand
+ intellectual preoccupation
+ sensory / aesthetic preference
        ↓
MEDIA APPETITE
~~~

Media appetite is not stable taste. A person can have broad long-term interests while only a narrow region of the media space is presently usable.

The core question is:

> **What can I actually want right now?**

## II. Motivational admissibility

[motivational admissibility](../entries/motivational-admissibility.md) names whether an option crosses the threshold from evaluative approval into actionable desire.

~~~text
"I know this is good"
        ≠
"I can actually start and sustain it"
~~~

That distinction explains why objectively suitable recommendations can feel unusable during a strong lock.

Genre lock, tone lock, and aesthetic lock are therefore different forms of **admissibility narrowing**.

## III. Latent feature bundle

The conscious category may be an approximation.

[latent feature bundle](../entries/latent-feature-bundle.md) names the deeper package of properties actually carrying reward.

For the original science-fiction case:

~~~text
"science fiction"
      ↓ may approximate
brooding
+ technological
+ nocturnal
+ urban
+ high contrast
+ dark story
+ small hope signals
~~~

This yields an important anti-collapse rule:

~~~text
taxonomy label
      ≠
causal appetite bundle
~~~

A nominal genre member can fail while a cross-genre object succeeds because the feature bundle, not the label, is doing the motivational work.

## IV. Lock families

[Genre lock](../entries/genre-lock.md) narrows appetite around a genre family.

[tone lock](../entries/tone-lock.md) narrows around emotional atmosphere or affective texture.

[aesthetic lock](../entries/aesthetic-lock.md) narrows around visual, sonic, formal, or stylistic organization.

These can overlap:

~~~text
genre lock
    +
tone lock
    +
aesthetic lock
        ↓
very narrow admissible media set
~~~

But they are not identical.

A tone lock can cross genres. An aesthetic lock can cross tones. A genre lock can contain several distinct tones and styles.

## V. Mood-congruent selection

[mood-congruent selection](../entries/mood-congruent-selection.md) provides one route from current affect into choice.

~~~text
current mood / desired mood regulation
        ↓
affective fit of candidate
        ↓
motivational admissibility shifts
        ↓
selection
~~~

The cluster does not reduce appetite to mood. Fatigue, novelty, current intellectual concerns, recent exposure, habit, and sensory appetite can all matter.

Mood congruence is one mechanism among several.

## VI. Open objects

The second branch begins after engagement.

[open object](../entries/open-object.md) names a valued bounded thing whose incomplete relation remains cognitively live.

~~~text
partial engagement
      ↓
represented remainder
      ↓
object remains mentally open
~~~

The distinction is:

~~~text
unfinished object
      ≠
open object
~~~

Thousands of things can remain unfinished without making any claim on attention.

An object becomes open when the person still represents it as **something in progress**.

## VII. Absorption remainder

[absorption remainder](../entries/absorption-remainder.md) is what still feels unabsorbed from the person’s point of view.

It may include:

- unseen story;
- unexplored space;
- unresolved interpretation;
- unlearned mechanics;
- unfinished mastery;
- untested implications.

The canonical Closure pressure formulation was:

> **There is still more of the object to absorb.**

So:

~~~text
technical remainder
      ≠
absorption remainder
~~~

A person can reach credits while still feeling a large experiential remainder.

A person can stop halfway and feel no meaningful remainder because they extracted everything they wanted.

## VIII. Closure pressure

[Closure pressure](../entries/closure-pressure.md) occurs when an open object with residual value and a salient absorption remainder begins exerting recurring pressure on attention.

~~~text
open object
   +
absorption remainder
   +
residual value
        ↓
CLOSURE PRESSURE
~~~

This is not completionism.

The pressure is object-specific and absorptive:

> **That thing is still open. There is still more of it to take in.**

## IX. Reactivation through appetite

This is the bridge between the two major branches.

~~~text
dormant open object
        +
current genre / tone / aesthetic lock
        ↓
object becomes motivationally admissible again
        ↓
remainder becomes salient
        ↓
closure pressure reactivates
~~~

This explains why an unfinished game can exert almost no pull for months and then suddenly become dominant when current appetite matches it.

The unfinished object did not necessarily gain value.

The **consumer state changed**.

## X. Deliberate closure

[deliberate closure](../entries/deliberate-closure.md) is the explicit alternative to compulsory finishing.

Its canonical formulation is:

> **“I have extracted what I want from this and I am done.”**

The remaining content can continue to exist while losing its claim on attention.

~~~text
technical incompletion
        +
deliberate closure
        ↓
psychologically closed relation
~~~

This protects the entire framework from becoming a disguised completionist ethic.

Closure can occur through finishing.

Closure can also occur through consciously relinquishing the remainder.

## XI. Whole-cluster model

~~~text
CURRENT PERSON-STATE
mood / fatigue / novelty / concerns / sensory appetite
        ↓
MEDIA APPETITE
        ↓
LATENT FEATURE BUNDLE
        ↓
MOTIVATIONAL ADMISSIBILITY
        ↓
genre / tone / aesthetic locks
        ↓
MEDIA SELECTION
        ↓
partial engagement
        ↓
OPEN OBJECT
        ↓
ABSORPTION REMAINDER
        ↓
CLOSURE PRESSURE
        ↓
finish / return / deliberate closure
        ↓
closed or still-open relation
~~~

This model is deliberately cyclical rather than linear.

A later appetite state can reactivate an old open object.

An open object can also distort the current choice set by becoming more salient than equally appetitive new objects.

## Anti-collapse rules

~~~text
stable taste ≠ media appetite
liking ≠ motivational admissibility
genre label ≠ latent feature bundle

genre lock ≠ tone lock
tone lock ≠ aesthetic lock
lock ≠ permanent identity

unfinished object ≠ open object
technical remainder ≠ absorption remainder
closure pressure ≠ completionism
closure pressure ≠ backlog guilt
closure pressure ≠ FOMO

finishing ≠ psychological closure
abandonment ≠ failure to close
deliberate closure ≠ passive forgetting
~~~

## Research frontier

Future work should compare this local vocabulary against:

- mood-congruent media selection;
- state-dependent preference;
- selective attention;
- motivational salience;
- active-goal and unfinished-task effects;
- Zeigarnik-like phenomena;
- need for closure;
- goal disengagement;
- aesthetic satiation;
- interest dynamics;
- recommendation systems that infer latent feature bundles rather than coarse genre labels.

External literature should test overlap and novelty without erasing the phenomenological distinctions that generated the local concepts.
