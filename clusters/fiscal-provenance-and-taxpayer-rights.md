# Fiscal Provenance and Taxpayer Rights

This cluster studies what happens **after a compulsory tax transfer is accepted as lawful**.

Its central question is not “is taxation theft?” but:

> **Which contributor rights, if any, survive collection—and why should collection itself extinguish them?**

The cluster originated in the 2026-08-22 discussion of tax provenance, general funds, fungibility, taxpayer-directed allocation, and the discontinuity between precise pre-collection individuation and post-collection “public revenue.”

The constitutional intuition is:

~~~text
the less voluntary the transfer,
the stronger the recipient's accountability burden should be
~~~

rather than:

~~~text
compulsion
   ↓
fewer obligations to the person compelled
~~~

## Foundational principle

The central entry is [Fiscal non-extinguishment](../entries/fiscal-non-extinguishment.md).

~~~text
lawful compulsory collection
        ≠
automatic extinction of every residual contributor right
~~~

It does not deny taxation, general funds, redistribution, or legislative budgeting. It blocks the narrower inference that collection itself terminates every contributor-specific claim.

Its broader normative ancestor is [Compulsion-generated rights](../entries/compulsion-generated-rights.md), which connects the fiscal theory to [Anti-domination](../entries/anti-domination.md) and [Contestability](../entries/contestability.md).

## The rights bundle

[Residual fiscal rights](../entries/residual-fiscal-rights.md) organizes the proposed surviving bundle:

1. **Provenance** — where did the value go?
2. **Attribution** — what did this contribution finance?
3. **Allocation** — which lawful public uses receive the contributor's discretionary share?
4. **Refusal** — which destinations can be excluded where law permits?
5. **Audit** — can the transformations be independently verified?
6. **Standing** — can the contributor personally enforce the right?

Not every political system must recognize all six at equal strength.

~~~text
fiscal provenance ≠ full taxpayer veto
residual rights ≠ continuing full private ownership
taxpayer sovereignty ≠ general political superiority by wealth
~~~

## Provenance architecture

[Fiscal provenance](../entries/fiscal-provenance.md) preserves the contributor-to-expenditure chain.

The accounting distinction [Physical pooling / legal individuation](../entries/physical-pooling-legal-individuation.md) says:

~~~text
money can be economically fungible
        while
claims remain legally individuated
~~~

That supports [Treasury fungibility / taxpayer sovereignty](../entries/treasury-fungibility-taxpayer-sovereignty.md): pooled treasury liquidity can coexist with persistent contributor rights.

A [Fiscal allocation graph](../entries/fiscal-allocation-graph.md) makes provenance executable:

~~~text
taxpayer
  ↓
tax event
  ↓
treasury pool
  ↓
appropriation
  ↓
agency / program
  ↓
contract / vendor
  ↓
final expenditure
~~~

The graph propagates fractional attribution rather than pretending particular banknotes travel intact. It supports a [Continuing fiscal claim](../entries/continuing-fiscal-claim.md), in which the taxpayer remains represented throughout the fiscal transaction.

## The extinction branch

[Claim extinction](../entries/claim-extinction.md) names the narrower mechanism:

~~~text
individual contribution
      ↓ collection / pooling
public revenue
      ↓
individual claim treated as gone
~~~

[Fiscal rights extinction](../entries/fiscal-rights-extinction.md) names the broader institutional failure mode in which collection becomes the boundary after which provenance, attribution, allocation, audit, refusal, or standing are treated as absent.

A vivid symptom is [Debtor–creditor asymmetry](../entries/debtor-creditor-asymmetry.md):

~~~text
BEFORE COLLECTION
state can individuate exactly what you owe

AFTER COLLECTION
contributor-specific accounting becomes unreasonable
~~~

The source phrase was: **You're visible as a debtor. You're invisible as a creditor.**

“Creditor” here is analytical analogy, not ordinary debt-law classification.

## Enforcement

[Fiscal standing](../entries/fiscal-standing.md) gives the contributor a direct contestability pathway:

~~~text
recognized right
    ↓
traceable attributable contribution
    ↓
violation
    ↓
personal injury
    ↓
review / remedy
~~~

The motivating case was deliberately tiny: if only a few cents are illegally routed in violation of a contributor-specific right, small magnitude should not erase the right.

## The commitment problem

[Fiscal commitment problem](../entries/fiscal-commitment-problem.md) adds time:

~~~text
narrow tax
  ↓
permanence
  ↓
generalization
  ↓
provenance loss
  ↓
broader discretion
~~~

The resulting hypothesis is counterintuitive: **strong residual taxpayer rights might make compulsory taxation more politically sustainable, not less**, because they make future bounds more credible. That remains an empirical question.

## Taxpayer sovereignty

[Taxpayer sovereignty](../entries/taxpayer-sovereignty.md) is the strongest allocation-side expression.

~~~text
authority over one's own contributed fiscal stream
        ≠
authority over other citizens
        ≠
greater human worth
        ≠
general political franchise weighted by wealth
~~~

A design could retain one-person-one-vote for general political authority while granting contributor-linked rights over bounded fiscal allocations.

Serious hazards remain: unequal budget influence, self-dealing, free-riding, underfunding universal goods, geographic inequality, strategic refusal, and administrative complexity.

## Anti-collapse rules

~~~text
taxation ≠ fiscal rights extinction
general fund ≠ claim extinction
fungibility ≠ loss of legal individuation
provenance ≠ allocation
allocation ≠ general political sovereignty
residual rights ≠ full retained ownership
taxpayer sovereignty ≠ plutocracy by definition
technical traceability ≠ normative legitimacy
small injury ≠ no injury
administrative convenience ≠ constitutional justification
~~~

## Research frontier

Future deep research should investigate:

- current tax-law treatment of taxpayer standing and contributor-specific claims;
- Wicksell and Lindahl on tax–expenditure linkage;
- Buchanan on earmarking, fiscal choice, and fiscal illusion;
- Puviani and fiscal illusion;
- historical general-fund development;
- taxpayer-receipt systems;
- tax-choice / participatory-taxation implementations;
- treasury data architectures;
- attribution under deficit financing and sovereign debt;
- privacy-preserving fiscal provenance;
- whether stronger commitment devices alter willingness to accept taxation.

External literature should locate neighbors and test novelty, not retroactively overwrite the recovered vocabulary.
