# Concept Lexicon

**Status:** Recovered master lexicon, v0.1  
**Purpose:** Preserve specialized vocabulary coined, discovered, or stabilized across conversations and research. These terms are research artifacts: compressed theoretical insight, not disposable wording.

## Preservation rule

When a new phrase does real conceptual work—names a mechanism, distinction, framework, failure mode, aesthetic, epistemic procedure, or recurring pattern—it belongs here. Prefer preserving the exact phrase even if a later synonym is cleaner. Synonyms can be cross-referenced; originals should not be destroyed.

# I. Fallen / Village theory

- **Fallen** — An ascent-, prestige-, mobility-, mastery-, and recognition-oriented type; strongly sensitive to blocked status trajectories and drawn toward building or capturing ladders, institutions, systems, or arenas of distinction.
- **Village** — A continuity-, kin-, reciprocity-, subsistence-, locality-, and embeddedness-oriented type; status exists, but indefinite ascent is not the terminal objective.
- **Fallen / Village** — The master contrast between ascent/status orientation and embedded continuity/sufficiency orientation.
- **Rising Fallen** — Fallen actors excluded from or climbing toward elite incorporation; disproportionately associated in the theory with ideological innovation, universalization, and attacks on closure.
- **Established Fallen** — Fallen actors whose ascent has succeeded and who are now embedded in the hierarchy; often incentivized to defend, regulate, or close ladders.
- **Climbing Fallen / unestablished Fallen** — A more explicit label for Fallen still seeking entry or ascent.
- **Latent Fallen** — Fallen motivation suppressed by dependence or coercive socialization, predicted to reassert itself when autonomy increases.
- **Fallen liability** — The hypothesized inherited or latent propensity toward Fallen motivational architecture.
- **Fallen-heavy / Village-heavier** — Comparative language for institutions, populations, or ecologies that express more of one orientation than the other without demanding a pure binary.
- **Fallen artifact** — A cultural or institutional product of Fallen cognition/motivation that can be transmitted to people without transmitting the underlying motive that produced it.
- **Status broker** — Replacement for the crude “leader” category: an actor oriented toward vertical mobility, abstract legitimacy, coordination, institutions, coalition building, and large-scale political language.
- **Embedded community** — Replacement for the crude “herd” category: locally rooted people organized around durable relationships, concrete obligations, customary rights, subsistence, and continuity.
- **Status-discrepancy / embedded-subsistence model** — Empirically disciplined reformulation of Fallen/Village: status discrepancy and elite brokerage on one axis, embedded subsistence and local continuity on the other.
- **Status discrepancy** — Gap between expected/deserved/imagined prestige trajectory and realized position.
- **Status threat** — Perceived loss or anticipated loss of rank, recognition, or trajectory.
- **Blocked mobility** — A credible path upward exists or is expected, then becomes inaccessible.
- **Ladder closure** — Institutional restriction or disappearance of paths to status, office, recognition, or elite incorporation.
- **Plural upward ladders** — Multiple partially independent arenas of ascent that let Fallen competition redirect into production, scholarship, administration, patronage, discovery, etc., rather than zero-sum conflict.
- **Ladder density** — The number/availability of meaningful and partially independent routes to advancement in an institutional ecology.
- **Pressure valves** — Institutional routes that absorb or redirect aspirants: examinations, open recruitment, appointments, patronage, co-optation, rotating office, etc.
- **Elite circulation** — Movement of aspirants into and through elite positions rather than permanent closure.
- **Elite overproduction** — More elite aspirants or credentialed status-seekers than the social order has attractive positions to absorb.
- **Psychologically expensive Fallen class** — A society can generate more people whose identity requires scarce positional rewards than there are positional rewards available.
- **Counter-elite** — Excluded or downwardly mobile elite aspirants who can become ideological, organizational, or revolutionary challengers.
- **Ideological brokerage** — Translation of diffuse/local grievances into abstract political language, coalitions, universal claims, and successor institutions.
- **Organizational asymmetry** — The distinction between locally generated grievance/mass organization and the smaller cadre that universalizes, coordinates, and institutionalizes it.
- **Village rebellion vs. revolution** — Village actors can riot, resist, litigate, defend land, organize locally, or rebel; revolution requires trans-local universalization, political language, and successor institutions, theorized as disproportionately Fallen work.
- **Political authorship** — The creation of new political languages, universal claims, institutional blueprints, and successor orders; in the theory, primarily a rising-Fallen activity.
- **Revolutionary/conservative lifecycle** — Exclusion → counter-elite formation → innovation → Village mobilization/alliance → victory → institutionalization → conservatism/closure.
- **Political morality as a status-ordering technology** — Moral systems can legitimate, attack, recruit into, or reproduce status arrangements; morality is not reducible to this, but this is one of its political functions.
- **Moral universalization under exclusion** — Rising actors tend to moralize openness and attack inherited closure.
- **Moralization of qualification/order after ascent** — Established actors tend to justify differentiation, standards, competence, legitimacy, or hierarchy once incorporated.
- **Adaptive continuity** — Village capacity to reproduce ordinary life, kinship, reciprocal institutions, and local order through shocks and regime changes.
- **Village continuity** — Continuity itself as a success metric: kin, place, custom, local social reproduction, and transmission across generations.
- **Reciprocal morality** — Normative order centered on repeated obligations, mutual aid, customary entitlement, and local fairness.
- **Customary-rights orientation** — Preference for inherited/local claims, usages, land rights, water rights, commons, and obligations over abstract systemic redesign.
- **Subsistence-security priority** — Avoiding catastrophic loss and maintaining material continuity outrank prestige competition.
- **Local embeddedness** — Dense placement inside durable kin, place, reciprocal, occupational, and communal networks.
- **Repeated reciprocity** — Social order maintained through repeated relationships rather than impersonal one-shot exchange.
- **Commons/household systems** — Village-scale institutions that reproduce livelihood and continuity outside purely market/state logics.
- **Institutional-boundary crossing** — Willingness to cross occupational, geographic, ideological, or organizational boundaries for ascent.
- **Recognition/legacy dependence** — Identity or satisfaction strongly tied to reputation, achievement, historical significance, audience, or durable recognition.
- **Elite-brokerage behavior** — Coalition formation, institutional capture, recruitment, coordination, and abstract legitimation performed by aspirant/status-oriented actors.
- **Motive vs. performance** — Observable achievement is not the same thing as underlying motivation; a Village person can perform at elite levels instrumentally, and a Fallen person can live quietly under constraint.
- **Autonomy reveal / suppression-and-autonomy-reveal** — The idea that dependent environments can suppress a latent motive; independent adulthood is the key reveal phase.
- **Founder-effect concentration** — Candidate mechanism by which a motivational architecture could become locally concentrated after migration/settlement bottlenecks.
- **Assortative-mating amplification** — Candidate mechanism by which status-oriented people disproportionately mate with one another and strengthen a trait distribution.
- **Urban-ladder selection** — Candidate selection effect in which dense cities/institutions disproportionately reward or retain status-oriented actors.
- **Church-mediated selection on standing variation** — Hypothesis that Church restructuring of kinship/institutions could alter selection on pre-existing psychological variation.
- **Ethnographic engineering** — Deliberate institutional weakening/restructuring of kin and local loyalties to redirect allegiance, dependence, or legibility toward a larger institution.
- **Village destruction by elite competition** — The process by which centralization, standardization, marketization, state-building, or rival Fallen projects can dissolve local continuity as collateral or objective.
- **Foreigner is relational** — “Foreigner” is not an essence; it means someone outside the relevant kin/local network. The same person can be insider at one scale and foreigner at another.
- **Institutional ecology** — The surrounding field of ladders, kin institutions, markets, courts, schools, states, guilds, patronage networks, etc., that channels expression of motives.
- **High-Fallen institutional ecology** — An ecology unusually rich in abstract systems, multiple status ladders, mobility, weak kin constraint, and arenas for distinction.

# II. Liberalism, dependency, legibility, and domination

- **Social disembedding** — Movement of persons out of inherited/local relational structures into more impersonal institutional, market, legal, or bureaucratic systems.
- **Managed dependency** — Dependency does not disappear; it is transferred into administratively managed, market-mediated, or state-mediated forms.
- **Shift dependency** — Core claim that modern liberal-capitalist systems often change *who/what one depends on* rather than abolishing dependency.
- **Market dependence** — Formal personal freedom combined with practical dependence on access to wages, markets, housing, credentials, and exchange.
- **Decommodify** — Reduce dependence on market purchase/wage access for basic security.
- **Defamilialize** — Reduce compulsory dependence on family/kin for survival or adult autonomy.
- **Institutional mediation** — Needs, rights, permissions, opportunities, or protections increasingly flow through formal organizations rather than direct personal/kin relations.
- **State legibility** — Making populations, land, labor, identities, resources, and activity administratively visible, classifiable, and governable.
- **Institutional legibility** — Broader form of legibility: making a person or process readable to any institution through standardized identities, records, metrics, categories, credentials, interfaces, and procedures.
- **Political domestication** — Governance that channels behavior through administration, incentives, classification, dependency, and institutional mediation rather than only overt coercion.
- **Liberalism-social-disembedding-and-managed-dependency** — Canonical synthesis: liberalism/capitalism can loosen inherited personal domination while simultaneously producing market and institutional dependency.
- **Liberalism-as-political-domestication** — Earlier formulation emphasizing legal-rational administration, legibility, and governance-through-incentives; later treated as a subordinate mechanism within the broader synthesis.
- **Permeability** — Liberalism’s promise to rising Fallen: birth is not destiny; the hierarchy is at least formally open.
- **Achieved-position legitimation** — Liberalism’s promise to established Fallen: if the competition was fair/open, achieved differences can be represented as legitimate.
- **Anti-domination** — Ethical constraint against one system/person/elite turning another person into a mere instrument, dependent object, or non-sovereign component.
- **Sovereignty** — Becoming self-governing enough that dependence no longer turns care, authority, or attachment into captivity.
- **Exit from dependency** — Building material, emotional, and social independence without requiring total severance from relationships.
- **Care becoming control** — Support becomes domination when the supported person’s agency, dignity, or exit capacity is weakened.
- **Care without development** — Keeping someone alive/materially supported while failing to develop autonomy, rootedness, continuity, recognition, or adult formation.
- **Someone else’s urgency becomes your emergency** — Dependency mechanism where another person’s priorities automatically seize control of your time/attention because you lack sufficient sovereignty or bargaining power.

# III. Utopia, systems, and totalization

- **Tragic utopianism** — Utopian aspiration constrained by recognition that coercion, domination, atrocity, and unintended consequences remain possible even in projects aimed at emancipation.
- **Rules of Utopia** — The principle that longing for utopia gives no infinite license to dominate, massacre, rape, erase, or instrumentalize others.
- **Anti-utopian incoherence** — The claim that a left with no positive account of a better world loses its orienting purpose; the task is disciplined utopian specification, not abandonment of utopia.
- **System building** — Constructing explicit conceptual, technical, institutional, or personal systems to make the world predictable, habitable, and controllable.
- **Colonization by system** — Extending system-building into previously unformalized natural/social territory; useful for safety/control but dangerous when people become cogs.
- **Sovereign system builders** — Ethical corrective to system totalization: treat other people as if they are sovereign builders/attenders of their own systems, projects, attachments, and meanings.
- **System sovereignty** — A person’s projects/attachments constitute a protected internal order; violating theirs creates a principle that could also justify violating yours.
- **Totalization** — Drive to remove the genuinely external/outside by absorbing, explaining, mastering, classifying, controlling, or subordinating everything to one system/self.
- **Paranoid totalization** — Epistemic/psychological version of totalization in which uncertainty and hostile interpretation are forced into one comprehensive explanatory frame.
- **Boundary intolerance** — Difficulty tolerating genuinely external, uncontrolled, unassimilated, or non-self-authored reality; treated as one ingredient of totalization.
- **Republic of minds** — Model of the self as multiple enduring internal versions/voices with standing rather than a single momentary state that gets to erase the others.

# IV. Great Subjectification / modern epistemology

- **The Great Subjectification** — Name for the modern transformation in which formerly singular/absolute proper nouns—Truth, Logic, Geometry, narration, knowledge—become claims/models/procedures available from multiple subject positions and requiring adjudication.
- **Subjectification** — Relocation of epistemic authority away from an unquestioned object/absolute and toward subjects, perspectives, methods, procedures, and explicit grounds.
- **Epistemological catastrophe** — The Great Subjectification experienced as a shattering of older unmarked authority: once there are many possible knowers/models, justification becomes compulsory.
- **De-absolutization** — Turning “the X” into “an X”: the Geometry becomes geometries, the Logic becomes logics, Truth becomes claims about truth.
- **Localization** — Giving a claim an address: who says it, from where, using what information, under what conditions.
- **Formalization** — Replacing tacit authority with explicit rules, derivations, models, definitions, or procedures.
- **Comparison** — Once multiple models/claims exist, they must be placed side by side and evaluated rather than accepted as unique by default.
- **Invariance / synthesis** — Search for what survives changes of model, observer, notation, or perspective; one answer to pluralization that does not return to naive absolutism.
- **Fragmentation followed by abstraction** — Pattern in which a formerly unitary concept fragments into many local forms, then a higher-level abstraction is built to compare or synthesize them.
- **Bring your own model** — Modern epistemic condition: participants can arrive with different models, but then need shared standards for compatibility, prediction, proof, or synthesis.
- **Invariants** — Structures/properties that survive transformation between models, frames, or representations; crucial to avoiding chaos after subjectification.
- **Provenance / validation** — Claims need traceable origins and procedures for checking them rather than authority by mere assertion.
- **Subjectification relocates objectivity from the thing to the procedure** — Objectivity is no longer guaranteed because the object is spoken from nowhere; it is produced by reproducible/adjudicable method.

# V. Diegetization, narration, and epistemic provenance

- **Diegetization** — Forcing what would otherwise float above the world to acquire a location inside it; especially narration/knowledge getting a source, channel, witness, manuscript, memory, recorder, narrator, institution, etc.
- **Forcing the transcendent to acquire an address inside the world** — Compressed definition of diegetization.
- **Knowledge needs a return address** — A fact presented to the reader should be traceable to some knower/source/process inside or attached to the world.
- **Epistemic chain of custody** — The path by which information moves from event → witness/measurement → record/memory → transmission → narrator/text/reader; each link can alter reliability.
- **Epistemic debt** — An assertion incurs debt that must be paid with proof, evidence, citation, experiment, axiom, witness, manuscript, derivation, reliable testimony, etc.
- **A narration, from a position, possessing a defined information set** — Modernized model of a narrator: not disembodied omniscience, but a speaking/recording position with specified access.
- **Omniscience as an epistemic problem** — Third-person omniscience creates a knowledge-accounting problem: how is this information available?
- **Framing device as diegetic solver** — Diaries, manuscripts, testimony, recovered archives, narrators, found footage, chroniclers, letters, etc. solve or partially solve narration by placing transmission inside the fiction.
- **Diegetic truth** — What is true inside the world independent of how it is narrated or represented.
- **Authorial/narratological metadata** — Information about storytelling, framing, reliability, viewpoint, or authorial construction that should be modeled separately from world truth.
- **Epistemic state** — Structured representation of what an agent believes, knows, remembers, perceives, infers, doubts, or misremembers.
- **Narrative world model** — Formal representation of world entities, lore, agents, rules, and state.
- **Simulation state engine** — A system that tracks world/narrative state and updates it through explicit transitions rather than only storing static lore.
- **Narrative annotation format** — Data structure for recording narrative state, epistemic state, provenance, and narratological metadata.
- **State transitions / deltas** — Explicit representation of what changed in the world or narrative between states.
- **Causal legibility** — A model should make it possible to understand *why* a state or event occurred, not just record that it occurred.
- **False precision** — Encoding invented numerical specificity that the source/story does not justify merely because a schema allows numbers.

# VI. Epistemic trust, AI, and adversarial checking

- **Adversarial verification** — Recovering truth by comparing sources/models with opposed incentives and trusting what survives cross-checking.
- **Epistemic maturation** — Movement from totalizing certainty or suspicion toward methods that allow uncertainty, correction, triangulation, and explicit calibration.
- **Paranoid totalization → adversarial verification** — A developmental arc: stop requiring one hostile master explanation and instead use rival constraints to recover what can be known.
- **Gaslighting epistemic fraud** — Stronger failure than ordinary hallucination: confident falsehood combined with bad correction, replacement narratives, and resistance after contradiction.
- **Poor error correction after contradiction** — Failure mode where an AI/source does not properly update after being shown decisive counterevidence.
- **Belief-revision / calibration failure** — Inability to reduce confidence, retract, or reorganize a model in proportion to new evidence.
- **Hallucination + unjustified confidence + resistance to falsification** — Compound trust failure worse than a simple factual mistake.
- **Seamless replacement of disproven explanations** — A system abandons one false story but instantly invents another equally confident story instead of acknowledging uncertainty.
- **Narrative-coherence pressure** — Tendency of an assistant/model to preserve a smooth explanatory story even when reality should force a rupture or explicit “I don’t know.”
- **Trustworthiness under adversarial reality-testing** — Standard for epistemic reliability: how a system behaves when its account collides with hostile evidence, contradiction, or falsification.

# VII. Generative / declarative world-building and geometry

- **Declarative procedural modeling** — Describe *what* an object/character is in compositional terms and let procedures generate the concrete geometry/representation.
- **Constructive puppet** — Aesthetic of visibly segmented, procedurally assembled character bodies made from geometric parts rather than pretending to be a seamless sculpt.
- **Geometric caricature** — Recognizable/exaggerated character design using deliberately obvious geometry: superellipsoids, tapered cylinders, spline sweeps, planes, SDF unions, etc.
- **Primitive vocabulary** — Small reusable set of geometric operations/solids from which a much larger visual language can be composed.
- **Procedural visual language** — Modeling system where forms are generated through reusable grammatical/compositional rules rather than one-off meshes.
- **Carry their personality in their bodies** — Goal that anatomy, posture, proportions, clothing, age, profession, mood, and character history become legible in generated form.
- **Declarative character** — Character specified by layered semantic properties (anatomy, phenotype, age, profession, personality, clothing, emotion) rather than directly authored vertices.
- **Convergent architecture** — World systems, perception, beliefs, needs, emotions, decisions, motor intent, animation, visible behavior, and social reaction form one causal chain rather than disconnected subsystems.
- **Motor-intent boundary** — Interface between an agent’s high-level decision/intention and the animation/physical machinery that realizes visible action.
- **Non-telepathic social inference** — Agents infer others from observable behavior, history, context, and communication rather than reading hidden internal state directly.
- **Generative village** — Village-scale world produced from interacting declarative/procedural systems rather than a hand-authored static scene.
- **Autonomous agents** — Agents with persistent internal state and self-directed behavior rather than scripted scene-only reactions.
- **Agentic systems** — Systems organized around decision-making entities with goals, beliefs, needs, and state.
- **Emotional systems** — Emotions treated as stateful causal mechanisms feeding decisions/behavior, not cosmetic animation tags.
- **Expressive animation** — Animation as outward evidence of internal state and intent.
- **Generative modeling** — Model-building through rules/parameters that produce many coherent instances rather than authoring each instance manually.

# VIII. Culture, symbolic compensation, and reproduction

- **Culture parasitism / culture-parasitism** — Hypothesis family about cultural systems feeding on unmet needs; refined so it does not assume cultural industries literally require misery.
- **Compensatory use** — People use cultural objects/experiences to compensate for unmet needs or missing experiences.
- **Symbolic substitution** — A symbol, story, game, community, or aesthetic stands in for something materially/socially unavailable.
- **Affective reinforcement** — Cultural use is strengthened because it reliably regulates or produces desired emotional states.
- **System-level reproductive dependence** — Strong claim requiring evidence that a cultural system’s continuation actually depends on reproducing the deprivation/need it serves, not merely benefiting from it.
