# Tarium Repository Ecology

Conceptarium recognizes a family of repository-scale knowledge systems collectively called the **Tarium ecology**.

The instantiated ecology currently includes:

- Conceptarium;
- Politicarium;
- Linguarium;
- Salvatarium;
- Somatarium;
- Dramatarium;
- Projectarium;
- Software Philosophy as an adjacent constitutional repository.

The canonical predicate ledger for instantiated repository entities is [`registry/repositories.yml`](../registry/repositories.yml).

Candidate, incubating, approved-but-uninstantiated, merged, rejected, and dormant repository proposals are separate pre-sovereignty objects in [`registry/tarium-candidates.yml`](../registry/tarium-candidates.yml).

## A novel ontological category

A repository is not merely another concept.

Conceptarium therefore treats **repository entities** as a distinct ontological category with their own registry and intentionally shallow materializations.

The repository category exists to answer questions such as:

- Does this repository exist?
- What is its canonical repository identity?
- What broad domain does it claim?
- How does it relate to the Tarium family?
- What public interface or relation has it explicitly declared?

It does **not** exist to reconstruct the repository's internal ontology from the outside.

A **Tarium candidate** is also not the same thing as a repository entity.

A candidate may have a stable Conceptarium identity, a sovereignty assessment, a host province, promotion evidence, and a materialized stub while no independent repository exists at all.

## Conceptarium as meta-repository

Conceptarium is the meta-repository of the ecology in a deliberately weak sense.

It is the place where the family may be described, compared, classified, and theorized from outside.

It is **not** the metropole.

Its role is:

> **external theorization without sovereignty**

Conceptarium may hypothesize that two tariums share a structure, that one is a domain projection of a broader pattern, or that several could interoperate.

Those claims remain Conceptarium's theories about the family.

They do not automatically become obligations imposed upon the modeled repositories.

Before repository instantiation, Conceptarium additionally acts as the **constitutional registrar of candidates**: it may preserve a proposed domain, test its claim to sovereignty, record incubation or merger, and retain the reasons for promotion or denial.

That pre-sovereignty role ends at the boundary of local repository authority.

## Local ontological sovereignty

Each instantiated repository remains authoritative over its own:

- objects;
- vocabulary;
- schemas;
- boundaries;
- artifacts;
- conclusions;
- internal state;
- local procedures.

A Conceptarium representation of another repository is therefore a **projection**, not a superseding ontology.

The governing rule is:

> **Conceptarium may model another tarium without thereby governing it.**

A member repository need not adopt Conceptarium's classification of it.

## Predicate knowledge without artifact capture

Conceptarium should normally know other instantiated repositories only at **predicate level**.

Permitted knowledge includes:

- that the repository exists;
- its canonical repository name;
- its broad declared purpose;
- its declared relation to the family;
- explicitly published interfaces or cross-repository contracts.

Excluded by default:

- inventories of internal artifacts;
- copies of repository-local objects;
- private-person records;
- unpublished files;
- current internal conclusions;
- live work state;
- detailed internal schemas not intentionally exposed for interoperability.

The distinction is:

```text
EXISTENCE METADATA
"Salvatarium exists."
        ≠
DOMAIN CONTENTS
"Here is what Salvatarium currently believes, stores, or concludes."
```

Predicate-level knowledge may be public even when the source repository remains private.

## Map of the archipelago

The intended architecture is:

> **a map of the archipelago without becoming the empire**

Conceptarium can maintain a family map because the map itself is useful.

The map must not silently become a master database whose convenience overrides local authority.

## Federation principle

The ecology currently follows four broad commitments:

1. **Local ontological sovereignty.** Each repository governs its own domain.
2. **Public predicate existence.** Conceptarium may acknowledge that a repository exists and state its coarse role.
3. **Optional interoperability.** Cross-repository interfaces are explicit rather than presumed.
4. **External theorization.** Conceptarium may model patterns across repositories without making those models locally binding.

## Canonical ownership

A cross-domain term can appear relevant to several tariums.

Canonical ownership should follow the thing's **deepest ontology**, not whichever repository happens to provide the most obvious vocabulary for discussing it.

For example, the Tarium Register has linguistic properties, political uses, aesthetic realizations, and personal provenance.

Its canonical cross-domain definition nevertheless belongs here because the underlying object is an epistemic-institutional communication regime rather than merely a linguistic register.

This same principle governs candidate consolidation. Animalia, Plants, and Animal Biology are preserved as considered candidates, but their deeper ontology resolves into Biotarium. Human Biology resolves into Somatarium. Nothing is lost merely because a proposed boundary is denied sovereignty.

## Tarium sovereignty and lifecycle

Repository creation is constitutional rather than automatic.

The family-level promotion contract is defined in [`Tarium Sovereignty and Lifecycle`](../meta/tarium-sovereignty-and-lifecycle.md).

The governing rule is:

> **Repository sovereignty is earned by ontological independence and structural pressure, not by enthusiasm alone.**

A strong sovereignty case normally requires:

- a distinctive canonical object of study;
- native entity types and relations;
- native questions not reducible to another repository's perspective;
- multiple substantial subdomains;
- factual and theoretical depth;
- meaningful genealogies or instantiated cases;
- room for phenomenological/evaluative material where relevant;
- enough structural pressure that continued projection into a host would become dishonest.

The lifecycle is:

```text
candidate
    ↓
incubating
    ↓
approved
    ↓
instantiated
    ↓
active
```

with `merged`, `rejected`, and `dormant` as preserved alternate outcomes.

Lifecycle state and physical materialization are distinct. In particular:

```text
lifecycle: approved
materialization: stub
```

means that a domain has earned sovereignty in principle but its repository has not yet been created.

The current approved-but-uninstantiated set contains eleven domains:

- Mathematics;
- Engineering;
- Designarium;
- Habitatarium (working name; also considered as Urbanarium / Civitatarium);
- Architecture;
- Economics;
- Biotarium;
- Geographia;
- Chemistry;
- Software Studies;
- Cuisine.

Typography, Sartorial Design, and Finance remain incubating provinces with explicit promotion triggers rather than sovereign repositories.

The candidate registry and stubs preserve these decisions without inflating the active repository census.

## General repository construction schema

The ecology has a family-level construction contract in [`Tarium Repository Schema`](../meta/tarium-repository-schema.md).

A Tarium is expected to preserve four distinct, first-class epistemic layers:

1. **Objective ledger** — externally auditable facts, observations, records, measurements, source claims, and evidence.
2. **Ontological structure** — types, identities, relations, states, mechanisms, significance, and structural representation.
3. **Integration and analysis** — arguments, syntheses, audits, interpretations, explanatory models, comparisons, and derived claims.
4. **Phenomenological / confessional layer** — Salvador's first-person experience, interpretation, memory, desire, fear, significance, narration, and confession.

These are not successive maturity stages.

They are **coequal repository layers with different epistemic functions**.

The family-level rule is:

> **Preserve fact, structure, analysis, and experience as distinct sovereign layers, then integrate them explicitly rather than collapsing them implicitly.**

The schema therefore rejects silent conversions such as:

```text
confession -> fact
analysis -> evidence
ontology -> proof
source claim -> verified fact
feeling -> external claim
```

Cross-layer integration should occur through explicit typed relations, provenance, citation, support, contradiction, motivation, or interpretation links.

A single real-world substrate may legitimately appear in several layers because each layer projects a different intentional question over it.

The physical folder layout is not centrally mandated. A Tarium may implement these layers using Markdown, YAML, graph structures, Rust types, databases, frontmatter, or another representation appropriate to its domain.

The semantic separation is load-bearing; the filesystem spelling is not.

## Somatarium

Somatarium is now an instantiated core member of the ecology at `sguzman/somatarium`.

Its coarse domain is Salvador's body and embodied organism, including source-backed human biology, anatomy, physiology, symptoms, measurements, interventions, hypotheses, and first-person bodily or mental phenomenology.

It is a particularly clear application of the Repository Schema because it must preserve:

```text
bodily fact / measurement
        ≠
causal or medical interpretation
        ≠
first-person bodily phenomenology
```

Phenomenology is first-class without acquiring automatic external truth.

Salvatarium remains the broader self-ontology. Somatarium owns the embodied organism. General nonhuman biology belongs to the Biotarium candidate domain.

Conceptarium records only Somatarium's predicate existence and coarse role, not its private bodily or medical contents.

## Tarium Register and Tarium Procedure

The ecology distinguishes two shared objects:

- [`Tarium Register`](../meta/tarium-register.md) — the recognizable prose realization;
- [`Tarium Procedure`](../meta/tarium-procedure.md) — the epistemic constitution beneath that prose.

The distinction is load-bearing.

The Register may evolve stylistically without changing the Procedure.

A terse machine-facing rendering, a friendlier public rendering, and a mildly 1910-inflected institutional rendering could all remain faithful to the same Procedure.

The Repository Schema and Sovereignty/Lifecycle constitution are additional family-level objects with different responsibilities:

```text
Tarium Procedure
    governs how claims are handled

Tarium Register
    governs how that discipline is voiced

Tarium Repository Schema
    governs which epistemic layers are preserved and kept distinct

Tarium Sovereignty + Lifecycle
    governs when a domain deserves an independent repository
```

## Current repository family

The instantiated family is described only at coarse domain level in [`registry/repositories.yml`](../registry/repositories.yml).

Repository-local materializations live under [`repositories/`](../repositories/).

Candidate state lives separately in [`registry/tarium-candidates.yml`](../registry/tarium-candidates.yml) with materialized stubs under [`candidates/`](../candidates/).

Repository pages are intentionally shallow.

If a repository page begins accumulating detailed foreign state, that is architectural pressure to remove detail rather than evidence that Conceptarium should become a central mirror.
